## Elevator pitch

**New York, late Prohibition (~1928–1933).** Five crime families run rackets the way they always have — unions, gambling, protection, smuggling — while **magic and the gods are real, known phenomena**, regulated like any other dangerous trade. The city has **no scientific-industrial path to “modern” wonder-tech**; instead, guilds, temples, and street alchemists iterate on **medieval magical craft and pseudo-science** until it *loosely* solves the same problems we associate with later inventions (long-distance talk, fast travel, healing, proof of guilt) — with different tools, epistemology, and failure modes.

The party are **operators** in the grey: fixers, muscle, ward-breakers, drivers, and “consultants” who are definitely not licensed clergy.

## What “tech” looks like

**Rule:** Mundane period gear is fine (Tommy guns, Packards, rotary phones, radio, telegraph). **Magical infrastructure** must read as **ritual, alchemical, or divine contract** — never “enchanted smartphone / CCTV / app.”

**Canonical detail:** domain-by-domain divergence, skyline rules, automaton labor, intimate craft, AI simulation grammar, and path-dependence seeds → **`SETTING-MAGITECH-DIVERGENCE.md`**. The table below is a **quick index**; agents must follow the grammar in that doc when inventing new devices.

| Need | Magical / pseudo-scientific analogue | What it is *not* |
|------|--------------------------------------|------------------|
| **Communication** | **Speaking-tubes** (paired brass horns, wax-sealed at rest); **mirror-scry** appointments (scheduled, line busy); **bound messenger** pigeons or street imps with return-charms; **telegram-splice** rituals at Western Union back rooms (operator hears a whisper, types plaintext) | Cell networks, always-on video, Discord |
| **Transport** | **Warded hack lines** and **glamoured carriages** (horses or early motor chassis *plus* path-shortening charms); **ferry contracts** with river spirits; **subway “below-stairs”** shortcuts that skip blocks at a toll | GPS, rideshare, maglev scifi |
| **Weapons** | Tommy guns and revolvers **plus** **etched rounds** (one-shot evocations), **blessed brass knuckles**, **poisoned blade oils**, **silence wards** on a room not on a camera | Smart guns, drones |
| **Medicine** | **Salon healers** (laying on hands, licensed or bootleg); **tincture alchemy** (cure wounds as patent elixir); **funeral-house necromancy** for “one question” witness interviews — tightly taboo | MRI, mass-produced potion vending as sci-fi |
| **Surveillance** | **Wire without wire:** gramophone needles on shared copper, **scry-tag** chalk marks that report who crossed a threshold, **informant gifts** (compelled honesty within a circle), **Federal listening seals** on speakeasy doors | CCTV, facial recognition, cloud logs |
| **Banking / records** | **Ledger-books** with **truth-bind pages**; **warded safes** keyed to bloodline or oath; **notary-clerics** who witness contracts the state recognizes | Crypto, blockchain |

**Failure mode (important for play):** magical systems are **craft, not firmware**. They need materials, hours, a patron saint, and sometimes a sacrifice. When they fail, they fail **weird** (wrong voice on the speaking-tube, healing that fixes the wound but steals a memory).

## Epistemology — how “research” works

There is no dominant **scientific method** as we know it. Knowledge advances through:

1. **Temple and cult precedent** — “Apollo’s surgeons tried this for three centuries; it works on feast days.”
2. **Guild craft iteration** — alchemists, scriveners, ward-smiths copy masters’ formulae; breakthroughs are **trade secrets**, not papers.
3. **Patronage** — families and churches fund **house magicians**; results are judged by **outcomes and omens**, not peer review.
4. **Pseudo-science** — humoral theory, astrology, sympathy charts; **sometimes accidentally correct** because the gods and ley-lines are real.
5. **Omens vs experiment** — a failed batch might mean bad stars, not bad temperature control.

**Gods:** Real. People argue *which* gods answer which rites, not whether prayer works. Priests are **licensed professionals** or **bootleg saints**. Arcane and divine are **competing bureaucracies**, not D&D class flavor only.

**Player-facing rules:** D&D-ish (classes as **roles**: muscle, fixer, driver, mage, “chaplain”). Spells are **known phenomena** — society has opinions, permits, and black markets for them.

## Prohibition ↔ magic

Parallel tracks, same pressure:

| Alcohol Prohibition | Magical Prohibition (setting default) |
|---------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Speakeasies, bathtub gin | **Blind tiger salons** — unlicensed healing, luck, ward-breaking |
| Rum-running from Canada | **Relic runs** — saints’ bones, temple incense, forbidden formulae |
| Treasury / IRS enforcement | **Bureau of Consecrated Commerce (BCC)** or **Office of Thaumaturgical Revenue (OTR)** — taxes charms, seizes “unregistered miracles” |
| Bribes, corrupt cops | **Ward-inspectors** on payroll; **truth-bind** evidence in RICO-style trials |
| Violence over routes | Families fight over **ley-taps**, **funeral-house franchises**, **speaking-tube exchanges** |

**Bootleg potions?** Yes — “bathtub elixir” that heals but leaves a telltale aura. **Banned rites?** Necromancy beyond one question; **pacts** without disclosure; **mass mind-touch** (the “charisma” crime). **Federal heat** can be Treasury + a **Magical Enforcement** desk — name TBD at table.
