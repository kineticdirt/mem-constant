# Blind Tiger, Second Curtain

## Scene

Mama Rosa's Tea Parlor on Hester Street sold oolong in front and **bathtub gin** behind the false pantry — everyone on the block knew, including the beat cop who drank there Tuesdays. What the block didn't advertise was the **second curtain**: past the gin, a narrow stair to a room that smelled like antiseptic and **incense**, where a gnome in wire spectacles ran **luck** trays for the numbers runners and, on new moons, fitted **portal-lace** for couples who couldn't afford Ashford's witness fees.

He was waiting for **Eddie Voss**, the gnome operator, to sign an extract clause: Valenti muscle in the alley, Moretti paperwork in the court, Eddie alive and **not** on a federal manifest. The gin was good. The ledger was better evidence than either family wanted.

The first knock wasn't police. It was **OTR** — two clerks, gray suits, clipboards like weapons. They didn't axe the door. They **audited** the pantry receipts while the tea parlor froze in polite denial.

Eddie's face went the color of old ivory. The second curtain might hold against gin. It would not hold against **wax with his voice on it**.

"Wrong day," Salvatore muttered. Comedy if it weren't his signature on the ledger. OTR wanted taxes; BCC wanted **kits**; NYPD wanted **credit** for the collar.
