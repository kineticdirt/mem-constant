# Continental Lighting

## Scene

The club on West Forty-Seventh called itself **The Gilded Wire** and smelled like cigarettes, perfume, and money pretending it had always been clean. Out front, a human doorman in a tuxedo checked names against a **scry-tag** chalk roster that wasn't magic to the guests — it was "the list." Behind him, brass speaking-tubes snaked into the wall, carrying orders to the kitchen and, if you knew the password, to the **back**.

Liriel — tiefling, horns filed and capped with silver caps that caught the footlights — worked the floor as **Mme. Liri from Marseille**, "lighting consultant." Every chorus girl wore a **glamour veil** refreshed between sets; the audience applauded the sparkle and called it **Continental effects**. If a drunk asked aloud whether it was witchcraft, the band played louder and the waiters smiled the way waiters smile at men who tip badly.

She wasn't hiding. She was **translating**.

Ashford's junior partner held court at the corner booth — human, thirty, hair oil perfect. He needed a **warlock advisory** retainer documented as **cultural brokerage** for the shareholders' meeting Thursday. Liriel slid into the seat opposite with a ledger that listed only **lighting fees**.

"The shareholders still ask if the glow is Edison," she said.

"Tell them Edison had a partner they haven't met." He didn't look at her horns. Midtown denial meant **not looking** was part of the service she sold.

A **ward-inspector** — NYPD, local, bored — wandered through at ten-thirty. He touched the door seal Ashford's lawyers paid for, nodded at the doorman, and paused by Liriel's booth long enough to make the junior partner nervous.

"Evening, **consultant**." The inspector's eyes flicked to her capped horns, then away. "OTR man's coming tomorrow. Receipts."

"Receipts are **lighting**," Liriel said.

"Sure." He moved on. Harassment here wasn't slurs; it was **surcharges** scheduled like tide tables.

In the service corridor, an orc dishwasher — too big for the floor — wiped plates in silence. He'd come up from Hell's Kitchen where nobody made him use the alley. Here he used the alley because the **performance** kept the club open. Liriel passed him a folded bill without comment. Midtown paid twice: once in cash, once in **pretense**.

At midnight the **luck tray** came out — not named, just a silver bowl of **incense** chips on the bandstand. A tourist won forty dollars and told his wife it was **stage luck**. The wife believed him because believing was the cover charge.

When Liriel finally took the speaking-tube call from the back room — a **link** fitting waiting, nervous human couple, licensed veneer and unlicensed **stitch** — she kept her voice low and her grammar clean.

"Appointments are **massage**," she told the tube. "Witness at eleven."

Denial tier wasn't ignorance. It was **everyone doing their job** so the federal men could pretend tomorrow's audit was only about receipts.
