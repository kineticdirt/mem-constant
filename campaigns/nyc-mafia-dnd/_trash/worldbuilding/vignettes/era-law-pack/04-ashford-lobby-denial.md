# The Appointment Fiction

## Scene

Ashford & Partners occupied the fourteenth floor of a brick tower that pretended it was taller than the Depression. The lobby was marble, ferns, and a receptionist who sold **appointments** the way a priest sold feast days — names in a book, smiles that meant **you may exist here if you match the ink**.

Rook — half-orc, jaw like a packing crate, collar buttoned high — had walked Red Hook that morning without a second glance. Here the revolving door refused him before the doorman did. Glass, brass, and the soft cough of a man who had practiced **not seeing**.

"Freight elevator," the doorman said, almost kindly. "Service corridor. Mr. Ashford's people use it for **deliveries**."

Rook's fixer — human face, good coat, ledger under one arm — was already inside, shaking hands with a junior partner. Through the glass Rook watched them invent him: **security consultant**, **dock liaison**, never **half-orc**. The junior partner did not look toward the alley. Midtown denial meant **not looking** was billable.

In the service stair, an elf courier with a sealed tube pouch nodded once — trade-coded, not scandal. She had a front-door appointment at three under a human surname. Rook had none. He carried a box labeled **lighting fixtures**. Inside: three unregistered **luck** trays wrapped in butcher paper. The Tribune that morning had shouted **FEDERAL MEN SEIZE WEST SIDE CLINIC**; it had not printed the word **miracle**. Rook understood the policy without reading it. Print named the raid. The alley named the work.

The speaking-tube in the stairwell hissed. The fixer's voice, cleaned for Midtown:

"Send the **atmosphere** up. Stair B. No lobby."

Rook climbed. At the landing a ward-inspector leaned on the rail, bored, counting horns he would not write into any public report.

"Evening, **delivery**," the inspector said. Eyes on the box. "OTR likes receipts that say **merchandise**."

"Merchandise," Rook said.

"Sure." A surcharge look — not a slur. Tide-table harassment. Rook paid the folded bill because the lease upstairs depended on everyone performing.

When the box hit the conference table, the junior partner smiled at the fixtures and never opened the paper. The luck trays would leave as **Continental samples**. If a photographer caught Rook in the lobby glass tomorrow, the Tribune society page would call it a **labor dispute at Ashford**, not ancestry, and the scandal would still cost someone a retainer.

Rook took the freight car down. On the pier they would have said **luck**. Here they said **atmosphere**. Same city. Wrong grammar gets you the alley — or the headline that never quite names you.
