## OCU — Organizational Structure

The NYPD Organized Crime Unit's **Arcane Division** is a specialized detachment housed at the **1st Precinct Annex** (Broadway and Stone Street, a converted bank building with warded evidence vaults in the old safe-deposit basement). It is not a standalone precinct — it's a task force attached to the NYPD's Organized Crime Control Bureau.

### Chain of Command

| Rank | Officer | Role |
|------|---------|------|
| **Captain** | **Captain Miriam Kostas** | Division Commander — runs the entire Arcane Division. 28-year veteran. Doesn't cast spells, doesn't need to. She reads people and paperwork. |
| **Lieutenant** | **Lt. Marcus Diaz** | Shift supervisor, day-watch (0600–1800). Former narcotics. Runs informant handoffs and warrant execution. Pragmatic, cynical, effective. |
| **Sergeant** | **Sgt. Elena Voss** | Forensics team lead. ALB-certified forensic mage who went blue instead of private sector. Runs the Arcane Trace Unit (3 detectives with *detect magic* at-will). |
| **Detective** | **Adaeze Chen-Okafor** | See `2026-07-30-npc-roster.md` — family connection to Chen-Okafor, works the Valenti-Ashford ledger case. |
| **Detective** | **Frankie O'Shea** | OCU veteran, 15 years. Wears a ward-glyph wedding ring (his wife's idea). Works the Kowalski waste-disposal beat. Trusts his gut, distrusts magic. |
| **Detective** | **Reynaldo "Ray" Santos** | Transfer from Vice — young, ambitious, one of the few detectives who trained with ALB voluntary coursework. Handles Ashford/Midtown cases. Speaks Infernal (picked up in seminary before he dropped out). |
| **Forensic Mage** | **Dr. Lena Marchetti** | Civilian contractor, PhD in arcane forensics from CUNY. Runs the Division's lab. Her testimony has survived defense challenges in five trials. Her brother is a Moretti capo — she doesn't talk about it. |
| **Forensic Mage** | **Kai Nakamura** | Night-shift forensic mage (1800–0600). Young, fast, less experienced than Marchetti. Carries a warded evidence satchel everywhere. His father owns a divination kiosk in Flushing. |

### Division Resources

- **Staffing:** ~14 detectives + 3 forensic mages + 1 data analyst (Carol Suarez, Access database, dial-up ALB feed) + 2 uniformed officers assigned to evidence vault.
- **Vehicles:** 4 unmarked sedans (grey, all same make/model), 1 marked van for evidence transport. The van's back compartment is a mobile ward — Class-C rating, anti-scry lining.
- **Evidence vault:** Bank basement on Stone Street. Two warded doors. Access requires badge + ALB cert + written order. A necromancy-tainted object triggers a salt-line alarm.
- **Budget:** Inadequate. The Division's 1999 operating budget ($1.2M) covers salaries and basic equipment. Everything else — informant payments, overtime for stakeouts, the van's ward maintenance — comes from the NYPD's discretionary fund, which Captain Kostas has to beg for quarterly.

## Magical Crime Taskforce — "The Taskforce" (Federal)

The **NYC Arcane RICO Task Force** is a federal body housed in the SDNY's Foley Square building (500 Pearl Street, 8th floor — no exterior signage, a locked elevator requires a DOJ badge). It is not part of the NYPD — it answers to the **U.S. Attorney's Office, Southern District of New York**.

### Staff

| Role | Name | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| **Lead AUSA** | **Andrea Pierce** | Head of the taskforce. 42 years old, Manhattan DA's office for 8 years before moving federal. Has never lost an arcane RICO case. Keeps a *protection from evil* charm in her briefcase — it's not just for show. |
| **AUSA** | **Tom Nguyen** | Second chair, manages grand jury presentation. Specializes in evidence chain-of-custody for magical items. Has a standing coffee order with the ALB liaison. |
| **DOJ Diviner** | **Selene Ashworth** | Contract diviner (DOJ pays $250/hour, a third of what private work pays). Reads scrying residue and spell-trace reports. She's good but overworked — the taskforce's pending cases are backed up 8 weeks. |
| **FBI Liaison** | **Special Agent Derek Cross** | FBI's Organized Crime Section assigns one agent to the taskforce. Cross is career FBI — he's been on mob cases since the Gotti trial. Doesn't believe in magic, believes in evidence. His skepticism is both a strength (chain-of-custody rigor) and a blind spot (he dismisses infernal contracts as "mob spook stories"). |

### How the Taskforce Differs from OCU

| Dimension | OCU (City) | Taskforce (Federal) |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Jurisdiction** | NYC only — any magical crime | Interstate or RICO-predicate magical crime only |
| **Trigger** | 911 call, ALB referral, patrol flag | Grand jury subpoena, federal warrant, multi-state pattern |
| **Tools** | Physical surveillance, informants, ALB records | Federal wiretap FISA, DOJ diviners, forensic accountants |
| **Evidence** | Admissible in NY state court | Admissible in SDNY federal court (tougher chain-of-custody rules) |
| **Pressure** | NYPD brass wants stats — clearance rate matters | DOJ wants indictments — conviction rate matters |
| **Weakness** | Underfunded, ALB-rotten, family-compromised | Slow, procedural, one diviner for the whole office |

### OCU–Taskforce Friction

The two bodies are **not allies**. They share information selectively, compete for cases, and dislike each other.

- **Case poaching:** If the Taskforce sees a case on OCU's desk that has federal potential, they can take it via AUSA Pierce's RICO motion. This has happened three times in 1999. Captain Kostas stopped sharing her open-case board after the second one.
- **Evidence sharing:** ALB-certified mage testimony differs between state and federal court. A forensic report prepared for NYS court needs re-certification for SDNY. Marchetti charges double for federal testimony (she dislikes the paperwork).
- **Informant trust:** OCU informants know that federal indictment carries longer sentences. They're less willing to talk to Agent Cross than to Detective O'Shea. Kostas plays this card: she knows Cross needs her informant network, and she withholds it for leverage.

### At the Table — OCU Pressure Scenes

When the crew is on OCU's radar, you can run any of these as a scene:

1. **The Interview Room.** The crew gets called in for a "courtesy conversation." Detective Santos takes the lead. He's polite, offers coffee, asks about a shipment the crew wasn't supposed to touch. Every question is a fishing expedition — how much does the crew know? Lie poorly, and a flag goes on their ALB file.

2. **The Stakeout.** The crew spots the grey sedan from three blocks away. O'Shea and a uniform are inside, sipping gas-station coffee and waiting for someone to make a mistake. The crew can avoid them, confront them, or feed them bad information.

3. **The Raid (Heat 4).** Four unmarked cars, the evidence van, and a forensic mage. The crew has 30 seconds to stash incriminating items before the door goes down. Salt lines. Enchanted cuffs. A ward-gloved hand reaching for the cursed item on the table.

4. **The Grand Jury Subpoena.** A crew member is called to testify. The courthouse is procedural and terrifying — a courtroom with a diviner sitting in the jury box, and a federal prosecutor who has never lost. The question isn't what the crew did — it's what they'll say under oath.

## Key NPC — Captain Miriam Kostas

A detail for the table, beyond the chain-of-command entry above.

**Age:** 51
**Appearance:** Short grey hair, practical suit, comfortable shoes. A scar on her left eyebrow from a 1993 raid in Bushwick (the one that made her believe in arcane evidence). Always carries a fountain pen — it's a family heirloom, not a magical item. She just likes how it writes.
**Manner:** Quiet, watchful. She doesn't raise her voice. When Kostas gives an order, it's because she's already decided. The Division works for her, not the other way around.
**Pressure point:** Her daughter is a student at NYU. The families don't know this. If they did, Kostas would transfer out of the Division within 24 hours.
**What she wants:** One clean RICO conviction — a real one, with admissible evidence, before she retires. She has 3 years. She knows the ledger case could be that conviction, but she also knows her own Division has a leak.

## Key NPC — Detective Rey "Ray" Santos

**Age:** 29
**Appearance:** Sharp dresser — even on stakeout he's in a pressed shirt. Short black hair, clean-shaven, athletic build. Wears a gold cross under his shirt (non-magical, but warded — a gift from his mother).
**Manner:** Professional, intense, the most educated detective in the Division. He reads ALB bulletins cover to cover. He took a night course in Infernal calligraphy at the New School in 1997. The other detectives think he's a try-hard. Kostas thinks he's the future of the Division.
**Weakness:** He's never made a big arrest. His file is clean procedure — no shootings, no corruption, no headlines. He's hungry for a case that makes his career. The cursed ledger is that case. The crew can spot this — he's the most likely to make a mistake if pressured.
**What he knows:** The Ashford-Valenti ledger dispute from the ALB side (he talked to a liaison before Kostas told him to stop). He has a theory that the ledger contains a list of ALB-bought amnesty licenses.

## Key NPC — AUSA Andrea Pierce

**Age:** 42
**Appearance:** Tailored suit, no jewelry, reading glasses on a chain. Keeps her hair in a short bob. Walks with purpose — she's never been late to a hearing.
**Manner:** Direct, impatient, intellectually ruthless. She structures every conversation like an opening statement. She doesn't bluff — if she says she'll indict, she has the evidence. Her conviction rate is 94% (1999 YTD).
**Weakness:** She's a prosecutor, not an investigator. She needs clean evidence, and she needs it before Kostas's case leaks to the families. She can't control the OCU's internal security, which frustrates her constantly.
**Connection to the crew:** The crew will never meet her face-to-face unless they're on the stand. But her presence is felt — every sealed search warrant, every grand jury delay, every asset freeze. She's the ghost in the justice system.

## Summary

> The OCU's Arcane Division is a underfunded, overstretched city operation that knows every family's patterns but can't act fast enough. The federal Arcane RICO Task Force is a procedural machine that moves slowly but hits hard. The two bodies don't trust each other. The families benefit from the friction.

> For the table: OCU shows up when heat rises. The Taskforce shows up when the crew's actions draw federal attention — and that's a different kind of threat. The grey sedan is a warning. The Foley Square elevator is a verdict.
