# B-1 — Borough Location Bible (5 Keyed Spots)

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Campaign:** NYC Mafia × D&D
**Item:** B-1 — Borough location bible (5 keyed spots)

---

## Design principles

These five locations are **session-ready**: each one has a faction tie, a supernatural element, a practical function for the crew, and a "wrong" thing that can go sideways. They're spread across four boroughs to give the campaign geographic texture without requiring a travelogue. The GM should treat these as the first layer — add neighborhood spots between sessions as the crew's reputation grows.

---

## 1. The Mulberry Social Club — Little Italy, Manhattan

**Territory:** Moretti (heartland)

**What it looks like:** A narrow three-story brick building wedged between a bakery and a barber shop on Mulberry Street. The ground floor is a functioning social club — old men playing cards, espresso machine hissing, a TV tuned to soccer. The basement is a **consecrated chamber** where the Morettis perform their ghost-binding burial rites. The top floor is Father Tommaso's private quarters and a locked archive of binding records going back to the 1940s.

**Faction tie:** Moretti family headquarters for spiritual operations. All ghost-binding contracts are negotiated here. Commission meetings sometimes convene on the second floor when the Morettis want to make a point about tradition.

**Supernatural element:** The basement is warded against divination (cast *Detect Magic* and the whole room hums). The bound ghosts manifest as cold spots and whispered Latin. If the crew ever needs to interrogate a dead Moretti soldier, this is where the ritual happens — but Father Tommaso won't perform it without a favor.

**Practical function for the crew:** Safe meeting ground (the ground floor is public and protected by unspoken Commission rules). Information source — the old men at the card table hear everything. Potential heist target — the top-floor archive contains records of every ghost the Morettis have bound, which is leverage against every family member who's ever lost someone.

**What goes wrong:** The ghosts in the basement are getting restless. Father Tommaso's crisis of faith (see A-3) is making the bindings unstable. A crew meeting on the ground floor might be interrupted by a cold draft and a voice that shouldn't be there — and the voice knows things it shouldn't know about the crew.

**Key NPCs:** Father Tommaso Moretti (chaplain), "Uncle" Sal Benedetto (card table regular, retired Moretti capo, knows where the bodies are buried — literally).

## 2. The Appraiser's Workshop — DUMBO, Brooklyn

**Territory:** Valenti (waterfront adjacent)

**What it looks like:** A converted warehouse gallery on the ground floor of a DUMBO loft building. High ceilings, exposed brick, track lighting over glass display cases full of "antiques" — some genuinely old, some genuinely cursed. The front is a legitimate appraisal business; the back room is where Lucia Valenti identifies and catalogs magical items. There's a loading dock in the rear that connects to a service alley.

**Faction tie:** Valenti family's magical logistics hub. Every cursed item that comes through the Red Hook docks passes through Lucia's hands. She decides what gets sold, what gets destroyed, and what gets reported to the family.

**Supernatural element:** The back room has a permanent *Detect Magic* field (Lucia's familiar — the "Seeing Eye dog" — is actually a re-skinned imp that maintains the ward). Items in the display cases are under *Nystul's Magic Aura* to fool casual inspection. The loading dock has a one-way ward: things can be brought in, but removing a cursed item without Lucia's permission triggers a *Glyph of Warding* keyed to the item's magical signature.

**Practical function for the crew:** The crew's most likely "fence" for magical goods. Lucia buys, sells, and appraises — and she pays fair, which is rare. She's also the crew's best source of intelligence on what's moving through the waterfront. If the crew needs a specific magical item identified, this is where they come.

**What goes wrong:** Lucia's personal vendetta against the Kowalski family (see A-3) is escalating. A Kowalski river-dead was spotted in the alley behind the workshop. Lucia is hiring extra security, and the crew might be asked to handle it — putting them between two families.

**Key NPCs:** Lucia Valenti ("The Appraiser"), her familiar (appears as a German Shepherd named "Cisco," actually a Chain Pact familiar with *Invisibility* and *Mage Hand*).

## 3. The Flushing Counting House — Flushing, Queens

**Territory:** Chen-Okafor (home turf)

**What it looks like:** A three-story building on a busy Flushing street, sandwiched between a Korean BBQ restaurant and a West African grocery. The ground floor is a "market research consultancy" — glass desks, Bloomberg terminals, young analysts on headsets. The second floor is the actual operation: a **divination lab** where Chen-Okafor fixers run probability models, I Ching readings, and Ifá consultations simultaneously, feeding results to the betting operations. The third floor is David Chen-Okafor's private office and a secure server room.

**Faction tie:** Chen-Okafor family's information brokerage. This is where divination meets data science. The family sells curated probability readings to stock traders, politicians, and — discreetly — other families. The server room contains encrypted records of every reading ever sold, which is a goldmine of blackmail material.

**Supernatural element:** The divination lab is a *Magic Circle* keyed to probability manipulation — inside the circle, *Augury* and *Divination* spells are enhanced (advantage on the reading), but *Zone of Truth* doesn't function (the Chen-Okafor family warded against it years ago). The server room has a custom ward that triggers *Sending* to David's phone if anyone accesses the files without authorization.

**Practical function for the crew:** Information brokerage. If the crew needs to know what's going to happen before it happens, this is where they pay for it. David Chen-Okafor is a professional — he'll sell to anyone, including the crew, as long as the price is right and the request doesn't threaten the family.

**What goes wrong:** David's younger sister is an NYPD detective who's been assigned to a joint task force with the OCU. She doesn't know what the family really does, but she's smart, and she's been asking questions about the "consultancy." If the crew does business here, they risk being seen by someone who might recognize them — or by David's sister, who might start pulling threads.

**Key NPCs:** David Chen-Okafor ("The Bookie"), his sister Detective Adaeze Chen-Okafor (NYPD, unknowing), "Auntie" Nkechi (Ifá priestess, runs the divination lab, doesn't trust outsiders).

## 4. The Kill van Kull Processing Plant — Staten Island

**Territory:** Kowalski (inner sanctum)

**What it looks like:** A decommissioned waste processing plant on the Staten Island shore of the Kill van Kull strait. Chain-link fence, "NO TRESPASSING" signs, a rusted gate that's never locked (the Kowalskis don't believe in locks — they believe in the river). The main building is a corrugated steel warehouse. The water access is a crumbling concrete pier. At night, the water around the pier glows faintly — not with light, but with *absence* of it.

**Faction tie:** Kowalski family's spiritual center. This is where Vera Kowalski communes with the river dead. The plant processes the family's "disappearances" — not bodies (that's a rumor), but the *evidence* of disappearances. Documents, clothes, personal effects — anything that could identify a missing person goes through the plant and comes out clean.

**Supernatural element:** The river dead are strongest here. Vera Kowalski can manifest up to six bound water spirits at a time within 100 yards of the pier. The water itself is supernaturally cold and slightly viscous — *Detect Magic* reveals necromantic resonance. The warehouse floor has a permanent *Hallow* effect (reversed — it repels the living and attracts the dead). Entering without an invitation from a Kowalski is dangerous; the river dead are territorial.

**Practical function for the crew:** The crew should avoid this location unless specifically invited or desperate. If they need to find someone who's been "disappeared," this is where the trail ends — or begins, if Vera is willing to ask the river. A Kowalski invitation is valuable currency; accepting one creates an obligation.

**What goes wrong:** The river dead are getting more active. Vera is aging and her control is slipping. Something in the deep water is pushing back — not hostile, but *hungry*. If the crew is here at night, they might see shapes in the water that aren't Kowalski ancestors. Something older.

**Key NPCs:** Vera Kowalski ("The Ferryman"), her grandson Piotr Kowalski (enforcer, handles the living-side operations, deeply protective of his grandmother).

## 5. Ashford Security Consulting — Midtown, Manhattan

**Territory:** Ashford (corporate home)

**What it looks like:** The 14th and 15th floors of a glass-and-steel tower on Sixth Avenue. Marble reception, abstract art, a waiting area with complimentary espresso and *Wall Street Journal*. The 14th floor is the legitimate security consulting operation — corporate clients, ALB compliance audits, ward installation contracts. The 15th floor is the **warlock pact brokerage** — soundproofed rooms, warded conference tables, and a library of extraplanar contracts bound in materials that aren't leather.

**Faction tie:** Ashford family's public face and supernatural revenue engine. Every warlock contract brokered through Ashford Security is ALB-compliant on paper. The 15th floor is where the real deals happen — Marcus Ashford meets with prospective warlocks, reviews their "applications," and connects them with extraplanar entities from the Ashford family's curated list of patrons.

**Supernatural element:** The 15th floor is under a permanent *Mordenkainen's Private Sanctum* — no scrying, no divination, no sound leakage. The contract library radiates transmutation and conjuration magic. Signing a contract in the conference room triggers a *Geas*-like compulsion (not the spell, but a supernatural obligation enforced by the patron). The elevator to the 15th floor requires a keycard *and* a verbal passphrase that changes weekly.

**Practical function for the crew:** The most professional supernatural service in the city. If a crew member wants to become a warlock, renegotiate a pact, or get an existing contract reviewed for loopholes, this is where they come. Marcus Ashford is expensive but thorough — his contracts are airtight, and he provides after-sales support (patron mediation, side-effect management). He's also a potential employer for the crew: Ashford Security hires "field consultants" for jobs that require deniability.

**What goes wrong:** Marcus has a clause in his own contract that he's never told anyone about (see A-3). Something is coming due. The extraplanar entities on the Ashford roster are getting harder to manage — one of them has been asking about the crew by name, and Marcus doesn't know how it got the information.

**Key NPCs:** Marcus Ashford ("The Consultant"), his assistant Priya Mehta (handles scheduling, knows more than she lets on, possibly a low-level warlock herself), the entity Marcus never speaks about (name unknown, communicates through the contract library — the pages turn on their own when it wants attention).
