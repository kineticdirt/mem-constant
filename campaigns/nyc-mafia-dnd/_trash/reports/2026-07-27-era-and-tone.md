## Era lock: 1999 New York

**Year:** 1999, pre-9/11, pre-smartphone, pre-social-media.

### Tech snapshot
- Flip phones (Nokia 5160), pagers, payphones, paper maps
- Dial-up internet / early broadband — slow, unindexed, not a universal crime tool
- No GPS tracking, no camera in every pocket, no social-media trail
- CRT monitors next to crystal balls — the campaign's visual signature

### NYC texture
- WTC stands, Giuliani-RICO era, NYPD Street Crime Unit active
- Docks still working-class, LES not fully gentrified, Washington Heights Dominican
- Graffiti on subway cars, arcades, CD-Rs, cassette tapes, Tommy Hilfiger / FUBU streetwear
- Y2K anxiety as ambient paranoia — hooks into the ALB's "mana-grid patch"

### Why 1999, not today
| Factor | What it gives |
|--------|---------------|
| **Mafia's cultural peak** | *The Sopranos* (premiering 1999), *Goodfellas*, *Donnie Brasco* — the 90s is the era of the Italian-American crime story. Players arrive with vibe baked in. |
| **No smartphones** | A flip phone can't GPS-track, video-stream, or photo-ID. A divination wizard is the *only* way to find someone — not Life360. |
| **Pre-gentrification NYC** | Times Square still has peep shows. Abandoned piers. Graffiti-covered trains. The city breathes danger. |
| **Limited surveillance** | No Ring cameras, no facial recognition, no social-media trails. A witness can actually disappear. |
| **Internet as tool, not god** | Dial-up, AOL chat, IRC. A hacker/artificer PC is viable but not trivially omniscient. |
| **Distinct identity** | "Modern day NYC but D&D" is generic. *Late-90s NYC but D&D* is a *choice* — cinematic, specific, evocative. |

### What stays the same
The Five Families, the Commission, the OCU, the ALB, the Below — all portable. Premise.md's open question is resolved: **1999, locked.** No setting element depends on the decade beyond texture.

## Tone: "Mob noir, low magic, high heat"

### Core feeling
*The city is wet. The food is good. The loyalty is real but the loyalty is also a leash.*

### Three pillars

| Pillar | What it means at the table |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **Grounded procedural** | Every job is a logistics problem: route, driver, mage, bribe, backup. Tension from planning, not spell slots. |
| **City as character** | Real NYC geography matters. Boroughs have distinct identity: Brooklyn = docks/dice games, Midtown = warlock consulting, Queens = magictech black market. |
| **Magic is scarce & costly** | Most people never cast a spell. A fireball draws the OCU in 15 minutes. Healing potions cost a week's wages. Warlock pacts = Faustian employment contracts. |

### What is NOT in this tone
- **No heroic fantasy** — you're trying to get paid, not save the world.
- **No cyberpunk** — Nokia 5110 and a pager, not netrunning and chrome.
- **No parody** — serious stakes, dark humor ("I can't believe this is my life"), not camp.

### The magic rule
> If a spell would be visible in a public place, it's a problem.

Subtle magic rules: enchanted objects, ritual circles, basement bargains. A fireball in a Midtown diner brings the Commission, the OCU, and whatever is Below — none friendly.

### The 1999 texture rule
> Everything that existed in 1998 exists here, unless the GM says otherwise.

Pagers, payphones, CD shops, video stores, *The Sopranos* on HBO, Giuliani's final term, dot-com boom in Midtown, NYPD stop-and-frisk. Use what works, ignore what doesn't.
