# B-2 — Law vs Underworld: OCU + RICO + Magic Evidence Rules

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Campaign:** NYC Mafia × D&D
**Item:** B-2 — Law vs underworld (OCU + RICO + magic evidence rules)

---

## Design goal

The crew operates in a city where **law is real but bendable**. This document gives the GM a framework for when law enforcement matters, how magic interacts with evidence, and what "legal consequences" actually look like in a world where the mob owns half the bureaucracy. The goal is **tension without bookkeeping** — the rules here should create dramatic pressure, not courtroom simulations.

---

## The two legal threats

### 1. NYPD Occult Crimes Unit (OCU)

**What they are:** A joint NYPD–ALB task force that investigates unlicensed magic, magical crimes, and supernatural public-safety threats. Underfunded, understaffed, and politically vulnerable. They have jurisdiction over any crime involving unlicensed magical activity in the five boroughs.

**How they work:**
- **Patrol level:** OCU beat cops (like Sgt. Adrienne Voss from the opening job) carry *Detect Magic* wands — low-sensitivity, short-range, prone to false positives near ley lines and subway deep sections. They can stop, question, and detain. They cannot prosecute alone; they need ALB or DA cooperation.
- **Detective level:** OCU detectives investigate magical crimes that cross RICO thresholds or make the news. They carry better equipment (*Zone of Truth* cuffs, *Arcane Lock* breaching kits) and have ALB liaison officers embedded in their squad.
- **ALB liaison:** Every OCU precinct has one ALB clerk on rotation who can issue emergency magic-use permits, certify evidence, and — critically — **determine whether a magical act was licensed or not**. This is the corruption point: the mob owns several of these liaisons.

**What triggers OCU attention:**
| Trigger | Response time | Severity |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| Unlicensed magic in public (witnesses) | Hours to days | Low — patrol response, possible citation |
| Magical assault with civilian casualties | Immediate | High — detective response, ALB liaison called |
| Unlicensed magic in a licensed facility (e.g., using spells in a warded counting room without clearance) | Days to weeks | Medium — ALB audit triggered |
| RICO threshold crossed (organized magical crime) | Weeks to months (investigation) | Very high — federal involvement possible |
| Press coverage of magical incident | Immediate political pressure | Variable — OCU forced to act regardless of evidence |

**What the crew should fear:** Not the OCU cop on the beat — she's one woman with a wand and a report form. What the crew should fear is **the paper trail**. Every OCU incident report goes into an ALB database. If the crew keeps showing up in reports, a detective will eventually connect the dots. The mob's counterstrategy is to keep the dots disconnected: bribe the clerk, lose the report, reclassify the incident.

---

### 2. RICO (Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act)

**What it is:** Federal organized-crime statute. In this setting, RICO applies to magical and non-magical crimes equally. The feds don't care about your fireball — they care about the **pattern of enterprise crime** that the fireball serves.

**How it works in play:**
- RICO is not a session-1 threat. It's the **campaign-level escalation**. The crew starts as freelancers doing jobs. If they keep working for the same family, using the same methods, in the same territory, they're building a RICO case whether they know it or not.
- **The threshold:** Federal prosecutors need a "pattern of racketeering activity" — two or more predicate acts within 10 years. In practice, this means the crew needs to be visibly connected to **two or more serious crimes** (armed robbery, magical assault, smuggling, bribery) before the feds start paying attention.
- **The mob's insulation:** The Five Families have been building RICO defenses for decades. Soldiers don't know capos. Capos don't know the Commission. Paper trails are laundered through legitimate businesses. The crew, as freelancers, are the **weakest link** — they're the ones who show up at the job, at the meeting, at the scene. If the feds flip a crew member, the whole chain becomes evidence.

**What the crew should fear:** Not the RICO indictment itself — that's endgame. What they should fear is **the flip**. When the feds identify the crew as the connective tissue between family operations, they'll offer a deal: testify, and walk. Refuse, and the RICO case gets built around you. This is the single most powerful lever the GM can use to create loyalty tension in the crew.

## Magic evidence rules

This is the part that makes the setting unique: **how does magic interact with the legal system?**

### The core principle: Magic is evidence, not proof

A *Zone of Truth* reading is not admissible in a New York court. Neither is a *Detect Magic* wand reading, a divination result, or a ghost's testimony. The legal system treats magical evidence the way it treats polygraph results: **investigatively useful, legally insufficient**.

This is deliberate worldbuilding. If magic could prove guilt, the families would be extinct. The ALB's position (lobbied for by the Ashford family, among others) is that magical evidence is inherently unreliable — spells can be resisted, warded against, or faked. Requiring mundane corroboration protects everyone, including the families.

### The evidence hierarchy

| Evidence type | Admissible? | Notes |
|---------------|-------------|-------|
| Physical evidence (weapons, drugs, cash, magical items) | Yes | Standard chain of custody. Magical items must be ALB-certified by a licensed appraiser (hello, Lucia Valenti). |
| Witness testimony (mundane) | Yes | Standard rules. Witnesses can be intimidated, bribed, or flipped. |
| Witness testimony (magical — e.g., *Speak with Dead*) | No | Inadmissible. But the information can lead to admissible evidence (e.g., the ghost tells you where the body is, you find the body, the body is admissible). |
| *Zone of Truth* results | No | Inadmissible. But a confession made under *Zone of Truth* that is **voluntarily repeated without magical compulsion** is admissible (the "clean room" rule — get them to say it again without the spell). |
| Divination results | No | Inadmissible. But a warrant obtained based on divination-predicted evidence is admissible if the evidence is found independently (the "magical tip" rule — same as an anonymous tip). |
| OCU incident reports | Yes (administrative) | Not criminal-court admissible, but they build the RICO pattern. ALB uses them for permit revocation and administrative hearings. |
| ALB permit records | Yes | Fully admissible. If the crew's ALB file shows a pattern of unlicensed activity, it's circumstantial evidence in a RICO case. |

### The "magical tip" rule (most important for play)

This is the single most useful rule for the GM. It works like this:

1. The crew uses magic to find something out (divination, *Scrying*, *Commune*, ghost interrogation).
2. That information is **not admissible** in court.
3. But it can lead the crew (or the OCU) to **physical evidence** that **is** admissible.
4. The physical evidence stands on its own — no one needs to explain how they knew where to look.

**Why this matters for the crew:** It means magic is incredibly useful for *finding* things but legally dangerous to *show*. A crew that uses *Scrying* to locate a rival's stash house can steal the goods, but if they're caught, the OCU can't use the scrying as evidence — they need to catch the crew in the act or find independent probable cause. Conversely, if the OCU uses divination to get a warrant, the warrant is valid as long as the evidence is found legally.

### Anti-magic defenses (what the families use)

The Five Families don't rely on the legal system to protect them from magical evidence. They use **countermeasures**:

| Defense | How it works | Who uses it |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Warded facilities** | *Mordenkainen's Private Sanctum* or equivalent blocks all divination and scrying. No magical evidence can be gathered from inside. | All families (Ashford provides the service) |
| **Magic-dampening materials** | Lead-lined rooms, cold iron fixtures, salt barriers. Reduce spell effectiveness and block low-level detection. | Moretti (burial chambers), Kowalski (processing plant) |
| **ALB permit shields** | Licensed magic use creates a legal record that the activity was permitted. Even if the permit is fraudulent, it creates reasonable doubt. | Ashford (permits-as-a-service), Chen-Okafor (divination licenses) |
| **Witness preparation** | Key NPCs are trained to resist *Zone of Truth* (Wisdom saves, *Mind Blank* amulets, or simply "I don't recall"). | All families for made members |
| **Bureaucratic delay** | The ALB liaison "loses" the report, the clerk "misplaces" the evidence log, the permit renewal "takes 6–8 weeks." | All families through owned clerks |

## Practical GM guidance

### When does law enforcement show up?

Use the **escalation ladder**:

1. **Background noise.** OCU patrol cars, news reports of magical incidents, the crew hears about a rival getting busted. No direct threat — just atmosphere.
2. **Proximity.** The crew is near an OCU response (wrong place, wrong time). A patrol car drives by during a job. A detective asks questions at a bar the crew frequents. The crew is not a target — yet.
3. **Connection.** The crew's activity shows up in an OCU report. Maybe a witness described them. Maybe their magical signature was detected. An OCU detective starts pulling threads. The crew might not know this is happening.
4. **Pressure.** The OCU or ALB takes administrative action: a permit is revoked, a safe house is flagged, a crew member's ALB file is flagged for review. The crew feels the squeeze but isn't arrested.
5. **Crisis.** The crew is directly targeted: arrested, raided, or offered a deal. This should be a mid-to-late campaign escalation, not a session-2 event.

### How do the families respond to law enforcement pressure?

- **Valenti:** Pay the fine, replace the crew member's permit, make the problem go away through the ALB clerk on their payroll. Cost of doing business.
- **Moretti:** Father Tommaso performs a "cleansing rite" (actually a *Modify Memory* on the witness through a bound ghost). Risky, but effective if the ghost cooperates.
- **Chen-Okafor:** David Chen-Okafor runs the numbers on the OCU detective — finds the pressure point (gambling debt, sick kid, career frustration) and applies it. Information is the family's weapon.
- **Kowalski:** The problem disappears. Literally. The Kowalski processing plant handles evidence, and sometimes people. This is the nuclear option and risks Commission sanction.
- **Ashford:** Marcus Ashford's "legal team" (warlocks who specialize in ALB administrative law) files motions, requests hearings, and buries the OCU in paperwork. The most expensive but cleanest approach.

### The crew's relationship with law

The crew should **never** feel fully safe from the law, but they should also never feel like the law is the primary threat. The law is weather — it comes and goes, and you dress for it. The families are the climate — they're always there, and they define what survival looks like.

The most interesting legal moments come when the crew has to choose between:
- **Using magic to solve a problem** (effective but creates evidence)
- **Using mundane methods** (safer legally but slower/riskier physically)
- **Calling in a family favor** (costs social capital, creates obligation)

## Quick reference: OCU ranks and capabilities

| Rank | Capability | Threat to crew |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Patrol officer | *Detect Magic* wand, basic training, report filing | Low — can be misdirected, bribed, or avoided |
| Senior patrol / sergeant | Better equipment, *Hold Person* authorization, precinct-level discretion | Medium — can escalate or de-escalate a situation |
| Detective | Full magical kit, ALB liaison access, investigation authority | High — can connect dots, build cases |
| ALB liaison officer | Permit certification, evidence classification, administrative authority | Very high — determines whether magic was "legal" or not |
| Federal prosecutor (RICO) | No magic, but unlimited resources and subpoena power | Campaign-level threat — the endgame |
