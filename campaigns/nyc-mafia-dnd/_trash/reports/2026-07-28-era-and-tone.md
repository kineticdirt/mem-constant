## Era lock: 1999 New York City

**Year:** 1999 (pre-9/11, pre-smartphone, pre-social-media)

**Tech snapshot:**
- Flip phones, pagers, payphones, paper maps
- Internet exists (dial-up, early broadband) but is slow, unregulated, and not a universal crime tool
- No GPS tracking, no camera in every pocket, no social media trails
- CRT monitors next to crystal balls — the campaign's visual signature

**NYC texture:**
- Pre-9/11 skyline (WTC stands), Giuliani-era policing, RICO active
- Docks still working-class, LES not fully gentrified, Washington Heights Dominican
- Graffiti on subway cars, arcades, CD-Rs, cassette tapes, Tommy Hilfiger / FUBU streetwear
- Y2K anxiety as ambient paranoia — perfect hook for the ALB's "mana-grid patch"

**What this era does for the game:**
- Communication is traceable enough to wiretap, ephemeral enough to slip (burner flip + payphone)
- No digital forensics — paper ledgers, hand-delivered envelopes, face-to-face meets
- Magic fills the gap: divination replaces surveillance vans, sending spells replace encrypted texts
- The families have institutional memory of who to bribe (pre-9/11 NYPD scale)

## Tone: crime drama, not superheroes

**Core:** The Sopranos × The Wire, shot through urban fantasy that never explains itself. Magic is infrastructure, leverage, pollution — not wonder.

**Three pillars:**

| Pillar | What it means at the table |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **Grounded procedural** | Every job is a logistics problem: route, driver, mage, bribe, backup. No heist fantasy. Tension from planning, not spell slots. |
| **City as character** | Real NYC geography matters. Vesuvio Bakery in Carroll Gardens ≠ a fantasy tavern. Boroughs have distinct identity (Brooklyn = docks/dice games, Midtown = warlock consulting, Queens = magictech black market) |
| **Magic is scarce & costly** | Most people never cast a spell. A fireball draws the OCU in 15 minutes. Healing potions cost a week's wages. +1 dagger = family heirloom. Warlock pacts = Faustian employment contracts. |

**Voice:**
- Dry humor, not slapstick. A wiseguy complaining his cleric's raise-dead fee went up.
- Violence has weight. Betrayal costs more than a scene.
- The Below (subway deep lines) = cosmic unease the mob doesn't control.

**Hard no:** Real-world gang glorification. Fantasy families, fantasy crime (smuggled artificer rings, not heroin). Borrow structure (Commission, crews, omertà) — not real violence specifics.
