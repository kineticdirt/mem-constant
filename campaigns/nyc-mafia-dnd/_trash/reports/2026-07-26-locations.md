# B-1 — Borough Location Bible (5 Keyed Spots)

**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Campaign:** NYC Mafia × D&D (1999, Brooklyn Gothic)
**Status:** DRAFT — table canon pending
**Predecessor:** `reports/2026-06-08-locations.md` (June draft, superseded by July Phase A lock)

## Design principles

Five session-ready locations across four boroughs, consistent with the July 2026 Phase A lock:
- **Era:** 1999 — no smartphones, flip phones, pagers, CRT monitors, pre-9/11 skyline
- **Tone:** Brooklyn Gothic — grounded crime procedural, low-fantasy high-density, city as character
- **Magic model:** Three-tier visibility (Public / Licensed / Hidden) with ALB tracking and OCU response
- **Families:** Valenti (Brooklyn docks), Moretti (Little Italy), Chen-Okafor (Queens), Kowalski (Staten Island), Ashford (Midtown corporate)

Each spot has: faction tie, supernatural element, practical crew function, and a "wrong" thing that goes sideways. Spread across boroughs for geographic texture. Does **not** duplicate the three opening-job locations (Sea Wolf Tattoo, Golden Dragon Mahjong, BQE Rest Stop).

## 1. Vesuvio Bakery & Backroom — Carroll Gardens, Brooklyn

**Territory:** Valenti (dock-adjacent buffer zone)

**What it looks like (1999):** A narrow corner bakery on Court Street — faded green awning, handwritten "Fresh Bread" sign in the window, a display case of cannoli and sfogliatelle behind smudged glass. A bell jingles when you walk in. The front is a legitimate Italian bakery run by the Vesuvio family (third-generation, no criminal ties). The **backroom** is accessed through a false pantry door behind the dry-goods shelf — a wood-paneled room with a Formica table, mismatched chairs, a rotary phone on the wall, and a locked filing cabinet that contains the past month's Valenti dock manifests. The phone rings when a Valenti crew leader needs a neutral-ground meeting.

**Faction tie:** Valenti neutral meeting ground. The bakery is on the border between Valenti-controlled Red Hook and Moretti-affiliated Carroll Gardens. The Vesuvio family pays protection to the Valentis but is respected by both sides — the backroom is the closest thing to a demilitarized zone in Brooklyn. Commission rules: no violence on the premises, no weapons drawn. The rule is enforced by the building itself — the pantry door was warded by a Valenti-employed artificer in '97.

**Supernatural element:** The pantry door is under a permanent *Arcane Lock* keyed to Valenti blood (Lena's thumbprint works; so does the ring worn by the current dock boss). The backroom is scry-warded — *Detect Magic* reveals a subtle abjuration hum in the doorframe. The rotary phone has an enchantment that scrambles the line against wiretaps and divination (Class-B licensed, filed under "bakery security system").

**Practical function for the crew:** Neutral meeting ground for jobs that cross family lines. Drop location for dead drops (the filing cabinet has a false bottom). Emergency safehouse — the Vesuvios keep a change of clothes, an untraceable burner flip phone, and a pre-paid ALB inspection waiver in an envelope behind the radiator. Also a good place to grab a coffee and a cannoli while waiting for a contact who's running late.

**What goes wrong:** The Valenti blood-ward on the pantry door is keyed to Lena's generation — but Lena has been seen with the PCs, and Moretti intelligence is asking questions about "the new faces at the bakery." A Moretti chaplain shows up for a pastry and recognizes one of the crew from a previous job. The backroom is supposed to be invisible, but the PCs' presence is drawing attention the Vesuvio family can't afford.

**Key NPCs:** Carmine Vesuvio (70s, baker, third-generation, never carries a weapon, knows everything about the neighborhood), his granddaughter Rosa (20s, runs the counter, studies accounting at Brooklyn College, doesn't know about the backroom).

## 2. The Gowanus Canal Safehouse — Gowanus, Brooklyn

**Territory:** No single family — Kowalski-adjacent water access

**What it looks like (1999):** A crumbling two-story brick building on the Gowanus Canal — former pump station, abandoned in the '80s, bought at a city auction by a shell company that traces back to the Kowalski family's waste-hauling front. The ground floor is a parking bay for a single van. The second floor is a single room — concrete floor, exposed pipes, a cot, a metal desk, a phone jack, a lockbox bolted to the wall. The windows are painted over from the inside. There's a dock door on the canal side that opens to a ladder down to the water. The place smells like low tide and motor oil.

**Faction tie:** Kowalski family disposable safehouse. The Kowalskis maintain a handful of these across the boroughs — locations for witnesses to stay the night, evidence to await disposal, or crew members to lay low. This one is managed by Dominika "Docks" Kowalski (see A-3). The family can deny any connection because the shell company doesn't technically lease to them — just "forgets to change the lock."

**Supernatural element:** The Gowanus Canal is historically cursed — industrial runoff mixed with alchemical waste from decades of unlicensed dumping has created a **slow necromantic bloom** in the water around the dock. Anything that dies in the canal within 200 yards of the safehouse rises as a *zombie* within 1d6 hours unless properly blessed. The Kowalskis don't talk about this. Dominika keeps a *Gentle Repose* scroll in the safehouse lockbox for "emergencies."

**Practical function for the crew:** Disposable safehouse — stay a night or two, no questions asked, no record kept. The lockbox contains a pre-paid burner flip phone, a few hundred dollars in cash, a set of car keys (a van parked in the bay — not fast, but anonymous), and the *Gentle Repose* scroll. The phone jack works for local calls. The crew can use this for: hiding from a family that's leaning on them, storing something that shouldn't be seen, or meeting a Kowalski contact off the books.

**What goes wrong:** The safehouse is supposed to be disposable, but the canal zombies are getting worse. A city inspector with the Department of Environmental Protection has been poking around the Gowanus water quality reports. If he finds the safehouse's dock door, the Kowalski chain of ownership unravels — and the family will cut ties, denying the safehouse exists. The crew staying there on that night becomes an awkward loose end.

**Key NPCs:** Dominika "Docks" Kowalski (controls access — the crew needs her approval to use this place), Piotr Kowalski (stops by once a week to restock supplies, leaves a plate of pierogi in the mini-fridge, pretends he doesn't).

## 3. St. Dymphna's Parish Hall — Lower East Side, Manhattan

**Territory:** Moretti (southern boundary — contested with Chen-Okafor influence)

**What it looks like (1999):** A red-brick church basement on East 4th Street — linoleum floor, fluorescent lights, folding tables, a kitchenette that hasn't been updated since 1972. The hall is rented out for AA meetings, community board sessions, and the occasional bingo night. The Morettis keep an office in the back — a converted storage closet with a desk, a filing cabinet of parish records, and a framed photo of Pope John Paul II. Father Tommaso Moretti (see A-3) performs weekly blessings here that double as ghost-binding maintenance for the neighborhood's unsettled dead.

**Faction tie:** Moretti family community outpost — the border where Little Italy's influence meets the Chen-Okafor orbit. The church has been Moretti territory since the 1920s, but Chen-Okafor-owned businesses are creeping north from Houston Street. The Morettis use the parish hall as a listening post: the AA meetings and community board sessions are intelligence goldmines.

**Supernatural element:** The hall is under a Class-B *Forbiddance* ward (radiant, keyed to Fiends and Undead). It doesn't affect spirits that are properly bound — they can enter during Father Tommaso's maintenance rites, which happen every Thursday. The parish records in the filing cabinet include a ghost ledger: every spirit bound by the Morettis in the last fifty years, with burial locations and binding expiry dates. This is a secret archive — the ALB doesn't know it exists, and the Church's Arcane Affairs office only knows about the above-ground bindings.

**Practical function for the crew:** Neutral ground in contested territory — the *Forbiddance* ward means no summoning, no undead, no warlock nonsense inside the hall. Information source: the ghost ledger is leverage against anyone who's lost someone to a Moretti binding (families who can't be contacted, souls that can't be moved on). Safe meet-up for crew members with Chaplain or Mage roles — Father Tommaso is a licensed cleric and can provide discreet healing or curse removal at a discount. Bingo on Thursday nights is unironically a good cover.

**What goes wrong:** The Thursday ghost-binding maintenance is overdue. Father Tommaso has been neglecting his duties (see A-3 — his crisis of faith). The bound spirits in the parish's ledger are getting restless — the *Forbiddance* ward is holding, but the ghosts *know* the maintenance is late. A crew member attending bingo might feel a tap on the shoulder and turn around to see a woman who's been dead since 1987. She wants a message delivered to her son in Bensonhurst.

**Key NPCs:** Father Tommaso Moretti (29, Chaplain's apprentice, driving a '95 DeVille, wants to digitize the ghost ledger), Tommy "Two Prayers" (covering for his cousin's lapsed maintenance — see A-3 NPC seed).

## 4. The Jamaica Market Kiosk — Jamaica, Queens

**Territory:** Chen-Okafor (eastern node)

**What it looks like (1999):** Inside the Jamaica Colosseum Mall — a bustling indoor market on Jamaica Avenue — a single kiosk on the second-floor mezzanine, no signage, just a blue tablecloth over a folding table, a cash register that doesn't work (purely decorative), and a stack of *New York Post* papers. Behind the table sits **Auntie Nkechi** (the Ifá priestess from A-3), who reads palm lines, cracks cowrie shells, and dispenses "financial advice" to a steady stream of Queens residents. The kiosk is always busy — there's a line of people waiting, a mix of regulars and first-timers. Nobody questions the woman who never seems to eat or sleep.

**Faction tie:** Chen-Okafor information triage point. The kiosk is the family's low-level divination front — walk-in traffic, no appointments, $20 reads. Auntie Nkechi doesn't just read fortunes for the public; she screens walk-ins for Chen-Okafor intelligence. Anyone who asks the wrong questions (about the family, about the Mahjong parlor on Main Street, about "the numbers") gets flagged. The kiosk is also a dead-drop relay — envelopes left under the blue tablecloth are picked up every two hours and delivered to the Flushing Counting House.

**Supernatural element:** Auntie Nkechi is a legitimate Ifá priestess with Class-C divination license. Her cowrie shell readings are genuine — she sees fragments of the next 24 hours with a high success rate. The kiosk's cash register is a warded container: any envelope placed inside it is under *Nondetection* for 8 hours. The *New York Post* stack is a misdirection — the papers contain encoded messages in the classifieds (cursive marginalia visible only through a *Detect Magic* lens).

**Practical function for the crew:** Quick divination without a formal appointment — $20 for a reading, $50 for a specific question. Dead-drop relay for messages to Chen-Okafor contacts. Low-risk meeting point (public, crowded, noisy). If the crew needs to contact David Chen-Okafor without going to the Counting House, they leave an envelope under the tablecloth with a coded letter and wait for a callback on a burner.

**What goes wrong:** The kiosk is under surveillance — not by the families, but by Detective Adaeze Chen-Okafor (David's sister, NYPD/OCU liaison — see A-3). She's off-duty, waiting in the food court, watching her aunt's kiosk. She doesn't know what the kiosk really is, but she's suspicious — and she's got a good eye for faces. If the crew visits the kiosk, she'll note them. If they visit twice, she'll run their plates.

**Key NPCs:** Auntie Nkechi (80s, Ifá priestess, sharp as a tack, doesn't trust outsiders but respects professionalism), the food-court samosa vendor (Ranjit, 40s, delivery driver cover, Chen-Okafor courier, knows everyone).

## 5. Murray Hill Pact Loft — Murray Hill, Manhattan

**Territory:** Ashford (neutral corporate — no street presence)

**What it looks like (1999):** A fifth-floor walk-up in a pre-war Murray Hill co-op on East 37th Street — the building is beige brick, doorman, lobby with a marble floor and a mail-slots wall. The loft itself is rented under a shell LLC owned by Ashford Security Consulting. Inside: polished wood floors, floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the rooftops of Midtown, an Eames lounge chair, a bookshelf of law tomes, and a locked briefcase chained to a radiator pipe. Minimalist. Clean. No personal effects. The kind of apartment that says "this is not a home."

**Faction tie:** Ashford family neutral negotiation space. When the Commission needs a face-to-face that can't happen at Ashford Security (because it would look official) or at a social club (because it would look factional), they use this loft. Julian Ashford (see A-3) keeps the lease, supplies the scotch, and never attends. The loft is also used for warlock pact consultations that don't need the full 15th-floor infrastructure — high-end clients who want to meet "off-campus."

**Supernatural element:** The loft is under *Mordenkainen's Private Sanctum* (15th-floor overflow — the same ward extends to this address). The briefcase chained to the radiator contains a **pact template** — a pre-inked warlock contract on enchanted vellum. Signing the template doesn't bind the signatory immediately; it creates a *Sending* that reaches the patron associated with the contract template. Marcus Ashford (the Consultant) keeps five templates in rotation, keyed to five different extraplanar patrons. The bookshelf has a hidden compartment containing the Ashford family's negotiation protocols — "What to say if the client asks about soul forfeiture" — written in a cypher only Julian knows.

**Practical function for the crew:** The most secure meeting space the crew can access short of Ashford Security itself. If the PCs need to negotiate with a family representative on truly neutral ground, this is where it happens. Entry requires a key from Marcus Ashford's office (or Julian's verbal authorization — difficult to get, but possible if the crew has leverage). The lockbox briefcase also contains a burner flip phone with a single programmed number — Julian's private line.

**What goes wrong:** The *Private Sanctum* is a two-way ward — it blocks scrying *into* the loft, but it also blocks scrying *out*. If the crew meets someone here who is secretly scrying *for* a third party, that person can't be reached in an emergency. More pressingly, one of the five pact templates in the briefcase has been *modified* — someone (an Ashford junior associate, possibly Priya?) has replaced one of the patron contacts with their own. The next person who signs that template will form a pact with something that isn't on the Ashford approved list.

**Key NPCs:** Priya Mehta (Marcus's assistant — handles loft scheduling, knows the door code, has her own key to the briefcase), a building doorman named Frankie (60s, former NYPD, sees everyone who comes and goes, sells that information to the highest bidder — currently working for Kowalski intelligence).

## Thematic connections

- **Vesuvio Bakery** and **Murray Hill Pact Loft** form the campaign's two "civilized" meeting options — one blue-collar (bakery, cash, no questions), one corporate (loft, scotch, contracts). A crew's choice of meeting ground says something about their style.
- **Gowanus Canal Safehouse** and **St. Dymphna's** represent the two poles of supernatural infrastructure — one practical and grimy (safehouse, canal zombies, disposable), one sacred and decaying (warded hall, ghost ledger, lapsed maintenance).
- **Jamaica Market Kiosk** is the wildcard — public, cheap, and exposed. It's the easiest location to access and the hardest to use without being seen.
- All five connect to the **Five Families expansion** (A-3) and the **opening job** (A-4): the bakery is where Lena's cutout works; the safehouse could hold the courier Danny; the parish ghost ledger might contain the spirit of the ledger's last courier; the kiosk is a route to Chen-Okafor divination for the job; the pact loft is where Harlow "Cipher" Duvall could be found.
