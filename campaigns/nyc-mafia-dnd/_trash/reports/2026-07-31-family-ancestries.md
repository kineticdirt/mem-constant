# Family ancestries — draft for table

*2026-07-31 · Agent worldbuilding draft — C-1*

---

## The question

Are the Five Families **human-only**, or do dwarven capos, goblin runners, and tiefling fixers operate alongside humans in the grey market?

## Answer: Mixed, with a veneer of "don't ask"

**The Commission surface is human.** The don of every family presents human to the public, to politicians, and to the ALB. Beneath that surface, every family runs mixed crews — ancestries are a practical reality nobody talks about in formal settings.

### Why not human-only?

1. **The underworld is meritocratic in the worst way.** A goblin who can squeeze through a duct to disable a ward is more valuable than one who can't. A dwarf who can smell forged scrolls before they're stamped is worth their weight in gold. Ancestry is skill in the life — and the life doesn't discriminate against useful people.

2. **Magic is infrastructure, and some ancestries have better tools.** Dragonborn resistance in a fire-walking job. Elven darkvision in the Below. Tiefling natural charisma for a confidence game. The families didn't *choose* integration; they *acquired* it because the other families were using it.

3. **The ALB doesn't track ancestry.** Licenses are tied to operators, not races. A dwarf licensed for artificer work is legal regardless of what the Commission *assumes* about dwarves. The bureaucratic loophole protects mixed crews — as long as nobody makes it political.

### The social reality

| Group | Status in the Families | Stereotype (unspoken) |
|-------|----------------------|----------------------|
| **Humans** | Default — every don is human | "Trustworthy because they look like the license photo" |
| **Dwarves** | Respected in Valenti (artificer) and Chen-Okafor (numbers) | "Drink hard, count harder, don't flip" |
| **Goblins** | The Ledger is majority goblin-run; other families subcontract | "Fast, cheap, and they'll pocket one in ten" |
| **Tieflings** | Ashford territory — pacts, persuasion, "consulting" | "Too pretty to be honest, too sharp to cross" |
| **Elves** | Rare — mostly independent diviners or ghost-talkers | "Don't stay long; they see too much" |
| **Half-orcs** | Muscle — Kowalski and Moretti primarily | "Paid to break, not to think" |
| **Dragonborn** | Very rare — usually freelance bodyguards or enforcers | "Fireproof and loyal to the contract, not the family" |
| **Halflings** | Kitchen crews, inn fronts, restaurant fronts | "Invisible — you forget they're there until the books are cooked" |

### What this means for the table

- **PC ancestries are wide open.** A dwarf Valenti enforcer, a tiefling Ashford accountant, a goblin Ledger fixer — all fit. The social friction (old-school human capos who mutter about "non-human help") is a playable tension, not a hard wall.
- **Crew dynamics play naturally.** A mixed crew is assumed; a *human-only* crew is suspicious (what are they hiding? who are they excluding?).
- **Commission sit-downs are human-mask formality.** Behind closed doors, everyone knows Capo Martino's best collector is a goblin and the Ashford "consultants" are half tiefling. The fiction is maintained because it's useful, not because anyone believes it.

### Tension hooks

- A human-only crew in Moretti turf is making noise — bad for business, good for a shakedown.
- A goblin runner in Chen-Okafor territory was caught with a message to the Valenti artificers. Accident or double agent?
- The Commission is considering a formal "majority-human sit-down" rule — which three families would lose their voting rep overnight?
