## The core rule: Three tiers

Not all magic is equally visible. The question isn't "does magic exist?" — it's "who sees what, and what does it cost them?"

### Tier 1: Public — Street magic (everyone knows)

Magic so mundane it's treated like a trade skill. No concealment needed.

| What | Who sees it | Who provides it |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| Healing kits (Cure Wounds potion at a bodega) | Anyone with cash | Licensed ALB apothecaries |
| Cantrip locksmith (Mage Hand to jimmy a car door) | The customer | Any "fixer" with the cantrip |
| Good-luck charms (blessing for a card game) | The gambler | Street clerics, hustlers |
| Diviner-for-hire (find a lost phone, track a debtor) | The client | Backroom "consultants" |

**Rule:** Public magic doesn't draw heat. The NYPD won't raid a bodega for selling healing potions — they'll fine it for no sales tax.

### Tier 2: Hidden — Underworld magic (family business)

Magic that would scare civilians, tip off cops, or break the Commission's rules. This is the party's primary operating layer.

| What | Who knows | Why it's hidden |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Combat spells (Fire Bolt, Magic Missile) | The shooter and the target | Draws attention; bullet wounds get treated, *burn* wounds get questions |
| Necromancy services (river disposal) | The Kowalski network | Straight-up illegal; witness tampering evidence |
| Warded rooms (private, scry-proof) | The renter and the contractor | Looks like a normal office; ALB only inspects if reported |
| Forged spell scrolls | The Valenti supply chain | Counterfeit magic — no QC, dangerous, ALB-sanctionable |
| Planar contact (summoning, pacts) | The caster and the thing | Unlicensed; attracts The Below's attention |

**Rule:** Hidden magic gets the party *made* if exposed. A Fire Bolt in a public housing courtyard means someone called 911 — and the OCU has a "thermal event" file.

### Tier 3: Licensed — Regulated magic (ALB permits)

Legal magic that requires paperwork, inspectors, and mob-owned supply chains.

| What | License needed | How the families profit |
|------|----------------|------------------------|
| Permanent enchantments (wards, locks) | ALB Class-2 Permit | Valenti sells forged permits at 60% of the real price |
| Commercial divination (fortune-telling parlors) | ALB Seer License | Chen-Okafor owns the inspection route |
| Clerical services (funeral rites, blessings) | ALB Chaplain Endorsement | Moretti controls the burial permits |
| Warlock consultancy (pact management) | ALB Binding Agent License | Ashford writes the contracts |

**Rule:** Licensed magic is the *front*. Every legitimate ALB permit has a mob kickback baked in. The party can use licensed magic freely — as long as their paperwork is clean.

## What this means for play

| Situation | Visibility tier | Risk |
|-----------|----------------|------|
| Healing a civilian in public | Public | Low — looks like a strong painkiller |
| Scrying a rival's counting room | Hidden | Medium — if detected, the family will retaliate |
| Using Fire Bolt in a shootout | Hidden | High — OCU opens a file |
| Running a fortune-telling parlor | Licensed | Low — pay your ALB kickback on time |
| Animating a corpse in Staten Island | Hidden | Very high — Kowalski's specialty, but every family watches |
| Selling healing potions from a food cart | Public | Low — get a vendor license and pay taxes |

---

## Party cheat sheet

- **Public** = cast cantrips freely, carry potions, use Light/Mage Hand in normal view
- **Hidden** = conceal components, avoid witnesses, have a cover story
- **Licensed** = keep your ALB card current, know which inspector owns which family
- **The OCU** doesn't know about Tier 3 licenses — they chase Tier 2 incidents
- **The ALB** doesn't enforce Tier 2 — they collect fees on Tier 3
