## Valenti — Brooklyn / Docks & Union Smuggling

The Valentis control waterfront Brooklyn — Red Hook to Sunset Park — and every crate, container, and cursed cargo that moves through it. Their real business isn't the union; it's the **artificer forgery rings** that let a pallet of untraceable wands clear customs as "industrial electronics." Valenti enforcers carry alchemical stunners disguised as mechanic's tools; their safehouses are warehouse lofts with dwarf-forged wards over roll-up doors. When a shipment of pact-forged goods goes missing, Valenti sends the *dock boss* — who knows every crane operator's name and which ALB inspector takes cash. The family operates on volume, not prestige: they make coin on every crate that touches a pier, legal or otherwise, and their books are the messiest in the Commission — intentional, because auditors can't audit what they can't inventory.

**NPC seed — Lena "The Grease" Valenti:** 38, dock boss for the Red Hook terminal. Lena wears steel-toed boots and a gold chain with a St. Michael pendant (consecrated at a licensed shrine). She signs invoices by pressing her thumb onto enchanted paper that glows if the amount is wrong. The PCs know her as the person who can smuggle anything out of Brooklyn — for a price or a favor, never cash. She has two kids in Catholic school and a wizard ex-husband who owes child support. Her ambition: open a route through the Holland Tunnel that bypasses the Moretti family's inspection fee.

## Moretti — Little Italy / Protection & Clerical Services

The Morettis sit at the historic heart of the New York underworld — Mulberry Street social clubs, linen-tablecloth restaurants, and a network of **cleric "chaplains"** who perform licensed blessings, bind ghosts to their burial sites, and whisper last rites that cost a fortune. Their protection racket is the city's oldest: every restaurant, bakery, and funeral home below Houston pays the Morettis, and in return, a Chaplain sweeps the premises for lingering spirits every Thursday. The family holds tight to old-country tradition — the Don's eldest son must serve a year as a Chaplain's apprentice before he can hold rank. Their real power, though, is the **burial rite registry**: the Morettis know who died with debts unsettled, which souls are bound, and which graves hold more than bones. The Chaplains answer to the Church's Arcane Affairs office, not the ALB — a jurisdictional loophole the family defends fiercely.

**NPC seed — Tommy "Two Prayers" Moretti:** 29, underboss-in-waiting, Chaplain's apprentice. Tommy drives a '95 Cadillac DeVille with a ward glyph in the glovebox. He handles the "delicate" blessings — soothing a restaurant owner whose dead partner won't stop haunting the kitchen, officiating a funeral where the body is also evidence. He wants to modernize the family: digitize the burial registry, buy a cellphone for every crew leader. His uncle (the Don) thinks cellphones are for degenerates. Tommy walks the line between filial loyalty and the creeping sense that the Moretti way is a gilded cage.

## Chen-Okafor — Queens / Gambling & Divination Fixing

The Chen-Okafors are the youngest Commission family — a coalition of Chinese and Nigerian underworld networks that consolidated in the late '80s — and they control the Flushing-to-Jamaica corridor with an iron grip on **gambling and divination-fixed numbers**. Every mahjong parlor, backroom poker game, and OTB satellite in Queens runs on their terms because they own the *luck*. Their diviners are Class-C licensed (on paper) but operate far beyond permitted limits: they read the odds on horse races, card draws, and dice throws before they happen, and the family adjusts the vig accordingly. The Chen-Okafors don't break legs — they break probability. Their money is the cleanest in the organization because it's never really "won" from anyone; it's just the statistical edge, weaponized. This makes them the most useful and most resented family at the Commission table. The older families don't trust a house that sees the future.

**NPC seed — David Chen-Okafor:** 34, the family's public face — runs a "community lending" office on Main Street, Flushing. David wears tailored suits and never carries cash; he pays for everything with credit cards that have perfect credit scores. He keeps a loaded divination die in his breast pocket — it always rolls 20 — but he has never used it. "Knowing the outcome," he tells the PCs, "is a kind of poverty." His father (co-founder) was recently diagnosed with early dementia, and David is fighting his cousins for succession. He needs the PCs to deliver a message to a Valenti crew leader who's been skimming from a joint smuggling route, and he needs it delivered *in person*, without magic — because the diviners told him a scry-message would be intercepted.

## Kowalski — Staten Island / Waste, Logistics & Disappearing

The Kowalskis are the Commission's trash men — literally. They own the waste-hauling contracts for three boroughs, run the Fresh Kills landfill's "special disposal" operation, and operate a fleet of trucks that move things the Department of Sanitation doesn't know exist. Their **necromancy taboo** is an open secret: rivals say the Kowalskis talk to the river, that bodies dropped in the Kill Van Kull never surface because they're *busy*, that the family patriarch keeps an ancestor in the basement. The family denies everything, but their drivers carry iron bells and never drive alone at night. What the Kowalskis actually do with their logistics network is move things that can't be moved any other way — evidence, witnesses, cursed artifacts, summoned entities that need relocation, and the occasional person who just needs to *not exist* for six months. They are the family nobody likes but everybody uses. The Commission tolerates them because garbage trucks can cross any checkpoint, and because asking too many questions about Kowalski business would reveal everyone else's.

**NPC seed — Dominika "Docks" Kowalski:** 45, logistics coordinator for the Bayonne-to-Staten Island route. Dominika is a heavyset woman in a reflective vest who keeps a rosary in one pocket and a *gentle repose* scroll in the other. She treats the family's macabre reputation as a business asset: "They're afraid of us," she says. "Good. Fear sets a fair price." She lives in a row house on Bard Avenue with three cats and a basement door that she has never opened. She needs the PCs to find out who's been dumping bodies in *her* landfill — corpses that weren't Kowalski-authorized — because if OCU traces them, the city revokes their hauling license and the family's cover is blown.

## Ashford — Midtown / Money Laundering & Warlock Pacts

The Ashford family doesn't look like a crime family. They operate from a 14th-floor office on Madison Avenue with a receptionist, a conference room, and a coffee machine that makes excellent espresso. They are the Commission's **white-collar arm**: money laundering, political connections, real estate, and **warlock pacts framed as corporate consulting agreements**. An Ashford "consultant" doesn't cast spells — they *negotiate* with entities that do, drawing up contracts in infernal legalese, structuring payment as deferred equity, and ensuring that when the pact comes due, the liability sits with a shell company in Delaware. The Ashfords are the cleanest suits in the room, which makes them the most dangerous. They don't carry weapons; they carry briefcases with enchantments that make judges sympathetic. Don Ashford serves as the Commission's neutral party — he hosts the quarterly sit-downs in his office, serves scotch, and never votes. The other families trust his neutrality because they don't understand what he gets out of it, and that confusion is the point.

**NPC seed — Julian Ashford:** 55, managing partner, Don of the Ashford family. Julian has never held a weapon, never ordered a hit, never personally touched contraband. He wears bespoke suits, plays squash at the University Club, and has a wife who thinks he's a corporate lawyer. His office has a single painting — a Renaissance-era portrait of a man whose eyes follow you — that he insists is an investment. Julian is the one person in New York who knows the *full* map of every family's magical debts, pacts, and dependencies. He keeps this knowledge in his head, not on paper. The PCs might encounter him when they need something erased from the Commission's collective memory — and the price is always a future favor, never a present payment.

## Commission dynamics

The Five Families maintain the peace through the Commission — quarterly sit-downs hosted by Ashford, with rules that keep the city profitable:

- **No magic hits on civilians without a vote.** Broken often, but never acknowledged.
- **Territorial disputes go to the Commission, not the street.** Going to war costs more than arbitration — the Ashfords bill both sides.
- **The Ledger** (independent fixers) is the neutral delivery service. Any family can hire them, and no family can kill them.
- **OCU is everyone's problem.** When the heat gets too high, the Commission pools resources to bribe, discredit, or reassign the investigators.
