## Era: 1990s (1995–1999, pre-9/11 NYC)

The premise seed said "present-day (or near-present)" — this report locks the era as **the late 1990s** and explains why.

### Why the 1990s

| Factor | Why it wins |
|--------|-------------|
| **Mafia's cultural peak** | The Sopranos, Goodfellas, Casino — the 90s is the *era* of the Italian-American crime story in the public imagination. Players arrive with the right vibe baked in. |
| **No smartphones** | A flip phone can't GPS-track, video-stream, or photo-ID. Magic feels occult, not surveilled. A divination wizard is the *only* way to find someone — not Life360. |
| **Gritty NYC texture** | Pre-gentrification, pre-Giuliani cleanup. Times Square still had peep shows. Abandoned piers, graffiti-covered trains, meatpacking district at night. The city breathes danger. |
| **RICO pressure** | The late 90s is the peak of federal racketeering. Every family is sweating informants, wiretaps, and flipped capos. Paranoia is the default — great for drama. |
| **Limited surveillance** | No Ring cameras, no social media, no facial recognition. A witness can actually *disappear*. A necromancer dumping a body in the Gowanus might get away with it. |
| **Internet as a tool, not a god** | Dial-up, AOL chat rooms, IRC. A hacker/technomancer PC is viable but not trivially omniscient. The web is a *place you go* — slow, weird, and unindexed. |
| **Distinct identity** | "Modern day NYC but D&D" is generic. *1990s NYC but D&D* is a *choice* — a specific, cinematic, and immediately evocative setting. |

### What stays the same

The five families, the Commission, the Occult Crimes Unit, the Arcane Licensing Board, and the Below — all portable. No setting element in `premise.md` or `factions.md` depends on the decade. The only change is the *texture*: suit cuts, car models, payphones, the smell of the subway.

## Tone note — "Mob noir, low magic, high heat"

One page, one voice. This is the note to read before writing any session or NPC.

### The feeling

_The city is wet. The food is good. The loyalty is real but the loyalty is also a leash._

### Three pillars

| Pillar | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Crime drama stakes** | Violence costs. A bullet is a bullet. Magic is a resource, not a spectacle. Every job is a transaction — money, favor, or survival. |
| **Supernatural as contraband** | No fireball in the street. Magic is a *practice*: warded rooms, forged scrolls, a cleric who runs a fake funeral home to launder healing. The Commission votes on who can sell what. |
| **Paranoia and loyalty** | RICO, informants, wiretaps. The families are families — you can't quit. The closest friend might be the rat. The underworld is held together by contracts and blood, not trust. |

### What's not in the tone

- **No heroic fantasy.** You're not saving the world. You're trying to get paid, stay alive, and maybe not disappoint your father.
- **No cyberpunk.** The 1990s are not the 2090s. No netrunning, no chrome, no megacorps. The tech is a Nokia 5110 and a pager clipped to a belt.
- **No parody.** The tone is serious, not campy. The supernatural elements are frightening, not silly. Dark humor is fine — "I can't believe this is my life" humor, not cartoonish.

### The magic rule

> **If a spell would be visible in a public place, it's a problem.**

Most magic is subtle: enchanted objects, ritual circles, bargains made in basements. If a wizard casts *fireball* in a Midtown diner, the Commission, the NYPD OCU, and whatever is in the Below all take an interest — and none of them are friendly.

### Closing image

A payphone rings at 2 a.m. in a Brooklyn bodega parking lot. A man in a leather jacket answers. The voice on the line says, "The ledger is real. The Valenti kid is dead. Don't go home." Rain on the asphalt. The streetlight buzzes. Somewhere in the tunnel below, something moves.
