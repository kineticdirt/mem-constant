## OCU — Organized Crime Unit (Arcane Division)

The NYPD's Organized Crime Unit has a **dedicated Arcane Division** — a squad of detectives with ALB cross-training, *detect magic* certification, and a rolling budget of jurisdictional headaches. They're not part of the ALB (which is a licensing/regulatory agency), they're **cops** — they build cases, execute warrants, and sit on federal task forces.

| OCU asset | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Staff** | ~12 detectives, 2 forensic mages, 1 data analyst (Access database, dial-up feed from ALB permit records) |
| **Arcane Trace Unit** | 3 detectives with *detect magic* at-will (ALB-certified), respond to Tier 3 spillover calls. Carry warded evidence bags. |
| **Informant network** | Paid ALB clerks, disgruntled family associates, turf informants. OCU pays in cash and reduced sentences. |
| **Equipment** | Enchanted handcuffs (anti-teleportation field, 10 ft range), warded interview rooms (scry-proof, Class-B), *zone of truth* polygraph (needs warrant + DA approval) |

**OCU's blind spot:** The Arcane Division has one assigned forensic mage per shift. A major incident in Brooklyn means Manhattan pulls its mage — response time jumps from 15 minutes to 45. The families know this schedule and exploit it.

**Key OCU NPC — Detective Adaeze Chen-Okafor** (introduced in B-1, Jamaica Market Kiosk): NYPD detective, younger sister of David Chen-Okafor, off-duty surveillance at the family's divination kiosk. She's not corrupt but she's **family** — her loyalties are untested. At the table, she can be a source of intel, a surveillance problem, or an ally who goes to ground when the case gets too close to Queens.

## RICO — Federal Pressure on the Families

The Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act is the **feds' hammer** against the Five Families. In the campaign's 1999 setting, Giuliani-era RICO enforcement is at its peak — the SDNY (Southern District of New York) has a dedicated **Arcane RICO Task Force** that combines federal prosecutors with DOJ-contracted diviners.

| RICO element | How it works |
|--------------|--------------|
| **Predicate acts** | Magic used to further a criminal enterprise counts as a RICO predicate. Unlicensed casting for profit, ALB bribery, smuggling pact-forged items — each is a predicate. Two or more within 10 years = RICO charge. |
| **Magic-specific predicates** | Necromancy (any), summoning a hostile entity, *modify memory* on a witness, *dominate person* in furtherance of a scheme. These carry a mandatory RICO enhancement. |
| **Asset forfeiture** | The feds can seize warded properties, alchemical stockpiles, and pact-forged items under civil forfeiture. The Ashfords have a standing retainer for forfeiture challenges. |
| **Grand jury timeline** | The opening job's cursed ledger has a **10-day grand jury deadline**. Federal grand juries sit in 1999 with arcane evidence specialists who review spell residue reports and ALB compliance records before issuing indictments. |

**RICO at the table:** The threat is existential, not mechanical. A RICO indictment doesn't mean a fight scene — it means frozen assets, tapped phones, cooperating witnesses, and every family cutting ties with the indicted crew. The PCs aren't RICO targets themselves (too small) but their **employers** are. If Valenti gets indicted, the dock routes shut down, Lena goes to ground, and the crew loses its primary income stream.

## Magic Evidence Rules — Admissibility & Chain of Custody

Magic evidence in 1999 NYC occupies a confused legal space — case law is thin, and the SDNY's arcane evidence rules are still being drafted.

| Evidence type | Admissible? | Notes |
|---------------|-------------|-------|
| **Spell residue** (forensic *detect magic* reading) | Yes — with sworn affidavit from a certified forensic mage | Must be collected within 24 hours. Defense can challenge the mage's certification. |
| **Zone of truth statement** | Only as corroboration — cannot stand alone | NY Court of Appeals ruled *People v. Marchetti* (1995): "Arcane truth-compulsion violates the right against self-incrimination if used as primary evidence." |
| **Scry vision testimony** | No — hearsay per *People v. Kowalski* (1989) | A diviner's report is not considered direct evidence. However, scry footage **can** be used to obtain a search warrant. |
| **Cursed object as evidence** | Yes — but requires blessing + dispel before handling | Chain of custody: two officers must witness the blessing. The cursed ledger from the opening job is a nightmare — it can't be blessed without destroying the ink, and it can't be examined without the examiner going catatonic. |
| **Witness with *modify memory* exposure** | Inadmissible — automatic mistrial if discovered | The SDNY maintains a *modify memory* victim registry. Any witness who has been magically tampered with is permanently barred from testifying in arcane RICO cases. |
| **Pact contract (infernal, signed)** | Admissible — treated as a written agreement under the Uniform Commercial Code | The Ashfords structure pacts as "consulting agreements" precisely to keep them under UCC, not criminal law. A raw infernal contract written in Abyssal? That's harder to introduce — requires a certified translator. |

**Chain of custody rule (the "Ward-Lock" problem):** Enchanted or warded objects must be secured by a licensed forensic mage before entering evidence lockup. If the chain breaks (the object was handled by an unlicensed person, the ward was dispelled without documentation, the evidence bag wasn't anti-scry-lined), the evidence is tainted. The families exploit this relentlessly — a Valenti dock crew caught with cursed cargo can claim the ward was broken during the arrest, and without a clean chain, the cargo becomes "unattributed magical material."

## At the Table — OCU Heat Scale

Use this when the crew's actions draw law enforcement attention:

| Heat level | OCU response | Table cue |
|------------|-------------|-----------|
| **1 — Notice** | ALB flag in permit database. Routine inquiry. | "Your Class-C permit renewal was flagged for follow-up. Expect a letter in 3–5 business days." |
| **2 — Interest** | Detective makes calls. Checks informant network. | "Your name came up in OCU chatter. Not a case. Yet." |
| **3 — Surveillance** | Physical tail, phone tap (warrant), ALB records subpoena. | "You notice the same gray sedan on Court Street three mornings in a row." |
| **4 — Raid** | Warrant + Arcane Trace Unit entry. *Detect magic* sweep of premises. Seizure of warded items. | "Four unmarked cars box in the bakery. Three detectives and a forensic mage approach the front door." |
| **5 — RICO charge** | Federal indictment. Asset freeze. Grand jury subpoena. | "The DA's office sent a letter. Not certified mail — hand-delivered. You know what that means." |

## Summary

> The law in 1999 NYC knows magic exists, has barely caught up, and is fighting a war against five families who understand the rules better than the people who wrote them. OCU has the tools but not the budget; RICO has the teeth but moves slowly; and cursed evidence sits in a legal grey zone that keeps defense attorneys employed and prosecutors frustrated.

The PCs navigate this by **staying below Heat 3** on family jobs and **below Heat 2** on personal business. A single unlicensed *fireball* jumps the meter straight to 4.
