# A-5 — PC Role Menu

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Campaign:** NYC Mafia × D&D
**Item:** A-5 — PC role menu (6–8 archetypes)

---

## Design notes

The party are **operators** — freelancers, family-adjacent specialists, or independents who get hired for jobs that cross family lines. Classes map to **roles**, not cosplay: a "wizard" is a corporate sorcerer with a burner phone and an ALB permit; a "rogue" is a fixer who knows which precinct clerk takes bribes. Each role below maps to a fantasy class (or multiclass), a crime-world function, a suggested motivation hook, and a **family tie** option to connect the character to the setting.

---

## The Roles

### 1. The Fixer (Rogue — Mastermind / Arcane Trickster)

**Function:** Moves information, money, and people. Knows which doors open with cash, which with a threat, which with a spell.

**Crime-world role:** You are the one who sets up the job, names the price, and makes the introduction. You find buyers for stolen goods, alibis for soldiers, and witnesses who forget what they saw.

**Class notes:** Rogue mechanics cover it cleanly — Expertise in Persuasion/Deception, Cunning Action for the urban chase scene. Arcane Trickster adds the supernatural edge: Mage Hand to pick pockets through walls, minor enchantments on burner phones.

**Family tie options:**
- Valenti dock contact — you grew up on the waterfront, learned to read cargo manifests and people.
- Ledger freelancer — you've been independent for years, and it's catching up to you.

**Opening question:** Who do you owe, and what did they do to collect last time?

---

### 2. The Enforcer (Fighter — Eldritch Knight / Champion)

**Function:** Shows up when the fixer's deal breaks down. Intimidation with a body count.

**Crime-world role:** Muscle, but smart muscle. You're not the one starting the fight — you're the one ending it, or making sure it never starts. You pull security details, run protection rackets, and handle "recoveries."

**Class notes:** Fighter chassis with Second Wind as the thing that keeps you standing in the third act. Eldritch Knight for the warlock-adjacent supernatural vibe (enchanted weapons, Shield spell when the bullets fly).

**Family tie options:**
- Moretti foot soldier — you used to carry their ghost-bound relics and you're done with that life.
- Kowalski problem-solver — Staten Island doesn't let go easy; you're still on call.

**Opening question:** What's inside you that made you this good at hurting people — discipline, rage, or something that isn't entirely yours?

---

### 3. The Warlock (Warlock — Pact of the Chain or Blade)

**Function:** You made a deal. Someone (or something) gave you power, and now you're working it off — or working around the terms.

**Crime-world role:** In-house supernatural consultant. You handle the jobs that need more than bullets and charm: warding a counting room, hexing a rival, calling something from the deep tunnels. You're the specialist every family wants on retainer and nobody wants at the dinner table.

**Class notes:** Invocations as the fine print of your contract. Your patron could be an Ashford client (extrapolating the Ashford "consulting" model), a dead god in the subway tunnels, or something in the Kowalski river.

**Family tie options:**
- Brokered by Ashford — your contract came through their "consulting" desk, and you handle the cases they don't want internally.
- Independent pact — you found something in the Below and made your own deal. Nobody knows. Yet.

**Opening question:** What does your patron actually want, and why are you sure it's what they told you?

---

### 4. The Cleric (Cleric — Grave / Life / Twilight Domain)

**Function:** You tend the dead, guard the living, and make sure the rituals work.

**Crime-world role:** Spiritual services with a body count. You run last rites for soldiers who can't go to a church. You ward the vaults, bless the shipments, bind the restless dead. The Morettis have chaplains; the Valentis need one who works the docks; every family needs someone who can lay hands on a dying soldier in a safehouse at 3am.

**Class notes:** Cleric spells map to urban fantasy seamlessly — *Spare the Dying*), *Detect Magic* (ALB compliance check), *Speak with Dead* (interrogate the inconveniently deceased). Twilight Domain for the "guardian in the dark vibe"; Grave for "I keep the dead in line."

**Family tie options:**
- Former Moretti chaplain — defrocked or excommunicated, you kept the gift and lost the collar.
- Independent occult medic — you work triage for whoever pays, underground clinics in Flatbush, the Bronx.

**Opening question:** Do you believe in what you do, or do you just know how to do it?

---

### 5. The Sorcerer (Sorcerer — Wild Magic / Aberrant Mind)

**Function:** Magic is in your bloodline — or it got there by accident. Either way, you didn't study it and you can't unlearn it.

**Crime-world role:** You're the unpredictable one. Maybe your magic comes from a Chen-Okafor divination line that went sideways, or a planar bleed incident in the deep subway, or a Kowalski river-dead ancestor you never met. You handle jobs that need raw firepower or reality-bending precision, and everyone's a little nervous when you reach for your spell.

**Class notes:** Wild Magic for the "uncontrolled supernatural in a controlled world" tension; Aberrant Mind for the planar-bleed angle. Metamagic as the thing that makes you dangerously versatile.

**Family tie options:**
- Chen-Okafor bloodline — you're a cousin nobody talks about, and your magic doesn't match their clean divination model.
- Unknown origin — you woke up with this. The ALB doesn't have a file on you, and that's dangerous.

**Opening question:** What happened the first time your magic went wrong, and who saw it?

---

### 6. The Bard (Bard — College of Eloquence / Whispers)

**Function:** You talk for a living. Negotiation, blackmail, social engineering, and the occasional enchantment.

**Crime-world role:** Consigliere adjacent. You sit in on Commission meetings, broker truces, run the family's public relations (or their blackmail archive). You know where every body is buried because you were in the room when they decided to dig.

**Class notes:** Bardic Inspiration as the "you owe me" economy of favors. Whispers for the blackmail angle; Eloquence for the negotiator who never fails a Persuasion check.

**Family tie options:**
- Moretti social club regular — you grew up around the old families and learned to read a room before you could read.
- Ashford "consultant" — you've been on the corporate side and you're tired of the suits.

**Opening question:** What's the one piece of information you carry that would bring down a family — and why haven't you used it?

---

### 7. The Ranger (Ranger — Gloom Stalker / Swarmkeeper)

**Function:** You know the city's bones — the tunnels, the rooftops, the places the maps don't show.

**Crime-world role:** Courier, tracker, and urban predator in the night. You move things through the subway deep lines, across rooftops, over the bridges. You're the one who knows how to get a wounded soldier out of Red Hook at midnight without being seen by OCU *or* the rival crew watching the docks.

**Class notes:** Urban re-skin of the ranger chassis — Favored Terrain (urban, underground, or aquatic), Natural Explorer as street-level navigation, Gloom Stalker for the "you see in the dark when the power goes out" horror vibe.

**Family tie options:**
- Kowalski tunnel runner — you've been through the deep lines, and what you saw down there made you leave.
- Independent courier — you move packages for The Ledger and you don't ask what's in them (mostly).

**Opening question:** What did you see in the deep tunnels that you can't tell anyone about?

---

### 8. The Artificer (Artificer — Armorer / Alchemist)

**Function:** You make things. Enchanted things. Illegal things, usually.

**Crime-world role:** The valentis' forgery ring runs on enchanted manifests — someone has to enchant them. The Ashford warlock pact brokerage needs warded contracts — someone has to ward them. You're the one who maintains the family's supernatural infrastructure: the counting room wards, the enchanted phones, the safe house sigils that keep divination out.

**Class notes:** Infusions as the bread-and-butter; tinkering as the flavor. Armorer if you want the "combat-ready enforcer with a tech edge" vibe; Alchemist for the support-focused healer/buffer.

**Family tie options:**
- Valenti artificer line — you've been in the forgery business since apprenticeship and you're getting out.
- Ashford contractor — you've been designing ward-systems for the corporate side and you're ready to go independent.

**Opening question:** What did you build that you wish you could unbuilt?

---

### Party composition advice

- **Minimum viable crew:** Fixer + Enforcer + one magic-user. The fixer gets the job, the enforcer handles the violence, the magic-user handles everything else.
- **Recommended crew size:** 4–5 players. One from each functional quadrant: social (Fixer/Bard), violence (Enforcer/Ranger), magic (Warlock/Cleric/Sorcerer), technical (Artificer/Sorcerer).
- **Conflict hook:** Mix family ties. A Moretti defector and an Ashford lawyer in the same crew creates built-in tension. A Kowalski tunnel runner and a Valenti dock kid have history they haven't discussed yet.
