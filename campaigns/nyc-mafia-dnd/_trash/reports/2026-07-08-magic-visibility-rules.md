# A-2 — Magic visibility rules

**Date:** 2026-07-08
**Campaign:** NYC Mafia × D&D
**Item:** A-2 — Public / hidden / licensed framework
**Proposed model:** **"Licensed Grey"** (see below)

---

## The open question from premise.md

> "Magic **public knowledge** or **mostly hidden**?"

Neither binary fully fits what the worldbuilding so far implies. The factions doc already describes the **Arcane Licensing Board (ALB)** — a state bureaucracy that issues legal magic permits. The ALB only makes sense if magic is **acknowledged to exist**, but it's not a normal part of daily life. This suggests a middle ground.

---

## Proposed: Three-tier visibility

### Tier 1 — Legal (licensed, visible)

Low-power magic has a **permitting process** through the ALB. Think of it like a concealed-carry license, a barber's license, or a TV broadcast license — you can get one if you have clean record, pay the fee, and pass a baseline competency check.

**What's legal:**
- Cantrip-level utility (detect poison, light, mending, purify food)
- Divination for business (zone variance, crop yield, traffic patterns — not people)
- Clerical rites registered with a recognized congregation
- Alchemy ingredients for industrial use (solvents, preservatives)
- Warding on commercial buildings (fire safety, burglar deterrent)

**Who has a license:**
- ALB-registered clerics in hospitals / funeral homes
- Licensed diviners for stock-market firms
- Industrial alchemists (cleaning corps, construction)
- Security wards companies (licensed by the city)

**Who enforces:** NYPD Occult Crimes Unit (OCU) raids unlicensed operations — but their budget is small and half the force is on somebody's payroll.

### Tier 2 — Grey (unlicensed, invisible)

This is the **family economy**. Everything the law bans but the city can't stop:

- Combat magic (firebolt, magic missile, web, hold person — ALB never licenses weapons)
- Illusion / enchantment for witness tampering, blackmail, cons
- Necromancy (Kowalski specialty — ALB refuses every application on principle)
- Artificer forgery (Valenti — cloned spell scrolls, counterfeit warded locks)
- Warlock pacts (Ashford — structured as "consulting agreements," no ALB paper trail)
- Teleportation circles (for moving product — registered circles are monitored)

**The Commission's rule:** no magic hits on **civilians** without a vote. This is about keeping the grey market **grey** — every dead innocent brings the OCU budget request and a Special City Council hearing.

The families don't hide that magic exists. They hide **what they do with it**.

### Tier 3 — Deep (things that shouldn't exist)

Planar bleed in the tunnels (The Below), curses that can't be dispelled, the ghost of a Moretti-chaplained burial that won't stay buried, the thing that lives under the Midtown post office. These are **campaign secrets** — the rules aren't written down.

**Public awareness of Tier 3:** zero. Tiers 1 and 2 provide a plausible world where magic is a regulated utility or a criminal tool — nobody needs to believe in demon incursions to explain the weird smell from that manhole.

## How the city sees it

| Role | Knows | Belief |
|------|-------|--------|
| **Average New Yorker** | Magic is a real thing you can get a license for | "I don't know anyone who does it; those ALB offices are probably a tax scam" |
| **Small business owner** | Warding companies are a line item on the lease | "The fire department checks the wards same as the sprinklers" |
| **Cop** | Unlicensed magic is a felony | "OCU guys are spooky; we call them when a bust goes weird and they show up with a silver briefcase" |
| **Politician** | ALB permits are a patronage pipeline | "Don't touch the magic budget — the families own three of the five commissioners" |
| **Family member** | Magic is a tool and a liability | "My warlock consultant costs more than my lawyer, and he only works at night" |

## Comparison: the three models the table could pick

| Model | Public knowledge | ALB role | Family advantage | Best for |
|-------|-----------------|----------|------------------|----------|
| **Licensed Grey (proposed)** | Magic is known but uncommon | Permits for low-tier use | Unlicensed weapons market | Crime drama with magic as infrastructure |
| **Public Magic** | Everyone knows; magic is a utility like electricity | Mainstream regulatory body | Control of supply lines | Cyberpunk-tinged crime |
| **Masquerade (hidden)** | The public doesn't know; magic is a conspiracy | Front for cover-up | Monopoly on secret power | Paranormal crime thriller |

---

## Recommendation: **Licensed Grey**

It fits what's already in the factions doc (ALB, Commission magic-hits rule, forgery rings, warlock consultants) without needing either full-public or full-conspiracy worldbuilding. The table still gets to play with visible ward companies and underground combat magic in the same session.
