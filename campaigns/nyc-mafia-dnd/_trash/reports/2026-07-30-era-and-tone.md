# Era and tone — draft for table

*2026-07-30 · Agent worldbuilding draft — A-1*

---

## Era: Present day (2026)

1990s was in play early on, but present-day gives us:
- **Smartphones everywhere** — every bodycam, Ring doorbell, and livestream is a threat to the visibility tiers. Magic must be deniable at fifty feet.
- **Digital cash** — the families launder through crypto front companies, signal app burners, and third-party payment rails. The old "bag of cash" drop still happens, but only between crews that don't trust each other.
- **Surveillance state** — NYPD cameras, ALB compliance sensors in licensed wards, OCU wiretap warrants. Operators learn to work in dead zones (subway tunnels, Faraday-caged counting rooms, the Below).
- **Old grudges, new tools** — The Five Families ran extortion and bootlegging in the 20th century. Now they run healing licenses, scroll smuggling, and "consulting" fronts. The methods change; the power structure doesn't.

**Why not 1990s?** A period piece is fun but limits the pressure. The constant threat of a civilian recording a spell, or an ALB drone picking up a necromantic signature, is what makes the setting tense. 1990s loses that. Near-future (2030s) adds more tech but the same problems — present is the sweet spot.

## Tone: Crime drama with a hard supernatural edge

### Ground rules

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| **Magic is infrastructure, not wonder** | Healing is a walk-in clinic. Warding is a security system. Summoning is a corporate consultant. The families treat magic like a utility — valuable, regulated, and worth killing over. |
| **Stakes are criminal, not apocalyptic** | No "save the world" arcs. The party is in the life for money, loyalty, survival, or all three. The biggest threats are a rival crew, a RICO indictment, or a don's debt. |
| **Supernatural exists, but it's concrete** | Ghosts are real but rare and bound to specific locations. Demons exist but they're expensive and leave evidence. The Below is a physical place you can walk into — and might not walk out of. |
| **Dark humor is permitted** | A mage who casts firebolt and then files an insurance claim. A goblin accountant who audits the Valenti books by candlelight. The absurdity of organized crime in a world with real magic — play it straight, but let the irony land. |
| **No real-world gang glorification** | Fiction first. The families are fictional constructs; their violence is a consequence of the setting, not its selling point. |

### What this means for Session 1

- The party gets hired for a job that **seems straightforward** (recover a cursed ledger from a rival crew's counting room).
- Complications come from **normal criminal factors** (police patrol, rival crew muscle, a witness) **plus magical ones** (wards that need disabling, a scroll that misfires, a ghost who saw too much).
- The tone should feel like **The Wire meets The Dresden Files** — not superhero fantasy, not pure cop show. The city is the antagonist.
