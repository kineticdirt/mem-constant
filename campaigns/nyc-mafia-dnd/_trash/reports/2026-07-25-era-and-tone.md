# A-1 — Era lock & tone note

**Date:** 2026-07-25
**Campaign:** NYC Mafia × D&D
**Item:** A-1 — Lock the era + one-page tone note

---

## Era lock: 1998–2005 — "Semi-Modern"

**Definitive call: 1999.** This is the tech-and-culture snapshot for the campaign's present day.

Why 1999:

- **Dial-up / early broadband.** The internet exists but is wild, slow, and unregulated — perfect for the Arcane Licensing Board to run half the ISPs and for families to fight over server-farm turf.
- **Flip phones and pagers.** Communication is traceable enough that the NYPD OCU can wiretap, but ephemeral enough that a burner flip + payphone is a viable countermeasure. No smartphones means no GPS tracking on everyone, no social media trails, no camera in every pocket.
- **CRT monitors next to crystal balls.** The anachronistic texture — cursed cargo manifests printed on dot-matrix, a Valenti fixer running a divination terminal off a 56k modem — is the campaign's visual signature. Magic and mundane tech sit side by side, neither fully replacing the other.
- **Pre-9/11 New York.** The city's criminal justice environment is different: RICO is active, Giuliani-era policing is in full swing, but the World Trade Center still stands. The skyline itself is a plot element (one that GMs can use or ignore). The NYPD is not yet militarized to post-9/11 levels — the OCU still has a modest budget and the families have institutional memory of who to bribe.
- **Cultural texture:** Y2K anxiety as low-grade ambient paranoia. Cassette tapes, arcades, CD-Rs, payphones, graffiti on every subway car. Street fashion: leather jackets, cargo pants, hoop earrings, FUBU, Tommy Hilfiger — all easily costumed at a table. The "millennium bug" is also a perfect in-world conspiracy hook (did the ALB Y2K patch actually fix the mana-grid, or did it install a backdoor for Ashford?).

**What this era lock does NOT mean:**
- The city has fantasy races with their own tech timelines (dwarven alchemical steel, elven rune-ISPs) — those are **out of sync** with the human 1999 baseline, which makes the world feel layered, not uniform.
- Magictech has its own industrial logic (batch production, recharge cycles, feedstock logistics) — it doesn't obey 1999's tech constraints, but it coexists with them.
- Specific years (1998, 2001, 2003) can be referenced for events; the GM can advance the clock by a year or two without breaking the tech snapshot. The lock is on the **tech/culture era**, not a freeze-frame date.

## Tone note: "Brooklyn Gothic"

### Core vibe

The Sopranos meets The Wire, shot through the lens of an urban fantasy novel that never explains itself. The magic is not wondrous — it is infrastructure, leverage, pollution. The city is the real character; the families are just the most organized parasites on its body.

### The three tonal pillars

#### 1. Grounded crime procedural — not a heist fantasy

Every job is a logistics problem first. Moving cursed cargo needs a route, a driver, a mage in the cab, a bribe for the bridge toll, a backup route if the OCU checkpoint shifts. The tension comes from planning, not from spell slots. A session about stealing a manifest from a Kowalski warehouse should feel more like *Heat* than like *Harry Potter*.

**Magical infrastructure replaces sci-fi gadgets:**
- A divination sweep replaces a surveillance van.
- A binding circle replaces handcuffs.
- A warlock pact replaces a lawyer retainer.
- A teleportation sigil replaces a getaway car that's hard to start.

But none of these are easy. They all have costs, cooldowns, and consequences.

#### 2. The city as character — NYC, not generic

The campaign is rooted in real New York geography, not a fantasy city with a different name. Players should be able to say "we meet at the Vesuvio Bakery in Carroll Gardens" and that actually matters — Valenti runs a card game in the back, the halfling baker is a numbers runner, the cops know the bakery's wards are paid up.

**Borough personality:**
- **Brooklyn:** Valenti and Chen-Okafor turf. Docks, brownstones, bodegas, the Gowanus Canal (which is definitely cursed). Ground-level crime, street crews, union halls.
- **Manhattan:** Ashford and Moretti territory. Midtown towers (finance warlocks), Little Italy (Moretti clergy), SoHo lofts (elf-run galleries that are front for illegal divination server farms).
- **Queens:** Chen-Okafor stronghold. Flushing, Astoria, Jackson Heights — ethnically layered, racially diverse, the magictech black market runs through basement workshops and halfling-run diners.
- **Staten Island:** Kowalski. Bridge tolls, waste management, necromantic recycling, triton deals in the harbor. The forgotten borough that the other four view as "out there."
- **Bronx:** Dwarven construction unions, Woodlawn ale halls, the Bronx Zoo's secret magical containment wing. Contested by Valenti and Kowalski.

#### 3. Low fantasy in high density — magic is scarce, expensive, dangerous

The world is not Hogwarts. Most people never cast a spell in their lives. A cantrip is a party trick; a 3rd-level spell is a serious event that leaves a mana signature the OCU can track for 48 hours.

**Scarcity shapes every encounter:**
- A single *fireball* in a street fight gets the OCU on scene in 15 minutes. The families have rules about flashy magic for this reason.
- Healing potions are not infinite. A hospital-grade *cure wounds* costs more than a week's wages for a normal New Yorker.
- Magic items are individually crafted, not loot-dropped. A +1 dagger is a family heirloom or a commissioned job from a dwarven smith who takes three months to deliver.
- Warlock pacts are a Faustian employment contract, not a character-build choice. The Ashford "consultants" who arrange them collect on debts that outlive the signer.

### What scenes feel like

| Scene type | Tone target | Anti-pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Negotiation with a capo | A tense backroom deal, sweating, coffee getting cold. | Anime-style dramatic confrontation. |
| Car chase with magical interference | The driver is a half-orc who knows the streets; the mage in the backseat is trying to keep the engine from boiling over. | Fast & Furious physics. |
| OCU raid on a Valenti stash house | Beats kick the door down, OCU mage in a silver vest nullifies the wards, one guy rats. | SWAT-team spectacle. |
| Funeral / ghost-rite | Moretti chaplain in a worn cassock, incense, East River-scented air, the dead man's shade says one cryptic thing and fades. | Overwrought gothic horror. |
| Kowalski waste disposal | A barge on the East River at 3 AM, a triton handler with a lantern, cursed crates sliding into the black water. | Gross-out gore. |

### What this tone is NOT

- **Not superheroes.** PCs are underworld professionals, not chosen ones. Their power grows through connections, not levels.
- **Not a magic school.** No one is learning spells from a textbook. You learn from your crew's fixer, or from a warlock you signed a pact with, or you steal a scroll and hope it works.
- **Not a morality play.** The families are criminals. The game doesn't need to condemn or celebrate — it shows the work, the cost, and the code.
- **Not gonzo.** No beholder crime bosses, no planar casino heists, no floating castles (at least not in session 1). The weird stays weird by being rare.

### One-sentence pitch

> The Five Families run New York's magical black market in 1999 — every deal is a logistics problem, every spell leaves a trail, and the city doesn't care who you killed, only whether you made the trains run on time.
