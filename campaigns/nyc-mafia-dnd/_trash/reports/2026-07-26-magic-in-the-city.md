# A-2 — Magic Visibility Rules (Public / Hidden / Licensed)

**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Campaign:** NYC Mafia × D&D (1999, Brooklyn Gothic)
**Status:** DRAFT — table canon pending

---

## The core question

> **Is magic public knowledge or mostly hidden?** (premise.md open question #2)

Answer: **Both.** The right model is a **three-tier visibility system** — public, licensed, and hidden — because a single on/off switch breaks both the premise (ALB, grey market, OCU) and the era (1999's information landscape is porous but slow).

---

## Tier 1 — Public magic (everyone knows)

Some magic is **legally acknowledged** and woven into civic life. Ordinary New Yorkers see it, use it, and don't blink.

| What's public | Example |
|--------------|---------|
| **Healing clinics** | Licensed clerics with storefronts. Health Department posted rates. "Cure Wounds, $50 — cash or Medicaid." |
| **Rune-ISPs** | Elven-rune broadband in select zones. Advertised on billboards. "Faster than dial-up. Ask about our Y2K-compatible plan." |
| **Municipal wards** | Subway tunnels have anti-rat glyphs. The MTA contracts wizards. Everyone grumbles about maintenance. |
| **Arcane Licensing Board** | City agency. Listed in the blue pages. You can apply for a cantrip permit. The wait is six weeks. |
| **News coverage** | The *Daily News* runs a "Magic Beat" column. NY1 covers ALB hearings. |

**Public rule:** If there's a permit, a storefront, or a city contract for it, it's public. The public has calibrated — nobody stares at a glowing streetlamp.

---

## Tier 2 — Licensed magic (permit required, traceable)

Magic that is **legal but controlled** — requires ALB registration, a specific permit tier, and leaves a paper trail. This is where 90% of family-associated magic operates.

| Licensed activity | Permit class | Consequences |
|------------------|-------------|-------------|
| **Arcane security** — warded doors, alarm glyphs | Class-B | Yearly inspection. ALB knows your address. |
| **Divination** — finding lost items, reading omens | Class-C | Limited to 3 readings/month without special waiver |
| **Transmutation** — alchemical materials | Class-A (Manufacturing) | EPA paperwork. Random ALB audits. |
| **Clerical services** — blessings, curses (non-lethal) | Class-D | Church-affiliation required; solo practitioners extra paperwork |

**Licensed rule:** You can do it legally if you're on the books. The family owns ALB clerks. Permits cost bribes, not coin. But a permit means **trackable** — when OCU finds a body with Class-C divination residue, they know which registered diviners to shake down.

**Licensed ≠ safe.** The Commission's rule: "No magic hits on civilians without a vote" — but a licensed charm-person on a city inspector is a Tuesday, not a capital offense.

---

## Tier 3 — Hidden magic (illegal, untraceable, the real plot)

Magic that is **off the books** — no permit, no record, no deniability if caught. This is where the campaign lives.

| Hidden activity | Who does it | Risk |
|----------------|-------------|------|
| **Necromancy** (any) | Kowalski (allegedly), lone operators | Mandatory life sentence. OCU drops everything. |
| **Summoning / planar contact** | Warlocks, cults, desperate crews | If it gets out, the Church sends people. Not cops. **People.** |
| **Mind magic on civilians** (charm, dominate, modify memory) | Anyone who wants to stay free | Felony. ALB + OCU joint task force. |
| **Unlicensed healing** | Back-alley clerics | Misdemeanor. Often tolerated. But if the patient dies? Murder one. |
| **Spellcasting to dispose of evidence** | Anyone in a pinch | Hard to prove. If proven, adds conspiracy to the charge list. |
| **Pact-forged items** (no serial number) | The Ledger, freelance artificers | Contraband. Confiscation + heavy fine. |

**Hidden rule:** The magic itself isn't illegal — the *lack of a license* is. A fireball cast by a registered Class-A Evoker in a licensed range? Fine. The same fireball in a Brooklyn warehouse by someone without a permit? Aggravated assault + unlicensed arcane activity + probable RICO predicate.

## What this means at the table

| Situation | Visibility tier | Rule of thumb |
|-----------|----------------|--------------|
| Buy a healing potion at Duane Reade | Public | No notice. Shelf item. |
| Hire a diviner to find the stolen car | Licensed | Takes a call. ALB knows. Inspector on payroll. |
| Ask a warlock to talk to a ghost | Hidden | Phone booth. Dead drop. No names. |
| Cast *light* to read a menu in a dark restaurant | Public | Nobody cares. |
| Cast *fireball* in a nightclub | Hidden → **immediate OCU response** | In 15 minutes. They have *locate spell*. |
| Sell an unlicensed scroll of *revivify* | Hidden | Grey market. Valenti docks. Meet at the bakery. |

## Detection: who finds out

| Source of visibility | How it works |
|---------------------|-------------|
| **ALB permit database** | Computerized in 1999 (early Access database). Slow, corrupt, but queryable. |
| **OCU arcane trace unit** | *Detect magic* sweep after known incidents. Range: 60 ft per caster level. |
| **Spell residue** | Every spell leaves a magical signature for 24 hours (diminishes). High-level spells linger for days. |
| **Witnesses** | New Yorkers are jaded. They ignore fireworks. But a screaming portal in Times Square gets reported. |
| **Scry networks** | The families watch each other. Ashford has a scrying pool in Midtown. Nobody admits this. |

## Summary — the ladder

```
PUBLIC (permit, storefront, city contract)
  ↕ upgrade with bribe/paperwork
LICENSED (permit required, ALB tracked)
  ↕ crossing this line is the campaign's default tension
HIDDEN (no permit, OCU target, story material)
```

The magic visibility isn't about hiding D&D from muggles. It's about **licensing, graft, and plausible deniability** — a system the families exploit, the ALB pretends to control, and the PCs navigate every job.
