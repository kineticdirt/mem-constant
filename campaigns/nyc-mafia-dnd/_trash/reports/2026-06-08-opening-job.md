## The Job: "The Red Hook Manifest"

### Elevator Pitch

The crew is hired to retrieve a **waterlogged shipping container** from a submerged barge at the Red Hook docks — a cargo manifest that contains **enchanted goods bound in a Moretti burial rite**, registered under a fraudulent ALB permit that belongs to no family at all. Three factions want it. The crew can only deliver it to one.

### The Hook

The contact is **Lucia Valenti** ("The Appraiser") — see A-3. A Valenti dockworker cracked open the wrong container red-flagged in the family's records. Inside: twelve hand-carved reliquaries, each containing a bone fragment bound with Moretti funeral-magic sigils. The manifest is Moretti property, the permit was issued through an ALB clerk on Ashford payroll, and the barge sank in a suspicious accident three weeks ago that the Kowalks are whispering is "the river collecting."

Lucia wants the reliquaries recovered and the manifest before the other families realize the barge is salvageable. She's offering **fair pay plus a favor from the Appraiser** — valuable currency for a crew that will need her eye down the line.

### Locations

1. **Red Hook Container Terminal (night)** — The job starts here. Graffiti-tagged fences, motion-sensing floodlights (mundane, can be bypassed), and at least one bored security guard who's Valentis. Water access is via the Buttermilk Channel access road. The barge sits under a crane that hasn't moved in two weeks.
2. **The Sunken Barge — "St. Brigit"** — A decommissioned cargo lighter, half-submerged in 20 feet of water. Interior is a zero-visibility nightmare. The reliquaries are in the captain's cabin. The burial magic on them is **active but dormant** — disturbing them without the proper counter-rite triggers a ghost-binding tie that anchors one random crew member until it's resolved.
3. **The Van (exfil route)** — Pre-positioned on Otsego Street. Any route off Red Hook passes through territory that's Valenti-adjacent but not Valenti-controlled — which means other eyes.

### DM Notes

- **Fail forward:** If the crew triggers every complication, this is still one session. The goal isn't to defeat all five — it's to get the cargo out with *something going wrong* and *someone noticing*. The crew's first session should end with a successful retrieval, one major complication in play, and a clear next job offered from an unexpected direction.
- **Session flow inspiration:**
  1. **Cold open** — Lucia hires the crew (15 min roleplay; let the crew negotiate; Lucia respects competence but expects results).
  2. **The dock** — Skill challenge: bypass OCU, reach the barge, deal with the water entry. (20–30 min.)
  3. **The barge** — Underwater salvage + the supernatural bite. One complication triggers inside. (20–30 min.)
  4. **Exfil + complication spike** — The escape route encounters the remaining complication(s). The crew makes a choice about delivery. (15–20 min.)
  5. **Denouement** — Lucia pays. Someone else makes contact. The crew has a target on their back they didn't ask for. (10 min.)

- **Session tone:** Scorsese dockwork for the first half, horror-tinged after the water, noir-bureaucratic on the exfil. If a player says something cool during a negotiation, Lucia files it away — she's the Appraiser, and she assesses *people* too.

### Three In-Opening NPCs

1. **Lucia Valenti** — Hiring contact. Professional, assesses the crew in the first meeting as much as they assess the job. Offers fair terms. Folds immediately if the crew tries to negotiate above her ceiling — she respects the hustle but has a budget.
2. **Sgt. Adrienne Voss (OCU)** — The patrol cop. Young, true-believer, the kind of cop who joined Occult Crimes because she actually saw something once and nobody believed her. She's not corrupt. She's dangerous in the most mundane way: she *files reports*.
3. **"The Thing in the Water"** — Name pending. A Kowalski river-dead, manifesting as a humanoid shape made of harbor silt and broken glass. It doesn't speak but communicates through water displacement — pulling, pushing, circling. Curious about the reliquaries (they're connected to the dead, and so is it).
