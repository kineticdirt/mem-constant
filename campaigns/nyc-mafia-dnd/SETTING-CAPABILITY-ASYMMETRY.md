# Setting Capability Asymmetry — NYC Mafia × D&D

*GM lock · **2026-08-02**. Primary philosophy SoT for **what the city is good at and bad at**. Read after `LOCKS.md` and alongside `SETTING-SPELL-TECH-TREE.md`.*

**Thesis in one line:** New York can **raise your uncle from the dead** and **lose a union to a self-willed brass giant**, but nobody in the Commission can **reconcile last week's numbers** without three accountants, a priest, and a prayer.

---

## 1. Why this file exists

Agents kept inventing gadgets by smearing one era across everything — "1958" culture glued to "late-1800s" brass glued to "existential automata" as slogans. That produces **flat** worldbuilding: a city that feels like cosplay.

The real lock is **capability asymmetry**: domains advance at different speeds because **guild magic** solves **embodied problems** (bodies, oaths, labor, death) far better than **abstract problems** (stable state, symbolic logic, prediction at scale). Culture can *feel* mid-century jazz-vice; machinery can *look* brass-and-steam; automata can *threaten* like a near-future labor panic — **without** pinning the calendar to a single year.

---

## 2. Uneven temporal stack (no single year)

**VOID:** "The year is 1958." **LOCK:** an **uneven stack** — each domain wears a different decade (or century) on its sleeve.

| Domain | Feels like | What players notice |
|--------|------------|---------------------|
| **Nightlife / voice / dress** | **Mid-century NYC/Jersey** (~1950s–60s) | Bebop sets, sharkskin, commuter trains from Hoboken, cold-war newspaper dread, Mulberry Street wakes |
| **Street hardware** | **Late 1800s industrial** | Steam El, **brass subway** (steam-assist, ward-lit), **ley-rail** / **ward-rail** (ritual conductors, capsule switch engines), brass speaking-tubes, warded roll-up doors, coal smoke, **slow motorized cabs**, **capsule** revolvers not powder guns |
| **Skyline / sprawl** | **Pre-skyscraper / early commuter** | **No steel towers**; 4–8 story brick, guild spires; **suburban sprawl** + **arterials** (Grand Concourse, Queens Blvd); **no interstates** |
| **Civic & office routine** | **Edwardian / pre-war clerical** | Carbon copies, ledger books, pneumatic mail — **no reliable thinking machines** |
| **Medicine & death rites** | **Ahead of street tech** | Brain bleeds closed in a salon; **raise dead** is scandalous but **real**; assassination politics assume resurrection exists |
| **Automata & bound labor** | **Modern-to-future threat** | Self-directed cores, plague of knockers, factories that run after everyone left — **existential**, not quaint clockwork |
| **Computation & prediction** | **Behind everyone** | Numbers rackets cheat with **divination**, not spreadsheets; guild "calculating engines" drift, lie, or need a bound ghost who hates math; **rail timetables** are **human/clerk chaos** — feast-day patron conflicts, ley drift, divination boards |

**Play the seams.** A capo wears a 1956 hat, rides a 1890 lift, gets his aneurysm fixed like it's 1975, and signs a contract his lawyer **cannot** model on any engine that isn't a tired clerk with a fountain pen.

## 3. The three lanes (agent invention gate)

Before adding any device, service, or institution, classify it:

```
                    ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
                    │  Is this primarily about…           │
                    └─────────────────────────────────────┘
                                      │
          ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
          ▼                           ▼                           ▼
   MEDICINE / LIFE              AUTOMATION /                  COMPUTATION /
   DEATH MAGIC                  SENTIENCE                      PREDICTION
   (STRONG)                     (STRONG THREAT)                (WEAK)
```

| Lane | Strong means | Weak means | Default failure mode |
|------|--------------|------------|----------------------|
| **Medicine / life / death** | Healing closes surgical gaps; potions cover niches; kill spells are instant; resurrection is politics not myth | — | Cost, scandal, who gets revived, aura bootleggers detect |
| **Automation / sentience** | Constructs labor, siege, replicate; **biotech is magic** (sigils, bindings, living patterns) — **not** CRISPR labs | They are **not** supercomputers | Runaway purpose, union extinction, FSA shoot-to-scrap |
| **Computation / prediction** | — | Complex math, actuarial truth, large ledgers, "AI" style reasoning | Wrong totals, haunted abaci, divination burnout, bribed clerk still beats engine |

**Agent rule:** If your invention is **computation**, default to **expensive, bad, or socially avoided** — unless GM explicitly locks an exception.

## 4. Why medicine outruns computation (deductive)

Guild magic **knows what a body is**. A corpus is a **patronized object**: blood, breath, bone, soul-thread — things oaths and rituals have named for centuries. **Cure wounds** does not simulate biochemistry; it **negotiates** with a body the culture already treats as a valid target of craft. **Raise dead** is expensive because **death is political**, not because resurrection is theoretically impossible.

Computation asks for something guild epistemology **never industrialized**:

1. **Stable abstract state** — registers that hold a number tomorrow the same as today, without a spirit's mood or a saint's feast day changing the result.
2. **Symbolic manipulation at scale** — algorithms as **repeatable** procedures divorced from the **operator's** body and patronage network.
3. **Truth without witness** — a conclusion nobody swore to, nobody sang, nobody bound with iron and ink.

Healing acts on **this patient, now** — embodied, witnessed, billable. A ledger engine acts on **symbols** — disembodied, jealous, easy to haunt. Temples monopolize **truth rooms** (*zone of truth*) for **people**, not for **mathematics**. Families trust **blood-signed contracts** and **tube recordings**, not **probability engines**, because the engines **drift**.

**Ponytail ceiling:** You can buy a **good** healing at a bad salon. You cannot buy a **good** citywide actuarial model at any price — only **good enough to cheat** at cards, and even then the Chen-Okafor room still employs a human who **feels** when the divination slips. Pemberton publishes a **timetable**; the **ritual conductor** and the **ley stakes** publish **reality**.

## 4b. Rail as magitech* lane (strong craft, weak schedules)

**Transport magitech\*** is **competent** at moving mass — **animate objects** drive rods, **bound steam spirits** boil water, **ward-rail** keeps trespassers honest. That is **guild-strong** (`SETTING-GILDED-INDUSTRY.md`).

**Scheduling** is **computation-weak**: feast-day patrons, crooked ley surveys, divination gambling on platform boards, clerks who **hate** arithmetic. A train can **run** and still be **unfindable** on paper.

| Strong (magitech\*) | Weak (computation) |
|---------------------|-------------------|
| Haul freight tonnage; ward-rail alarms; capsule switch engines | On-time performance; actuarial commuter bonds; unified national schedule |
| Ritual conductor **recall** scroll stops disaster | Predicting **which** platform without a human who knows someone |
| Foundry **loader** outlifts a teamster | Routing manifest without forgery or **ghost schedule** |

**Agent rule:** Inventions that **move weight** via spell-craft are allowed to **work**. Inventions that **coordinate** thousands of cars without human mess are **not**.

## 5. Why automata threaten without being supercomputers

**Sentience, force, and labor ≠ calculation.**

A Jersey foundry can bind an **elemental** into a **core** that **wants** to keep the line running. That core can:

- Lift more than a teamster;
- Work through the night without gin;
- **Rewrite its purpose** when the binding circle cracks;
- Build **siege engines** in an abandoned warehouse without understanding **interest rates**.

It does **not** need to outthink a mathematician. It needs to **outlast** a union. The panic is **existential** in the way **automation** panics are in our world — not in the way **superintelligence** panics are.

**Magic-biotech** in this setting is **not** fluorescent labs. It is:

- **Sigils** cut into brass myocardium;
- **Bindings** that teach silk to remember a face;
- **Living patterns** grown in consecrated vats — **artificial life** as **ritual artifact**, not genome editor.

The horror is **category violation**: a thing that **lives** and **obeys economics** without being **human** or **properly dead**. OCU does not raid a lab coat; FSA raids a **core** that **breathes**.

## 6. Implications (families, law, jazz, Barry tone)

### Families

- **Moretti / parish lane:** healing salons, resurrection politics, "who stays dead" as leverage.
- **Chen-Okafor:** numbers rackets use **divination** and **human** runners — not actuarial engines. Their edge is **cheating perception**, not **closing the books**.
- **Kowalski:** disposal of **cores** and **failed biotech bindings** — hotter than bodies.
- **Ashford:** contracts written for **people who cannot trust calculators** — literalism, oath loopholes, Midtown **polite denial**.
- **Valenti:** dock wars over **construct loaders** — labor, not silicon.

### Law

- **FAB** audits **licenses and stamps** — paper truth, not algorithmic compliance.
- **FSA** shoots **runaway automata** — the state fears **force**, not **ChatGPT**.
- Courts admit **ward residue** and **tube recordings**; they do **not** admit "the engine said so" without a **sworn operator** who will perjure themselves.

### Jazz / vice

- Horns as **foci** — emotion magic, message cantrips, **bootleg healing** in the same back room.
- Vice is **high-touch**: you cannot automate **cool** or **shame** reliably. A clockwork bartender is **threatening**; a clockwork **accountant** is **broken**.

### Barry tone

People use **terrifying magic casually** and **cannot run a spreadsheet**:

> *"We fixed his head." "Good." "It cost eight hundred." "Fine." "Also the clerk says we're down four grand but the numbers don't match." "Fire the clerk." "We fired the clerk. The numbers still don't match." "…Get the priest?"*

Violence is dry; **bureaucracy is absurd**; **healing is a line item**; **math is a spiritual problem**. Capsule reloads are **embarrassing** — wrong patron whisper, cap under the radiator, fizzle at the worst moment (`SETTING-ARMS.md` §6).

## 7. Agent invention rules (checklist)

1. **Classify the lane** — medicine/life, automation/sentience, or computation/prediction?
2. **If computation** — make it **weak, expensive, haunted, or human-backed**. Show **failure**, not Silicon Alley.
3. **If medicine** — you may be **competent and costly**. Show **politics**, scars, aura, who pays.
4. **If automata** — ask **operator-required vs self-directed**. Only the second is **headline fear**. No lab-coat biotech; use **sigils, bindings, cores**.
5. **Do not re-lock a calendar year** — use **uneven stack** language ("mid-century club," "brass-tube office," not "1958").
6. **Cross-check** `SETTING-SPELL-TECH-TREE.md` §2 procedure — still run Goal → Need → Spell → Cost → Failure.

## 8. Scene seeds (tone, not inventory)

**Healing stronger than street tech:** A sax player takes a bottle to the skull in a stairwell fight. The back-room bandage-girl **stops the bleed** before the ambulance horse is saddled. The player owes **money** and **a favor**; the FSA never gets called because **everyone** knows salons exist. The horror is **ordinary**.

**Automata without superintelligence:** Red Hook pier, 4 a.m. A **loader** keeps stacking crates after the foreman went home. It is not **plotting**; it is **fulfilling** a contract nobody revoked. The union throws **bricks**, not **firewall** arguments.

**Computation failure:** Chen-Okafor's back room — three runners, one **diviner** with nosebleed, a brass **tally engine** that keeps landing on **666**. The **human** bookie wins. The engine gets **blessed and thrown in the river**.
