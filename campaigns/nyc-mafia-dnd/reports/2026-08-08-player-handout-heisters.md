## The Setup

New York. Culture reads 1950s–60s — jazz clubs, social clubs, numbers runners, parish politics. Street tech is late-1800s guild brass: speaking-tubes instead of phones, motor-carriages instead of traffic, warded safes instead of alarm companies. Magic is real, licensed, taxed, or bootleg. The Five Families run the grey market. The OCU arrests people for it.

**You are a heist crew.** Not made. Not family. Independents with a reputation for doors that open.

---

## The Leash (what your crew knows)

Your last job paid rent. It also turned out to be a **Valenti** counting room, which you learned when you saw the seal on the ledger you were standing over. Two weeks later a woman with a cargo hook and a missing finger joint handed you a typed note:

> *Blue Note. West 52nd. Cellar safe. Artie Shaw's book. Return it to me. We're square.*

You have the address, the shape of the building from a walk-by, and about **48 hours**. You are not in a position to say no.

## Crew Roles

Six seats. Double up or leave seats empty — the plan adapts. Class is your business; the seat is your job.

| Seat | You are the one who… | 5e-friendly fits |
|------|----------------------|------------------|
| **Mastermind** | Builds the timeline. Watches the whole board from a booth across the street. | Rogue (Mastermind), Bard, Wizard, Warlock |
| **Face** | Walks in the front door like money. Talks the crew out of what talking can fix. | Bard, Rogue, Sorcerer, Paladin (yes, really — nobody expects the devout) |
| **Muscle** | Holds doors. Ends conversations. Carries what doesn't want to be carried. | Fighter, Barbarian, Monk, Ranger |
| **Second-story** | Goes up. Fire escapes, rooflines, service stairs. Never uses the front. | Rogue, Monk, Ranger, anybody with a climb plan |
| **Wires / Ward-breaker** | Silver chalk, rowan wedges, gloved hands. Opens what was sealed by people better paid than you. | Rogue (Arcane Trickster), Wizard, Artificer-flavored anybody |
| **Wheel** | Keeps the motor-carriage warm and the route warmer. Knows which bridge is yours. | Anybody. Driving is a calling, not a class feature. |

**Candice Graves is slotted Muscle** (sheet: `characters/candice-graves.md`). Everything else is open.

**Ancestries:** the city runs on diasporas — German elves, French half-elves, genasi and tieflings, Spanish humans and dragonborn, Welsh firbolgs, Scottish goliaths, Jewish goblin-kin (Erklings/Goddarts), Irish and Italian blocks full of half-orcs and plain humans. Homebrew and reskins are welcome — run it past the GM, and it becomes true. (`SETTING-PEOPLES-RACES.md` for the full map.)

## Arms (no gunpowder — anywhere, for anyone)

Guns here are **capsule frames**: brass cylinders, wax-sealed, stamped or bootleg. A revolver frame holds six.

| You'll actually carry | What it does | Street price |
|-----------------------|--------------|--------------|
| **Guild pulse** (licensed) | 2d10 force shot, clean | $3–5 / cap |
| **Dock sling** (licensed) | 2d6 slug, dumb and reliable | $4–8 / cap |
| **Blue kiss** (bootleg pulse) | 2d8 force, louder, cheaper | $2–4 / cap |
| **Silence choke** | 15-ft silence bubble on impact — the professional's favorite | $30–50 |
| **Fog burst** | 20-ft of nobody-sees-anything | $15–25 |

**Two things every heister knows:** reloading is a four-step ritual that makes you look stupid in a fight — carry a second loaded frame or a pair of knucks. And spellcasting near unlicensed constructs (you will meet at least one) can make them decide you're a threat.

**Healing is good here.** Salons fix bullet holes same-night for cash. What the families sell that you can't buy at a salon: silence, discretion, and capsules that make wounds *stay*.

## The City in Six Lines

1. **Valenti** — Brooklyn docks. Smuggling, construct cores. *You owe them.*
2. **Moretti** — Little Italy. Protection, chaplains, the funeral business.
3. **Chen-Okafor** — Queens. Numbers, lending, divination.
4. **Kowalski** — Staten Island + Jersey. Disposal. You don't want disposal.
5. **Ashford** — Midtown. Laundry, politics, consultants in good suits.
6. **The OCU** — magic-aware detectives with ward-sniffers. Overt spellcasting, public wards, and family violence all raise **heat**. Heat buys stakeouts.

Neutral ground exists: the Golden Dragon Mahjong Club in Flushing answers questions for favors. Sea Wolf Tattoo in Red Hook does ward-glyph ink and hears everything on the docks.

## What You Know Going Into Session 1

- The Blue Note is a licensed jazz club on West 52nd between 6th and 7th. Green awning, two doors, sets at 9 / 11 / 1.
- The cellar holds a safe. The safe holds the book. The safe is **warded** — bring chalk, a rowan wedge, and gloves.
- The front-of-house doorman is a half-orc in a tux who goes by Big Mike. He works a rotation.
- Something in the cellar washes glasses and isn't alive.
- You have 48 hours, one forged valet tag, and each other.

## Table Conventions

- **Quoted** = your character says it. **Unquoted** = what your character does. **(Parens)** = table talk to the GM.
- Tone is *Barry*: dry, a little sad, funny until it isn't. The band keeps playing no matter what happens in the cellar.
- The GM reveals the twist in play. You will know when.

*Jazz stays on. The job does not.*
