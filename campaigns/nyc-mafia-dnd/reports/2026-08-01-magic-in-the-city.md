# Magic in the City — Visibility, Licensing, and Rackets

*Status: Canonical · Locked GM Decision · Supersedes all prior magic in the city reports*

---

## 1. The Ward-Tiered System (B2)

Magic in the metropolitan area is not hidden, but its visibility is strictly regulated by neighborhood wealth and social class. The city is divided into three distinct visibility tiers.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                           VISIBILITY TIERS                            |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Tier 1: OPEN (Red Hook, Tenements, Slums)                            |
|  - Glowing hands, street-corner mending, raw elemental steam.         |
|  - Healing lines outside parish chapels, open ward-stamps on doors.   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Tier 2: LICENSED (Midtown Clubs, Union Halls, Parish Chapels)        |
|  - Magic packaged as respectable craft or entertainment.              |
|  - Licensed restorative salons, warded safes, neon-lit illusion stages. |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Tier 3: POLITE DENIAL (Ashford Corridor, Madison Ave, Upscale Lobbies)|
|  - Magic is completely integrated or hidden behind service stairs.    |
|  - Speaking-tubes are brass pneumatic mail; no glowing hands.         |
|  - Midtown pretends magic does not exist; it is just "fine craft."    |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Passing and Etiquette
In polite wards like Midtown or the Ashford Corridor, showing raw magical energy is a social crime. 
- A wizard-for-hire must carry their focus in a leather briefcase.
- Glowing hands or visible runes must be hidden under gloves or silk scarves.
- Magical servants (like unseen servants) are restricted to service stairs and freight lifts. If a glowing silhouette enters a Madison Avenue lobby, the doorman will call the Federal Seizure Agency.

## 2. Underworld Magic Rackets

The Five Families control the flow of illegal, unlicensed, and bootleg magic. What they sell is **licensed craft vs. bootleg ritual**.

| Service | Licensed Face (The Straight Game) | Bootleg Underworld Racket (The Real Money) |
|---------|-----------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| **Healing** | **Parish Band:** A licensed temple clinic. Long queues, high taxes, and a federal audit trail. Price: $10 per cure. | **Back-Room Salon:** Unlicensed mages set bones, close bleeds, and heal bullet wounds in basement jazz clubs. Price: $25–$800 depending on severity (**medicine is strong** — see asymmetry SoT). |
| **Security** | **Guild Door Ward:** A registered ward stamped by the Locksmiths' Guild. Price: $100/year. | **Stolen Keys:** Forged guild stamps or ward-breaking chalks sold to burglars. Price: $50 per job. |
| **Luck** | **Temple Fundraiser:** Licensed bingo and charity dice games taxed by the city. | **Chen-Okafor Probability Room:** Back-room mahjong and numbers games manipulated by subtle divination. |
| **Disposal** | **Kowalski Sanitation:** Licensed waste hauling and scrap processing. | **Deep Disposal:** Vanishing bodies, hot construct cores, and cursed artifacts in Staten Island landfills. |
| **Contracts** | **Ashford Consulting:** Legally binding corporate contracts filed in Delaware. | **Infernal Lease:** Pacts signed in blood that lease soul-equity or future favors to Midtown warlocks. |
| **Arms** | **Iron Guild stamped pulse caps:** Licensed ward-revolvers for registered carriers. | **Specialty capsules:** Healing-deny, silence choke, ward-pierce — family smuggle lanes; OCU traces **residue**, not "smart" aim. |

### Capsule arms (B16)

Guns use **magical capsules**, not powder. Because **healing is strong** and **aim-assist is weak**, the racket is specialty loads and awkward reloads — not bigger magazines.

- **Pulse** leaves blue ward dust; **sling** leaves ballistics.
- **Healing-deny** caps are Class A; salons are the counter-economy.

## 3. The Automata Threat (D2)

Automata are not rare mechanical toys; they are an **existential threat** to the social and economic order of the city — **modern-to-future labor panic**, not supercomputers.

- **The Labor Conflict:** Guild foundries in New Jersey produce self-directed clockwork constructs. These machines do not sleep, do not strike, and do not join unions. The Longshoremen's Union (backed by the Valenti family) will riot if an unlicensed construct loader is spotted on a Red Hook pier.
- **The Rogue Core Panic:** A "true automaton" runs on an autonomous spirit-bound core (sigils and bindings — **not** lab biotech). If the binding circle degrades, the construct rewrites its own purpose. A runaway automaton can turn a factory floor into a slaughterhouse or build a siege engine in an abandoned warehouse — **without** being good at math.
- **The Law:** The Federal Seizure Agency has a standing "shoot-to-scrap" order on any construct operating without an active, registered human operator.

## 4. Operators in the Grey

Player characters operate in the grey zone between the law and the families. They do not carry magical spellbooks; they carry tools of the trade.

- **The Gear:** A ward-breaker's kit includes silver chalk, rowan-wood wedges, and brass wire. A club musician's focus is a brass trumpet or a reed saxophone, used to channel emotion-altering charms during a late-night set.
- **The Evidence:** There are no cellphones to record crimes. OCU detectives build cases using:
  - **Ward Residue:** Scrapings of glowing dust left behind by a teleportation or ward-breaking spell.
  - **Tube Splices:** Wax cylinder recordings taken from tapped brass speaking-tubes.
  - **Bound Ghosts:** Summoning the victim of a mob hit to answer one question in a funeral-home basement.
- **The Legal Heat:** Carrying an unlicensed focus, a bootleg healing band, or speaking-tube splice tools is a felony. A bad night on the street means a one-way trip to the federal audit cells in upstate New York.
