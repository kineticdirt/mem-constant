# Era and Tone — Mid-Century Culture × Late-1800s Spell-Tech

*Status: Canonical · Locked GM Decision · Supersedes all prior era and tone reports*

---

## 1. What Year Is It? (Uneven Stack — No Single Year)

**VOID:** pinning the campaign to an exact year (e.g. "1958"). **LOCK:** an **uneven temporal stack** — domains wear different decades. Full map: `SETTING-CAPABILITY-ASYMMETRY.md` §2.

Fictionalized New York metro — five boroughs, Jersey commuter belt (Newark, Bayonne, Hoboken). Don't ask what year it is at the table. Ask what's broken.

### The Cultural Veneer (Mid-Century Feel)
Bebop and cool jazz own the night. Trumpet, sax, double bass — not background noise, **focus tools** for emotion magic and message cantrips. Rock and roll leaks in at the edges on licensed jukeboxes. Men in sharkskin and fedoras; women in church hats and domestic dresses (operators can take suited male duties if they deliver). Italian longshoremen on Red Hook, Irish union bosses in taverns, Jewish merchants on the Lower East Side, Black jazz in Harlem, Puerto Rican families in East Harlem — tight human neighborhoods with fantasy ancestries **in** them, not standing in for them.

Postwar boom, cold-war paranoia. The *Tribune* (mechanical typewriter, no kidding) whispers about foreign artificers and rogue automata — **labor panics**, not silicon gods.

### Domains Ahead & Behind (Capability Asymmetry)

| Domain | Player-facing feel |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Medicine / death** | **Ahead** — salons fix what hospitals cannot; resurrection is politics |
| **Automata** | **Modern-future threat** — cores, runaway lines, union extinction |
| **Computation** | **Behind** — ledgers, odds, and "engines" are **bad**; clerks and divination win |

See `SETTING-CAPABILITY-ASYMMETRY.md` for philosophy. People use terrifying healing casually and **cannot reconcile the books**.

### The Street Technology (Late-1800s Industrial)
Steam, coal, brass, warded iron. Tenements with steam lifts. Streets lit by continual-flame lamps — Lamplighters' Union has opinions about overtime. No smartphones, TV, or default radio. Long distance = paired brass speaking-tubes and guild switchboards; urgent = expensive *sending* appointments.

Commuters ride the El, **brass-and-brick subways** (steam-assist grades, ward-lit platforms — schedules **glitch** near automata cores), **ley-rail** from Jersey and Long Island (ritual conductors, feast-day stalls — see `SETTING-GILDED-INDUSTRY.md`), or **slow motorized carriages** pretending to be cabs ($1.50–$2.50 midtown ↔ docks; illegal ward-boost if you're in a hurry). Electric streetcars here and there. Rich ride warded steam-carriages. Bands: `SETTING-URBAN-FRINGE.md` §4–5.

**Gilded texture:** Limestone **cliffs** hide tenement radiators that still knock. **Pemberton** and **Harlan** names on hospital wings and foundry smokestacks — robber-baron money under mid-century hats. Patronage politics: a **commuter bond** vote matters as much as a Commission sit-down.

**Urban order:** Cities dominate — ward boards, guild charters, Commission turf. Vampires and werewolves are **real** but live at the **fringe** (sprawl edge, Below, Jersey wilds, night markets). Not voters. Druids get the **communist terrorist** label — some cells blow steam mains; timber and foundry donors **benefit**. `SETTING-URBAN-FRINGE.md`.

**Skyline:** No steel towers. Brick, stone, guild spires, factory stacks — mostly 4–8 stories. Sprawl rings the boroughs; **arterials** (Grand Concourse, Queens Blvd, Jersey Commuter Causeway) connect hubs. **Grand Central** ward-hall is the **ley-rail cathedral** — brass concourse, schedule board that lies near automata cores, **Vandermeer** tube trunk below (`SETTING-GILDED-INDUSTRY.md`). No big interstates — smuggling uses arterials and **ward-rail** night freight.

**Fire:** Steam pump companies plus magic brigades (temple hose, foundry anti-construct foam). Engine 8–12 min; prepaid ward buildings get magic in 5–8.

**Firearms:** No gunpowder. **Capsule arms** — ward-revolvers and sling-rifles, sealed brass loads (mana pulse or kinetic slug). Specialty loads trade like bootleg ritual. Reload is clumsy — key twist, wax seal, patron whisper, tap iron. "Smart" caps drift (weak computation). Salons fix bullet holes; killers compete on **how** you die. No Tommy guns. `SETTING-ARMS.md`.

## 2. Tone: Jazz-Club Noir with a Hard Edge

The tone is a gritty crime drama with a hard supernatural edge. It is about institutional weight, back-room deals, and the cold reality of the streets—not superhero fantasy.

| Tonal Pillar | Playable Reality |
|--------------|------------------|
| **Magic is Infrastructure** | Magic is not flashy or academic. It is a trade. Healing is done in back-room salons; roll-up doors are protected by guild wards; contracts are sealed with minor infernal pacts. |
| **Criminal Stakes** | The game is about paper trails, tube wiretaps, crew debt, and witnesses. A firebolt is not a combat spell; it is an arson tool or a warning before a Commission sit-down. |
| **Vice is Central** | The campaign revolves around jazz clubs, unlicensed salons, bootleg healing elixirs, and numbers rackets. Vice is the economic engine of the underworld. |
| **No Glorification** | The mobsters are not heroes. They are desperate, greedy, or bound by blood and oaths. The law is corrupt, and the streets are cold. |

## 3. Humor / Tonality (Barry Reference)

**GM compass:** HBO's *Barry* — not in-world, not a character, not a fourth-wall joke. Voice filter for how crime sits against ordinary life: dry, awkward, darkly funny until it suddenly isn't.

| Do | Avoid |
|----|-------|
| Understatement after violence ("He put the gun away and asked if anyone wanted coffee.") | Quippy one-liners, wink-at-camera, Marvel banter |
| People failing normal social scripts — capos who can't apologize, musicians who treat a hit like a bad gig | Cartoon mafia accents played for laughs every scene |
| Ordinary errands colliding with horror (tube repair, union dues, church bake sale) | Slapstick or pratfall comedy |
| Long pauses, wrong tone, someone saying the worst possible thing calmly | Sitcom rhythm (setup/punchline every beat) |
| Jazz-club glamour that curdles — vice as texture, not punchline | "Ha ha drugs" or ironic nostalgia without stakes |

Mid-century lens stays: cool jazz, cigarette smoke, licensed-salon vice. Humor is **structural** — the gap between what people think they're doing (normal Tuesday) and what they're actually doing (bootleg cores, Commission debt, a clerk who cannot make the numbers match). Sex and prudery (B12) land **uncomfortable**, not horny sitcom.

### Sample beat (Barry-adjacent voice)

*The Blue Note, 1:40 a.m. Artie Shaw's ledger is still in the cellar safe. Upstairs, Vic Moretti's nephew Danny is explaining to the sax player why the set has to end early.*

Danny: "We need the room."

Sax player: "We're in the middle of *Night in Tunisia*."

Danny: "I know. That's why it's a problem."

The player looks at the trumpet case. Danny has not brought a gun. He has brought a manila envelope and the expression of someone returning a library book late.

Danny: "My uncle says the Audit desk sent two men. They don't drink. They take notes."

Sax player: "So let them take notes."

Danny: "They take notes *and* they have a seizure warrant."

A woman at the bar laughs once, too loud, then covers her mouth like she forgot where she was. Danny nods at her like they're sharing an elevator.

Danny: "Finish the tune. Then everyone goes home like nothing happened. Especially you."

He leaves. The band plays the last chorus slower than it needs to. Nobody claps.

## 4. Sensory Palette

- **Sounds:** A trumpet solo echoing down a wet brick alley at 2 a.m.; the hiss of steam escaping from a tenement radiator; the metallic clatter of speaking-tubes; the iron bell of a sanitation truck.
- **Smells:** Acrid coal smoke from foundry chimneys; river mud and salt water at the piers; garlic and oregano from Italian social clubs; funeral incense and stale tobacco.
- **Lights:** Neon signs glowing outside licensed clubs; flickering gas mantles in the slums; the dim blue aura of a warded doorframe; the harsh glare of a detective's flashlight.

## 5. Gender Norms (Diegetic Reality)

The campaign world reflects a conservative, male-dominated mid-century society.
- **The Default:** Men run the family rackets, hold union offices, and serve as captains. Women are expected to manage the home, the church socials, and the family ledgers.
- **The Operators:** Female characters who enter the underworld must be twice as sharp and twice as tough as their male counterparts. They are accepted as "operators" (fixers, ward-breakers, mages) if they deliver results, but they must navigate the diegetic prudery and patronizing attitudes of traditional capos.
- **Underworld Expression:** These dynamics are shown through prices (e.g., different licensing fees for male vs. female-run salons), social club rules, and the patronizing tone of old-school dons.

## 6. Anti-Patterns (What to Avoid)

- **NO** smartphones, computers, television, or default radio.
- **NO** automatic firearms (no Tommy guns, no submachine guns).
- **NO** gunpowder or chemical propellant — **capsule arms** only (`SETTING-ARMS.md`).
- **NO** "magic painted on modern NYC." The city is low-rise brick, guild spires, and factory smoke—not glass skyscrapers or interstate belts (B18; `SETTING-URBAN-FRINGE.md` §7).
- **NO** vampires/werewolves as ordinary voters or licensed salon owners without a human front.
- **NO** cartoon druid eco-villains — cells have politics; the **terror** label serves **someone's** ledger.
- **NO** Volstead Act or bootleg gin clones. The illegal trade is in **unlicensed construct cores**, **bootleg healing bands**, and **unregistered spirit-binding foci**.
- **NO** casual necromancy. Raising the dead is a massive scandal and a federal crime.
- **NO** "science discovers magic" explanations. Magic is guild craft, temple precedent, or patron revelation.
