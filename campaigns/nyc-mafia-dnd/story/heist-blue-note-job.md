## 1. The leash (what they owe)

The crew's last job — the one that put food on the table and bought them another month — was prying open a counting-room door that turned out to belong to a family. Not a storefront. Not a competing crew. A family.

Which family? **Valenti.** (Canon lock: `reports/2026-08-01-five-families.md` — Valenti runs Red Hook docks; the counting room was a Valenti-contract shipping clerk's back office the crew hit for a dead-drop retrieval job. They didn't know whose door they were kicking until the ledger book on the desk had a Valenti seal.)

The debt collector arrived two weeks later. Name: **Lena "the Crane" Lombardo** — Valenti capo, Red Hook union hall. She didn't threaten. She handed over a typed note:

> *Blue Note. West 52nd. Cellar safe. Artie Shaw's book. Return it to me. We're square.*

The crew took the job. They are not in a position to say no.

**Session open:** the crew has the address, the shape of the building from a sidewalk pass, and a 48-hour deadline.

## 2. Casing notes (what they know going in)

Gathered from a single late-night walk-by and a drink at the bar (face/muscle cover):

**Front (public):**
- **The Blue Note** — 52nd Street between 6th & 7th. Licensed jazz club. Green awning, neon script sign. Two-door entry: main street door, service alley side door.
- **Hours:** Sets at 9 p.m., 11 p.m., 1 a.m. Last call 2:30 a.m.
- **Staff:** Doorman (half-orc, tuxedo, goes by "Big Mike"), bartender (human, thin, nervous), two waitresses, three-piece bebop quartet.
- **Patrons:** Midtown money — attorneys, club owners, Ashford-adjacent faces. No obvious family colors on a slow night.
- **Price check:** $2.50 a drink at the bar. $15 for a back-room "holy water" shot — minor healing elixir, not on the menu.

**Service alley (north side):**
- Iron door, no visible lock (magetic or push-bar), single bare bulb.
- Trash cans, coal chute cover, speaking-tube junction box with a Valenti guild stamp (scratched, old).
- Alley connects to 52nd midblock; foot traffic drops after 1 a.m.

**Building shape (exterior):**
- Four-story brownstone. Club occupies ground floor and cellar. Upper floors: rented offices (dentist, tailor, "consultancy" — Ashford front on the third floor per `reports/2026-08-01-locations.md`).
- Fire escape on the east face, drops to a locked gate in the service alley.

**What the crew does NOT know (GM's pocket):**
- The club owner (Artie Shaw) owes Chen-Okafor, not Valenti. He operates as a neutral-front man but the Chen-Okafor debt means he reports to Flushing, not Brooklyn.
- The Ashford "consultancy" on the third floor has a speaking-tube splice down to the cellar. It's not a coincidence.
- The clockwork bartender in the cellar is unlicensed and unregistered — if it glitches during a casting, it reads magic as a threat.

## 3. Role beats (by seat)

### Mastermind — the plan

The mastermind's job starts before the crew steps inside. They built the timeline from the casing notes and the crew's known capabilities:

**The plan (crew-facing):**
- **23:30** — Wheel drops crew at the 52nd service alley. Keeps the motor-carriage warm, engine not cut.
- **23:45** — Face enters through the front. Buys a drink. Watches Big Mike's table rotation, counts the waitress intervals to the back office, notes who is in the private booths. Signal: napkin on the bar = clear.
- **23:55** — Second-story goes up the fire escape, drops into the third-floor "consultancy" hallway, takes the service stairs down to the cellar. Wires or Ward-breaker follows twenty seconds behind — same route.
- **00:10** — Muscle and second-string arrive through the service alley. Muscle holds the iron door. Wires/Ward-breaker and second-story crack the cellar safe.
- **00:25** — Retrieve the ledger. Everybody out. Alley rendezvous. Wheel pulls around the block.

**Mastermind's contingency:** if the face's drink gets cut (Big Mike escorts them out), abort to Plan B: full group through the service alley, muscle takes the doorman, everybody goes cellar-direct. Time window stays the same.

### Face — the invitation

The face buys a drink at the bar. Covers the crew's entry window with presence — regular customer, well-dressed, nursing a bourbon.

**Beats:**
- Compliment the quartet between sets. Tip the bartender. Ask about the holy water shot (not for you — just curious).
- Watch Big Mike's pattern: he works the door on a 30-minute rotation. A second doorman (younger, human, cheap suit) covers him for smoke breaks — that's the window.
- If the mastermind needs Plan B, the face starts a minor distraction at the bar (spilled drink, loud complaint about the bourbon being watered) to pull Big Mike off the door.

**Gap:** the face has no cover to get past the velvet curtain into the back office. That's not this seat's job.

### Muscle — the door

Candice Graves or whoever slots here. Job: hold the service alley door, keep it clear, don't let anyone lock the crew in the cellar.

**Beats:**
- 23:30 — arrive with the crew. Case the alley mouth. Note the fire escape landing. Note the coal chute — secondary egress, but tight fit.
- 00:10 — take position at the iron door. Keep it cracked. If Big Mike or a third-floor employee comes down the stairs, block and redirect: "Building super. Steam pipe's leaking. Gotta check the basement." (Weak cover — buy time, not a conversation.)
- **If alarm triggers:** muscle becomes rear guard. Hold the stairwell until the crew is up. Retreat to the alley. Do not fight the clockwork bartender — that thing is a distraction; the real fight is the stairwell.

### Second-story — the roof

Route: fire escape → third floor → service stairs → cellar. No front-door exposure.

**Beats:**
- 23:45 — climb the fire escape. The lock on the third-floor gate is a standard pin tumbler (Locksmiths' Guild, not warded — the Ashford office doesn't want to set off its own alarms going in and out).
- Through the third-floor hallway. Take the service stairs (at the east end, behind a door marked "PRIVATE — EMPLOYEES ONLY"). No windows — travel by touch and memory of the mastermind's timeline.
- 00:10 — arrive at the cellar door ahead of Wires. Hold position. Listen for the clockwork bartender's cycle (wash → pause → wash; ~8-second silence between loads — that's the window to move across the cellar floor).

**Gap:** the second-story doesn't have the ward-breaker tools. Their job is positioning, not the safe.

### Wires / Ward-breaker — the safe

The cellar safe carries a **Locksmiths' Guild Ward** (from `opening-job.md`): silver-chalk boundary lines on the floor, rowan-wood wedges in the door frame, a trigger-gylph on the safe handle that reads living touch.

**Beats:**
- Carry: silver chalk (one stick), rowan-wood wedge (one, pre-carved), heavy cotton gloves (no bare skin on the handle).
- 00:12 — enter the cellar behind second-story. Move during the clockwork bartender's pause cycle.
- **Disarm the ward:** lay the rowan wedge across the silver-chalk boundary line (breaks the circuit — the ward reads the wedge as part of the floor). Touch the safe handle with gloved hand — the trigger-glyph senses cloth fibers, not skin; partial bypass. The safe body resists *knock* or force — must be picked or key-opened.
- If the safe has a mechanical lock (ward body only, no keyplate): pick it. Standard Guild pin-tumbler behind the ward — the ward was the hard part.
- If the safe has a keyplate and the crew didn't bring the key (they didn't — Artie Shaw carries it): drill the plate with a hand brace and a hardened bit. Loud. Fifteen-second window before someone upstairs notices.
- **Retrieve the ledger.** Do not open it. Do not read it. Bag it. Move.

**Gap:** Wires is the most exposed cast during the drill window. If the clockwork bartender glitches from nearby spellcasting, it triggers — and Wires is right there.

### Wheel — the exit

The wheel stays with the vehicle. 52nd Street midblock, service alley mouth.

**Beats:**
- 23:20 — arrive. Park at a hydrant (ticket risk — the crew has a forged valet tag to hang on the mirror, bought from a Chen-Okafor runner for $5). Engine off, key in pocket.
- Keep the route warm: Hudson Street north → 57th → West Side Highway ramp. Two-minute escape window from alley mouth to highway cover.
- If the alley mouth is blocked (patrol car, OCU wagon, second vehicle): alternate route — east on 52nd to 6th Avenue, south to 42nd, then scatter. The wheel calls out the diversion on a pocket whistle or tube tap; no time for a full signal.

**Contingency:** if the crew doesn't emerge by 00:45, the wheel makes one loop around the block (5 minutes) then leaves. Rendezvous at the Iron Rail Diner, Hoboken. The crew walks or finds their own ride across the ferry.

## 5. What the ledger actually IS

The book is not Artie Shaw's private record. It is a **Spirit-Ledger** — a bound book where each page is a signed contract between a family and either another family, a supplier, or a Below-adjacent entity. The signatures are written in the signatory's own blood, bound by a Jersey ward-artificer whose name appears nowhere in the book.

**Page contents (what the crew sees if they flip it open — and they should not):**
- Bootleg healing-band shipments — Valenti from, Moretti to, Chen-Okafor storage.
- Construct-core delivery dates — unlicensed, unregistered, moving through Red Hook.
- Names — five families, supply-side names, clockwork runners. Nothing the OCU doesn't already suspect.
- **The deeper layer** (inked in a reactive script visible only to certain wards): a chain of **pact signatures** — one per family, pledging a portion of their Grey income to a Below entity called **the Bone Index** in exchange for access to specific Below routes under Manhattan. The Valenti signature is there. The Moretti signature is there. The Kowalski signature is there. The Ashford and Chen-Okafor pages are blank — or appear blank; the Ashford page may have been written in a script that only the pact-holder can see.

**Why everyone wants it:**

| Party | Why they want the ledger |
|-------|--------------------------|
| **Valenti (Lena)** | The family's Below-pact signature is on the page. If it reaches the OCU, that's a magical conspiracy charge — life imprisonment or binding. If it reaches the Commission, that's a violation of the territorial charter. Valenti needs the book back before anyone reads the deeper layer. |
| **Ashford (parallel contract)** | The Ashford page is either blank (meaning the family is not bound to the Bone Index — leverage over the other four families) or written in a script Ashford controls (meaning they *placed* the pact and now want the evidence back before the Commission asks questions). Ashford put a second contract on the ledger through a cutout — they want it too. |
| **OCU (Det. Adaeze Chen-Okafor)** | A bound book with blood signatures and Below-tunnel access details is a slam-dunk RICO case under magical conspiracy statutes. The OCU has a warrant drafted, waiting for the ledger to surface. |
| **The Commission** | If the families are secretly titheing to the Below without Commission approval, that voids every territorial agreement. The Commission needs the ledger to adjudicate — or to burn it and pretend they never saw it. |
| **The Bone Index (Below)** | The pact is the Index's claim on the families. If the ledger is destroyed, the pact is broken and the Below loses the tithe. If the ledger is read aloud by any mortal who understands the deeper script, the pact is *fulfilled* — and the Below collects immediately. |

**Session fuel:** The crew retrieves the ledger, hands it to Lena Valenti, and thinks they're square. Then Ashford's man (Eddie Trillo from the handout) shows up at the pier saying "you have something of mine." Then the OCU calls in a courtesy conversation. Then the crew hears a sound like dry bones rattling from the manhole cover outside the diner. They are holding a grenade with the pin pulled, and everyone in the room knows it.
