# Crew Start — The Party Are Heisters

*Status: Canonical direction (GM ask 2026-08-08) · Campaign-start framing · Sits under `story/premise.md`*

---

## 1. The frame

The party does not start as made men, earners, or associates climbing a family ladder. They start as a **heist crew** — independent operators who steal things for money, already two or three small jobs into working together when Session 1 opens.

This is the contract with the players: you are the people other criminals hire when a thing needs to come out of a place it is locked inside of. The families are clients, bosses-by-debt, and weather — not employers with a dental plan.

## 2. Why heisters (diegetic logic)

- **The Grey runs on product that moves without papers.** Bootleg cores, hot ledgers, unregistered foci — all of it spends most of its life inside someone else's safe. The crew that opens safes eats.
- **Deniability.** A family that sends its own button men into a rival's counting room starts a war. A family that hires a freelance crew loses a retainer and keeps its hands clean. The party is expendable on paper; play is what happens when they refuse to be.
- **The law hunts the crew shape, not the family shape.** The OCU and FSA are built to squeeze made hierarchies — flip a soldier, roll up a capo. A job-based crew with no colors and no social club is a harder target, which is exactly why the families tolerate them and the law hates them.

## 3. Crew roles (table-facing)

Classic heist geometry, spell-tech skin. Players slot where they want; overlap is fine, gaps are session fuel.

| Seat | Job | Setting skin |
|------|-----|--------------|
| **Mastermind** | Plans the job, holds the ledger of who owes what | Reads the scry-press society pages like a defense brief |
| **Muscle** | Doors, guards, carried safes | Candice Graves (see `characters/candice-graves.md`) |
| **Face** | Gets invited in | Works clubs, charity dinners, and temple steps |
| **Second-story** | Gets uninvited in | Rooftops, dumbwaiters, pneumatic service runs |
| **Wires** | Alarms, wards, locks, tube-splices | Bootleg spell-tech; a solder iron and a stolen guild stamp |
| **Wheel** | Gets everyone gone | Slow motor-carriages, el-train timing, subway service tunnels |

## 4. Where they start (Session 0/1 state)

- **Reputation:** known in the Grey as competent and so-far-honest about splits. Not feared. Not protected.
- **Money:** one bad month from the pawnshop. The crew fund lives in a coffee tin.
- **Heat:** a shared OCU file with no names yet, just a case number and a sketch of Candice's ears.
- **The job ladder they already climbed:** back-alley collection → warehouse windows → a counting-room door that turned out to belong to a family (that debt is Session 1's leash).

## 5. The leash — Session 1 hook (canon, from `premise.md` §4)

The Blue Note job: recover Artie Shaw's hot ledger from the cellar safe of **The Blue Note** jazz club on West 52nd. The crew takes it because the family that owns that counting-room door from §4 calls the debt. It reads like a straightforward heist and ends with the party holding something the Commission, the OCU, and at least one thing in the Below all want.

## 6. Standing rules for the crew frame

1. **Jobs are the spine.** Between-jobs play (council of war, casing, crew upkeep, personal heat) hangs off the next score.
2. **The crew is not a family.** No one is owed a funeral. This may not survive contact with play; that erosion is the drama.
3. **No gunpowder** (LOCKS). Heist violence is capsule arms, brawl, blade, and bootleg spell-tech — loud jobs fail differently than quiet ones.
4. **Player sheets trump this doc.** Where a player's backstory conflicts, the sheet wins and this doc bends (Candice's criminal background already fits the muscle seat — no rewrite needed, just a crew to stand in).
