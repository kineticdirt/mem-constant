*GM lock · **2026-08-02** · **B21 LOCKED** (see `LOCKS.md`). Voice: binder (B19). No exact calendar year.*

**Thesis:** A "photo" here is usually **not** silver chemistry. It is a **captured sight** — bound light in ward-glass, a living-ink impression, or (for most people, most days) a **projection** you call up with a printed **glyph-index** at the newsstand. The city **sees** well and **archives** badly. Computation is weak; perception magic is guild-strong.

## 1. Scry-capture — what a "photo" is

Players and NPCs will say **photo**, **still**, **plate**, **lantern-slide**, **verity-shot** — same family, different price and legal weight.

### Three capture tiers (all coexist)

| Tier | Name (street) | What it is | Who has it |
|------|---------------|------------|------------|
| **A — Projection still** | **Lantern-slide**, **verity-board**, **scry-still** | A licensed **scry pool** holds a moment; a **verity-lantern** (continual-flame housing + ground-glass lens) **projects** it onto a board or frosted window. The "image" is **live fetch** from a guild vault or newsroom **index** — not ink on paper. | Newsstands, *Tribune* lobby, OCU briefing room, Ashford society fundraisers |
| **B — Bound-light plate** | **Ward-glass plate**, **bound still**, **copper slide** | A **minor image** or **silent image** ritual **freezes** light into treated ward-glass or copper-backed plate. Physical object. Survives without the pool. | Court exhibits, family blackmail, wedding studios on Second Ave, German elf lens-grinders |
| **C — Living ink** | **Breath impression**, **pulse-print** | Alchemist **living ink** (often Erkling trade) takes a **one-use** impression from a scry moment; fades in weeks unless re-fed. Luxury papers, scandal sheets, forged "originals." | *Herald-Post* color supplement, Chen-Okafor forgery shops, Harlem society photographers |

**Public default = projection (Tier A).** Most New Yorkers who "saw it in the paper" **read the headline** and **watched the lantern** at the stand. They did not hold a photograph. This is not a failure of technology — it is **economics and weak computation**. Halftone reproduction of a plate at scale would need **stable math** the city does not have. Instead: **print the index, project the truth.**

### How capture works (play grammar)

1. **Witness** — a licensed **scry-operator** (guild or *Tribune* stringer) attunes a **verity lens** to a scene, or a fixed **arcane eye** (licensed) feeds a pool.
2. **Bind** — for a plate, the operator spends focus and consumables; the plate **holds** until shattered, **dispel magic**, or ley-weather blanking.
3. **Index** — newsrooms assign a **glyph-code** (e.g. `TRIB-LANTERN-4471`) stamped beside the headline. The code is the **database key** — the database is a **room of clerks and plates**, not a computer.
4. **Project** — newsstand **verity-lantern** (coin slot or day's password in the paper margin) fetches the still from the **pool**. Wrong code → wrong face (see §5).

**Not default:** silver-nitrate chemistry, wirephoto radio networks, television. Those are **our** timeline. Here, **scrying is the camera.**

### Perception strong / archival weak

German **elf** optical houses grind lenses; **scry-glass** is a real export (`SETTING-PEOPLES-RACES.md`). The city can **see** across wards (licensed). It cannot **search** a million plates by face — no facial recognition engine, no reliable catalog at scale. OCU detectives still **carry** bound plates to court; they do not **query** a cloud.

## 2. Printing press — what actually hits paper

Gilded-era **iron presses** + guild **fabricate** batches (`SETTING-SPELL-TECH-TREE.md`) still dominate. Ink is ink. What changes is **what gets reproduced.**

| Output | Method | Lifespan | Role |
|--------|--------|----------|------|
| **Line cut** | Engraved block, steam press | Years | Portraits of commissioners, factory owners, ships — **drawn** faces, not scry |
| **Sigil-stamp** | Warded rubber + ritual ink | Months | Ward-tags, FAB forms, seizure manifests |
| **Shadow-print** | Cheap **silent image** off a plate, one generation | **Fades** (weeks–months) | Tenement weeklies, handbills, wanted posters on lampposts |
| **Projection token** | **Glyph-index** + caption only | Until edition sells out | Mass "photo" in the *Tribune* — **call the lantern** |
| **Luxury plate** | Bound-light original bound into **folio** | Decades | Ashford weddings, Pemberton bond prospectuses, museum |

**Mass "photo" on the front page** is almost always a **projection token**: bold headline, **glyph-box** in the corner, instructions (*"Lantern 4 — Fulton stand"*). The Ashford-owned **Herald-Post** runs **living-ink** color on Sunday for subscribers who pay for a **physical** still — status object, not news efficiency.

**Press barons (Gilded texture):** *Tribune* (Midtown, polite denial), *Herald-Post* (Ashford lean), Yiddish **Forward**-style sheets on the Lower East Side, Harlem **Defender**-style neighborhood papers. They share **Pemberton** rail for plate crates; they **compete** on pool access and **stringer** scry licenses.

## 3. Newspapers — front pages and evening editions

Culture reads **mid-century** (`reports/2026-08-01-era-and-tone.md`): cold-war dread, labor panic, jazz ads. Hardware reads **late-1800s**: typewriters that weigh forty pounds, pneumatic tubes from courthouse to newsroom, boys on bicycles with **plate pouches**.

### Why evening editions without wirephoto

There is **no wirephoto network** — no fax of an image across the country. There **is**:

- **Scry pools** — licensed guild vaults; *Tribune* rents hours from **Vandermeer**-adjacent switchboard clerks who also route speaking-tubes.
- **Stringers** — kenku couriers, half-orc boxers with a **verity lens** in a trumpet case, elf photographers who never leave the club balcony.
- **Clerk schedules** — a human writes **which lantern** gets which index at **which hour**. Math is addition with mistakes.

So **evening edition** means: afternoon raid → plate bound by 4 → engraver draws **line cut** for morning, **glyph** for evening lantern → stands project by **6**. Not instant. Fast enough for **heat** and **football**.

### What the front page looks like

| Element | *Tribune* (denial-tier) | Open-ward sheet (Harlem / Yiddish) |
|---------|--------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Headline** | RAID, SEIZURE, "UNLICENSED CLINIC" — names the **event**, euphemizes magic (`ward-visibility-press-and-passing.md`) | Names the **rite**, the **consultant**, the **horn** if it's news |
| **Image** | Glyph-box + **lantern stand** number; line-cut **mayor** or **factory**; rarely the raid itself | Bound still **or** projection; club glamour **visible** |
| **Rumor column** | "Sympathy vice," unnamed consultants | Who got healed, who got cursed |

**Ashford** buys ads disguised as **society** — Continental lighting, guest lists, no service door. The *Tribune* prints **scandal** as **inventory**.

## 4. Legal / crime — OCU evidence and forgery

Extends `reports/2026-08-01-law-and-magic.md` §3.

| Evidence | Legal weight | Beat / forge |
|----------|--------------|--------------|
| **Bound-light plate (original)** | **Very strong** — physical chain of custody; guild seal on rim | **Shatter and rebind** (expensive), **swap plate** in pouch (Valenti dock trick), **ley-blank** alibi |
| **Projection still (pool fetch)** | **Strong in briefing**, **weaker in court** — "any lantern can lie if the clerk is bought" | Bribe **index clerk**; **desync** code in print (§5) |
| **Shadow-print** | **Medium** — fades; defense argues **copy of a copy** | Chen-Okafor **forgery** shops; living-ink **one-night** fake |
| **Line cut engraving** | **Weak for identity** — artistic likeness | N/A — not proof you were there |
| **Tube recording** | Still **very strong** (unchanged) | Splice-out |

**Can you forge a scry-still?** Yes, with pain. **Minor illusion** on a lantern fools **crowds** for minutes. A **court-grade** plate needs **living ink** + stolen **guild rim-seal** + a **scry-operator** willing to perjure — Ashford rates apply. OCU keeps a **DOJ diviner** on retainer to read **residue** on the glass (like ward dust).

**Image deny (rare):** Parallel to **healing-deny** — a **nondetection** / **private sanctum** variant tuned to **visual truth**. Targets **capture**, not memory. Temple **Veil rites** on a corpse; **celebrity** sanctum consultants. **Not** on every mob soldier — expensive, licensed, scandalous. When it works, the plate shows **smear** or **empty doorway**; OCU calls it **"lantern sickness"** and looks for tube recordings instead.

## 5. Failure modes

| Failure | Cause | Street result |
|---------|-------|---------------|
| **Ley-weather blank** | Storm, feast-day patron clash, automata core **glitch** nearby | Plates **white**; projections **snow**; today's still **gone** — "*the glass forgot*" |
| **Projection desync** | Clerk typo in glyph; rival **swaps index card**; pool **double-booked** | Wrong face on the stand — **headline right, martyr wrong**; riots, lawsuits, comedy |
| **Caption drift** | Two editions, one plate; **no master clock** | Morning paper says **GUILTY**, evening lantern shows **acquittal** — nobody reconciles |
| **Schedule chaos** | Weak computation (`SETTING-CAPABILITY-ASYMMETRY.md`) | **Football score** on the murder slide; boys sell **two passwords** for the same slot |
| **Polite denial** | *Tribune* prints **goods** not **miracles** | Public **believes** magic is rumor; open-ward kids **laugh** at the lantern |

**Play:** Trust **paper** less than **plate**; trust **plate** less than **tube**; trust **tube** until Ashford **files FAB objections**.

## 6. Example (Barry-dry)

Detective Mara O'Brien buys the **evening *Tribune*** outside the Chambers Street el. The headline says **FEDERAL MEN SEIZE WEST SIDE CLINIC**. The glyph-box says **LANTERN-12**.

The newsstand kid cranks the **verity-lantern**. The board flickers. A door. Men in **iron boots**. A chalk sign that says **HEALING** in letters too honest for the *Tribune*.

Mara says, "That's not the West Side."

The kid says, "That's what it says."

Mara looks at the **plate pouch** on her belt — **original**, sealed, **still warm**. The lantern shows **Tuesday**. Her plate shows **Thursday**. Same raid. Different **clerk**.

She folds the paper. She keeps the plate. The kid asks if she wants a refund. She says, "You want a refund when the glass forgets?"

He shrugs. He sells her a **second password** for **LANTERN-11**. That one is a **football score**.

Mara walks toward the federal building. Somewhere, an Ashford lawyer is already typing about **inadmissible projection**.
