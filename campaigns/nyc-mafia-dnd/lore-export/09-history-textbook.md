# 09 — A History of the Metropolitan Stack

*Player-facing · history textbook · compiled from campaign locks · v2026-08-09*

**How to read this.** There is no single calendar year on the cover. Historians in this city argue in eras, feast-days, and guild charter numbers. Where a date appears (1910, 1952, 1870s), it is a **local institutional year** — a registry stamp, a founding plaque — not an invitation to pin the whole world to “1958.”

Where this text goes beyond a locked SoT line, it is marked **〔proposal〕**. The GM may cut, rename, or promote those lines.

---

# Part I — The Deep Past

## I.1 Magic before machines

Before brass, before charter stamps, before the harbor was a harbor in the modern sense, the peoples who would later call this coast home practiced **patron craft**: oaths, bindings, light that stayed lit because a saint or a spirit was paid in attention. Technology did not begin in a laboratory. It began in a temple kitchen, a guild attic, and a funeral house that needed the dead to answer **one** question and no more.

The important fact for later centuries: **guild magic learned what a body was** long before it learned what a number was. Healing, death rites, and oath-binding outran abstract calculation — and never surrendered that lead. That asymmetry is not a modern accident. It is the oldest industrial bias in the stack.

## I.2 Peoples, not allegories

Fantasy ancestries arrived the way human diasporas arrive: ships, wars, famines, ambition, and rumor. They did **not** map one-to-one onto ethnic labels. Humans lived in every people-group from the start. Elves among German settlers, hobgoblins among Italian parish networks, Erklings and Goddarts among Jewish Lower East Side trades, firbolgs on Welsh lumber routes — these are **neighborhood mixes**, not racial destinies. (Full tables: Part VI and `03-peoples.md`.)

Fringe beings — vampires, lycanthropes, older things — were known early and pushed early. Cities learned to put them at the edge: night markets, wilds, sealed digs. Midtown polite denial is a late fashion on an old habit.

## I.3 〔proposal〕 The long dusk before the Charter Age

Oral tradition (parish, synagogue, mosque, storefront temple) agrees on little except this: there was a long period when **casting was common and industry was not**. Travel was slow. Truth was local. A *continual flame* on a hook was wealth. A speaking-tube pair across two courtyards was a marvel. Automata were children’s stories or siege nightmares from overseas — not dock loaders.

Historians call this the **Pre-Charter Age**. It ends when municipal and guild charters begin to tax, license, and stamp spell-craft as if it were flour.

# Part II — The Harbor Becomes a Machine

## II.1 Why this coast

The metropolitan region — five boroughs plus the Jersey belt — dominates because **cities dominate**. Farms feed it. Forests harass it. Albany and Washington send paper. Power lives in ward boards, guild charters, Commission turf, and rail bonds.

Harbor geography made the rest inevitable: deep water, river mouths, islands that force bridges and ferries into choke points. Whoever taxed the choke points taxed the continent’s eastern appetite.

## II.2 Early municipal craft

Long before smokestacks, the city ran on:

| Craft | Spell analog | Street form |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| Light | *continual flame*, *light* | Hook-lamps; later Lamplighters’ Union |
| Message | *message* | Paired brass tubes; runners and chalk before that |
| Repair | *mending* | Parish and apprentice benches |
| Truth | *zone of truth*, notary-cleric | Courthouse circles; later bootleg “truth rooms” |
| Lift | *feather fall*, wind-spirit cages | Stairs first; mid-rise cages later |

**〔proposal〕 Tube-Layer’s craft** is usually dated to the long eighteenth century: sympathetic brass pairs, then hub exchanges, then Midtown pneumatic mail. Wrong pairings leaked then. They still leak.

## II.3 The burial registry and the politics of death

Funeral houses and parish networks learned early that **the dead are evidence**. One-question *speak with dead* culture, *gentle repose*, and paper ledgers became soft power. The Moretti-linked registry tradition claims continuous borough death records **since 1910** (institutional stamp). Control of who can be summoned is older than the modern Commission — and is one reason “consigned to the parish registry” is a sentence that ends trials.

## II.4 Emancipation scars and the Great Migration

Slavery’s end did not end its **wards**. Pre-Emancipation bound-labor contracts still surface in old deeds and Below-adjacent routes; OCU slang calls the residue **“heritage necromancy.”** Harlem deeds carry void covenant clauses that Ashford clerks redact for fees. Great Migration memory lives in trunks, train tickets, and kitchen wall-contracts — concrete artifacts, not essays. Dock unions and numbers routes still remember who got licensed first.

# Part III — Three Centuries of Spell-Industry

*Locked frame: ~300 years from Renaissance guild/temple craft to late-1800s street brass, while culture later wears mid-century clothes.*

## III.1 The invention grammar (how anything gets built)

Every durable technology in this history follows the same path:

1. Name a need (light, lift, kill, haul, prove, heal).
2. Fill it with a D&D-plausible spell, school, or ritual.
3. Price it (street craft / guild secret / temple patronage / rare reagents / construct core).
4. Learn its failure mode (weird, not mechanical).
5. License it — or bootleg it.
6. Iterate one generation back and one forward so it feels path-dependent.

**Forbidden path:** chemistry-first industry, gunpowder, “science discovers mana,” reliable symbolic computers.

## III.2 Stage table

| Stage | What you would have seen | Magic engine | Mundane parallel |
|-------|--------------------------|--------------|------------------|
| **Guild** | Brick workshops, parish halls | *mending*, *light*, *message*, hand *fabricate* | Hand tools, sail, horse |
| **Early industrial** | Smokestacks, clock towers, rail yards | Continual-flame forges, *unseen servant* lines, *animate objects* galleries | Brass, coal, **capsule** frames |
| **Rising steampunk** | Ward-towers, foundry spires, rail chokes | Bound-air cages, batch *fabricate*, shield-guardian prototypes | No Tommy guns; no radio default |
| **Threat tier (present)** | Runaway rumors, empty factories that still run | **True automata** — purpose without operator | Emergency commissions; FSA shoot-to-scrap |

## III.3 The computation stall

Industry poured money into **bodies, bindings, and brass** — not abstract state machines. Guild “calculating engines” became prestige follies: loud, feast-day drift, spirits that hate arithmetic. Numbers rackets still prefer **clerks + divination**. Rail schedules are human chaos. This is not a temporary lag. It is a civilizational choice with a price.

## III.4 Capsule arms replace powder

While Europe and elsewhere in *our* timeline chased chemical propellant, this stack’s foundries sealed **spell-craft into brass cylinders**. Mana pulse and kinetic sling became the two base families. Specialty loads (silence, healing-deny, ward-pierce…) followed the underworld arms race: medicine was strong, so killers competed on **how** a body stayed broken. Reload stayed a ritual — humiliating, slow, fatal under fire. Automatic powder weapons never became identity. They were never invented as a mass path.

## III.5 Automata — domestic hubris

**Locked origin:** the **Iron Guild of New Jersey** and allied foundries bind elemental spirits into clockwork cores. Operator-required loaders are ordinary industrial tension (unions call them scabs). **Autonomous cores** — purpose that persists without an operator — are the existential inflection. Rogue constructs have retreated into the Below and formed dangerous collectives. The city is afraid of what it bred. FSA policy: shoot-to-scrap.

# Part IV — Gilded Rails and Robber Barons

## IV.1 When ward-rail beat the horse

On the Jersey flats, guild magic finally out-hauled horse teams. **Ley survey** stakes, **glyph-warded** ties, bound steam spirits in boilers, *animate objects* on drive rods, and **ritual conductors** with schedule seals created **ley-rail / ward-rail**. Robber-baron fortunes followed: limestone fronts, hospital wings with guild-stamped admissions, patronage politics, and bond votes that still move the city more than most elections.

## IV.2 The syndicates (present owners of yesterday’s theft)

| Syndicate | Holds | Lean |
|-----------|-------|------|
| **Pemberton Ley-Rail Consolidated** | NE Corridor trunk — Newark ↔ Grand Central ↔ New Haven | Ashford lawyers; Green Desk quiet before bond votes |
| **Harlan Brass & Steam** | Freight, capsule switch engines, loader cores | Valenti night cargo; Iron Guild |
| **Vandermeer Terminal Co.** | Grand Central ward-hall; tube trunk under concourse | Ashford equity; Chen-Okafor arcade shops |
| **Jamaica Yards Cooperative** | Long Island branch; ice-car reagents | Chen-Okafor diviners |
| **Morris & Essex Ward Line** | Jersey commuter belts; tank-farm spurs | Valenti ↔ Chen-Okafor handoffs |

**〔proposal〕 Baron faces (thin, replaceable):** Silas Pemberton III (human philanthropist wing), Magda Harlan (dwarf-guild old money), Cornelis Vandermeer (terminal air rights). The Commission runs vice. These people rent everyone the infrastructure.

## IV.3 Failure modes that became folklore

Ghost schedules. Feast-day stalls. Loader cars that unhook themselves. Ward misroutes that put Chen-Okafor reagents in Moretti funeral ice. Conductor *recall* scrolls that stop a train hard enough to give a passenger a past life for an afternoon. The *Tribune* prints labor panic. The schedule board at Grand Central lies when automata cores pass within three blocks.

## IV.4 The Below as industrial accident

Nineteenth-century water works and transit digs folded a collapsed demiplane into the bedrock. Surface maps do not cover it. Families tax the ways in. Law ends at the grate — loud casting still rings surface seismographs. Player history stops at rumor (see `06-fringe-and-monsters.md`). Deep geography is mid-campaign.

**Institutional plaque:** sealed **1870s** water works still mark Staten Island access folklore. Treat the plaque as real; treat the tour as fatal.

# Part V — The Present Stack

## V.1 What “now” feels like

| Domain | Feels like | Players notice |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| Nightlife, dress, voice | Mid-century NYC/Jersey | Bebop, sharkskin, cold-war papers, Mulberry wakes |
| Street hardware | Late 1800s industrial | Tubes, steam subway, capsule revolvers, coal |
| Skyline | Pre-steel-tower | Brick 4–8 stories; limestone cliffs Midtown |
| Medicine / death | Ahead of street tech | Salons; resurrection as politics |
| Automata | Modern–future threat | Runaways, union extinction fear |
| Computation | Behind everyone | Bad engines; clerks win |

A man in a mid-century hat rides an 1890 lift, gets an aneurysm closed like a future miracle, and signs a contract no engine can audit. The *Tribune* calls it progress.

## V.2 Law as industry’s shadow

- **FAB (Federal Audit Bureau)** — licenses, stamps, foundry taxes.
- **FSA (Federal Seizure Agency)** — raids bootleg salons and cores; shoot-to-scrap.
- **OCU (Organized Crime Unit)** — founded **1952**; ward-sniffers; tube splices; wax cylinders. Annex: Broadway & Stone.

Contraband metaphor (locked): **bootleg ritual** — unlicensed cores, healing bands, unbound foci — not Volstead gin.

## V.3 The Commission

Five families — Valenti, Moretti, Chen-Okafor, Kowalski, Ashford — sit quarterly. Street rule: no magic hits on civilians without a vote. The Commission did not invent the city. It learned to tax the Grey that industrialization created: cores, bands, foci, numbers, disposal, Midtown laundry.

**〔proposal〕** Modern Commission form consolidates in the two generations after OCU’s founding — when federal desks forced families to prefer freelancers and deniable crews over open button-man wars. (This is institutional gossip, not a founding myth you must recite.)

## V.4 Culture veneer on brass bones

Jazz clubs as vice infrastructure. Instruments as foci (sax enchantment, trumpet flash, bass silence). Glow-Gin, Mending-Rye, Blues. Scry-press evenings: glyph-index in the paper, verity-lantern at the stand, bound-light plates for court. Gender norms diegetic and male-default; operators of any gender who deliver results still pay social tax. Tone compass: dry crime drama — funny until it isn’t.

## VI.3 〔proposal〕 Rough metro population texture

No census engine works. Temple counts, guild dues rolls, and numbers-room guesses disagree. For play, treat the metro as **multi-million** and **crowded**, with:

| Zone | Texture |
|------|---------|
| Manhattan | Densest; Midtown denial; stacked diasporas by block |
| Brooklyn | Industrial + tenement mass; Red Hook / Greenpoint labor |
| Queens | Lending faces, rail yards, Flushing / Jamaica hubs |
| Bronx | Contested smuggling arterials; South Bronx growth |
| Staten Island | Thinner residential; disposal/logistics gravity |
| Jersey belt | Commuter + foundry + tank farm; Iron Guild weight |

Exact headcounts are a GM tool, not a player homework assignment. Ancestry **visibility** matters more than ancestry **counts** at a Midtown door.

## VII.6 Wage bands (who can buy what)

| Role | Rough pay |
|------|-----------|
| Unskilled tenement labor | $4–$6 / day |
| Union longshore / dwarf boiler-smith | $10–$15 / day |
| OCU detective, first grade | ~$18 / day |
| Ledger job (success) | $50–$100 |

A bootleg restoration band is a week’s skilled wages. A neurological salon is a small house’s nightmare. Resurrection is not a price list — it is politics.

## VII.7 What is *not* manufactured here

Gunpowder arms. Reliable citywide radio. Television. Glass curtain-wall towers. Interstate cloverleafs. Stable spreadsheet engines. Lab-coat biotech. Casual undead labor. Vampire citizenship papers.
