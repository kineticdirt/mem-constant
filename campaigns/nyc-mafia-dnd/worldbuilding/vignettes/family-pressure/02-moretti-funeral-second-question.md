# The Chair That Asks Twice

## Scene

St. Rocco's prep parlor on Mulberry kept the curtains drawn and the incense cheap. Licensed front: one **ghost-bind** question per corpse, logged for temple **Arcane Affairs**, not BCC — the loophole Moretti chaplains lived inside. The body on the board had been a baker who owed protection and died mid-argument; the first question was already answered and written in the registry book in a gnome scribe's careful hand.

*Who held the knife?* — *His brother-in-law, drunk, not hired.*

**Tommy "Two Prayers" Moretti** stood in the doorway in a coat too fine for the smell of formalin. Chaplain's apprentice, uncle the Don's favorite cage. The **'31 Packard** waited outside with a ward glyph in the glovebox he wasn't supposed to flash on parish streets.

The widow wanted a second question. Cash on the sideboard, thick enough to smell Depression-hungry. Illegal. Everybody in the room knew the price chart from the blind-tiger economy: second question was ghost-bind without license — Kowalski river talk adjacent, Moretti franchise poison if BCC sniffed residue.

"Ask who paid the brother-in-law," she whispered. "I heard a **link-house** rumor — that he wasn't drunk, he was **stitched** calm." Measured, ugly, possible. Tommy felt the temptation like a second incense: power, modernization, the speaking-tube copies his uncle called degenerate — proof that the registry could hold more than one truth.

He opened the glovebox glyph in his mind and shut it.

"One question," Tommy said. "Temple log. Chaplain franchise. You buy a second and we buy a federal desk."

The widow's money stayed. Tommy took only the licensed fee and a name for the protection books. On the way out he told the gnome scribe: "If anyone asks twice tonight, you call me — not the river men."

In the Packard he almost dictated a tube copy of the registry page to Staten. Almost. Filial loyalty won by an inch. The second question waited in the widow's purse like a job for operators who weren't yet Made.
