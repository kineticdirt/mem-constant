# What the Tide Refuses

## Scene

Bard Avenue after dark: three cats on the stoop, basement door shut like always, **Dominika "Docks" Kowalski** in a reflective vest counting truck slips at the kitchen table. Rosary one pocket. **Gentle repose** scroll the other. Fear set her prices; she preferred the fear honest.

The night driver — human, iron bell on his belt, never alone — put a crate receipt down that wasn't hers.

"Fresh Kills special," he said. "Not on the book. Cash in the candle box at Ferry Candle. Someone used our route to dump **three** that didn't come through Commission vote."

Dominika didn't open the basement. She opened the ledger of what Sanitation wasn't supposed to know — and what the Commission **was**.

"No miracle hits on civilians without a vote," she recited, flat. Broken often. Never acknowledged. "Unauthorized corpses are how **federal ward-men** learn our plates. Hauling license goes, cover goes, everybody who uses us starts shopping Valenti docks."

The driver wanted ethics simplified: dump, deny, cash. Dominika wanted the river to stay a **tool**, not a confession.

"You find who paid the candle boxes. You do not animate. You do not ask the water a second question. You bring me a name." She slid the scroll across — repose for the three already in the fill, so they wouldn't surface talking. "We disappear what must not exist six months. We do not gift BCC a shoreline of witnesses."

Outside, a garbage truck rattled toward the bridge checkpoint with iron bells and human stevedores — no clockwork scabs, no Midtown pretense. Open-ward Staten said the quiet part: nobody liked Kowalski; everybody used them. Dominika locked the kitchen and left the basement alone. Ethics, for the trash family, meant **keeping the taboo useful** — and finding the cowboy dump before the tide brought it back as evidence.
