# The Die That Stays Pocketed

## Scene

Main Street, Flushing, a Tuesday that smelled like coal smoke and wet wool. The lending office said **community credit** on the glass; behind the glass, **David Chen-Okafor** signed a **truth-bind IOU** for a laundry that would never repay on time and both of them knew it. The paper glowed once — amount honest — then went dull. Clean money. Older families hated that.

The runner from the **Jade Scratch** corridor came in without knocking: human kid, ink still under his nails from last night's luck tray. He put a sealed numbers slip on the desk and a question with it.

"Valenti's skimming the joint route again. Cousins want you to **read** Thursday's haul before we send muscle. Mirror-scry. Ten minutes."

David's hand went to his breast pocket — the loaded divination die that always rolled well — and left it there.

"Knowing the outcome is a kind of poverty," he said, the way other men said grace. "You deliver. In person. No scry. Diviners on both sides are listening for intercept wax."

The kid's face went sour. "Class-C says we can—"

"Class-C says you can tell a widow which horse likes rain." David stood, tailored, cashier's checkbook open. "Beyond the license we own **odds**, not **certainty**. Certainty is what BCC seizes when the parlor smells like prophecy instead of tea."

He wrote a check — not street cash — and a one-line note for **family-adjacent operators**: *Hand to Lena's cutout only. No tube. No mirror. If you see the future on the way, throw it away.*

Outside, a numbers board clicked wooden tiles. Open-ward Flushing said luck aloud. David locked the office and walked past the parlor without looking at the tray. The die stayed pocketed. The cousins would call it weakness. The Commission would call it discipline. David called it keeping the house from becoming the product.
