## Turf hardness rules (Q2=A — hard cores, soft edges)

- **Core wards are sacred.** A family's home ward cluster is theirs in deed and practice. Crossing to shake down a core business, run product on a core corner, or muscle a core fixer is an open provocation.
- **Edges are contested.** Border wards (Ridgewood, Astoria, Hudson Yards bleed) are grey zones. Friction, tribute negotiations, and small-scale skirmishes happen here without full war.
- **Cross-borough jobs are common** — noir operators move between territories. The hardness is in *ownership*, not *access*. A Red Hook crew can take a job in Little Italy; they just don't set up a permanent desk there without Moretti's nod.
- **Commission exists** to mediate edge disputes before they escalate to open war.

---

## Chen-Okafor dual anchor (Q6=A — Queens=home, Chinatown=franchise)

- **Flushing parlor** is the family hearth: ancestral shrine, ledgers, Commission deputy seat.
- **Chinatown Manhattan** is a franchise. Chen-Okafor number-walkers, divination readers, and luck-product runners operate there, but pay a cut (roughly 15–25% of Chinatown earnings) back to Flushing.
- Local Manhattan tongs and Moretti-adjacent crews know the arrangement. It is *respected*, not loved. No internal family rivalry; this is a clean business split.
- **Do not** treat Chinatown Manhattan as Chen-Okafor sovereign territory. It is an outpost with a franchise fee.

---

## Harlem rule (Q7=A — open patronage market)

- **No family owns Harlem.** Every family has *some* interest — Ashford fronts clubs, Valenti moves product through night-life channels, Moretti's chaplains serve Catholic parishes — but none owns the street.
- **Protection is à la carte.** Speakeasies, dives, and legitimate storefronts buy from whatever family offers the best rate or least hassle that month. Switching patrons is common.
- **Elf patron-artist minority** (C6) acts as a bridge: artists who can get a Valenti product runner into an Ashford-fronted club, or a Moretti chaplain into a Chen-Okafor parlor. The elf social network is a parallel patronage currency.
- Harlem scenes default to **politics and negotiation** — who is paying whom, what favor is owed, who gets squeezed next. Do not treat any family as having automatic street deed.

## Implications for play

- PCs are **family-adjacent operators, not borough landlords** (B8). They move between cores, edges, and patronage markets.
- A job in Harlem means talking to three families and a club owner. A job in Red Hook means Valenti gets a cut or a favor.
- The edge-ward friction is a job engine: tribute disputes, crossed lines, Commission mediation, and the inevitable escalation when a family pushes too hard on a soft edge.
