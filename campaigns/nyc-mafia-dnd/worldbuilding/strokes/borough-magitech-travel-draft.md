## Travel choke points (Q9=A — bridges/ferries are the choke)

Borough borders mean **water crossings**. Play treats these as the primary constraint. Culture is 50s–60s commute voice; kit is late-1800s steam ferry and watched bridges.

| Choke type | Role in play |
|------------|-------------|
| **Bridges** | Watched by audit/seizure desks and family checkpoints (toll of time, cash, or favor). A bridge crossing is a **scene beat**. |
| **Ferries** | Slower, fewer, easier to avoid watch — also easier for gangplank shakedowns. Isolation mid-river. |
| **Ward tubes / local nets** | Fragmented (D3). One-off shortcuts between nearby wards; each tube a different operator. |
| **Below shortcuts** | **Rare plot spice only** until mid-campaign (B6). |

**In practice, cross-borough jobs always pay a cost:**
- **Time:** crossing eats a beat
- **Inspection risk:** desks or family check at landing
- **Favor toll:** family-controlled ferry = nod, cut, or future return

**Do not** invent a citywide magical subway monopoly (D3). **Do not** use Below as routine travel (B6). **Do not** treat ward tubes as a free bypass.

## Implications for play

- Cross-borough jobs: budget an extra beat for **bridge/ferry crossing + inspection**.
- Bootleg bands / foci move via ferry or smuggler's bridge — product routing is a job hook.
- Midtown (Ashford denial) vs Red Hook (open ward) differs by **visibility theater**, not seed counts.
- Automata threat (D2) shows up as dock/foundry plot pressure, not rare garnish.
- Ferry scenes are isolated — no quick backup, no easy escape. Good for negotiation, ambush, or ghost encounters.
- Ward tube as plot device: only one operator runs the tube, the party needs their favor, and the tube only connects two specific wards.
