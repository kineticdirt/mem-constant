## What is locked [canon]

Halkin-Gaul is the named target on the guild quest sheet. It sits in a **frontier swamp basin** past a mountain range from the kingdom’s settled road-spine. The land is being **settled** — not a romantic wilderness park, not a rich trade port. Cobble-and-plaster houses cling to a **slight rise** so they are not always wet to the sill. Around them: bog, marsh, tree cover to the horizon, mildew in the nose, dirt roads that squelch.

About **one to two hundred homes**. Unkempt. Locals look **seedy** and keep their mouths shut. They do not drive strangers out with pitchforks; they also do not smile and offer soup. A **guard shack** of wood and cut stone stands cleaner and more deliberate than most houses. A larger building with **oak doors** and steps reads as **town hall**; pipe-smokers on the steps give the party a once-over and go back to smoking.

The job from SmithsVille’s guildmaster:

- Investigate the town’s **several problems**
- They lack adventurers against **monsters**
- Plus **disappearances**
- **Discretion**
- Thin intel — “be prepared for anything”
- Pay: **1000 gp** total, **200** up front; roughly a **month** of work expected
- Travel: about **ten days** by horse cart when the road behaves

Nobody at the guild could name a culprit. Camila’s road gossip matches that blankness: people go missing and nobody even knows what sign to watch for.

## How the place feels (GM voice)

This is not Louisiana bayou tourism and not a fairy fen. Think **cold overcast frontier** — rain that never quite finishes, mud that remembers every cart wheel, wooden fingerposts rotting at the posts. The stone road that got you through the empty marches ends; bog paths and wooden signs take over. The town exists because someone decided this rise was worth claiming before the water did.

People here live close enough to drown a secret. They also live far enough from SmithsVille that a guild letter takes a long time and a pigeon from a mid-road inn is already a bureaucracy. Insularity is survival manners, not a cartoon “evil village” switch.

When you describe a street: plaster greying at the waterline, cobbles uneven where frost heaved them, a shutter that never quite latches, someone watching from a doorway and then deciding you are not their problem.

## Layout seeds [canon + proposal]

### Raised outcrop (town proper) [canon shape]

The inhabited core sits on the dirt-and-rock rise. Everything below it wants to be wetland again after a hard rain.

**[proposal]** Call the rise **Hard Knoll** in local speech (or leave unnamed until a local says it). Streets are short, crooked, and mostly dirt with cobble only near the hall and the better houses. Alleys dump straight toward soft ground.

### Town hall [canon]

Larger building, steps, oak doors, smokers. Natural first stop for questions.

**[proposal]** Clerk hours are short; the hall doubles as a grain / tax ledger room. Disappearance reports may be incomplete, late, or deliberately thin — discretionary job cuts both ways.

### Guard shack [canon]

Wood + artisanal stone. Stands apart visually.

**[proposal]** Two to four guards on a good day; more concerned with drunks, theft, and road tax than monster patrols into deep bog. They know the town lacks “proper” adventurers. They may resent SorePunks and Chelcins equally as outside trouble.

### Edges into the wet [proposal grounded in arrival text]

- **Wooden fingerposts** through the bog — some point to Halkin-Gaul, some to peat cuts, some to nowhere useful after storms twist them.
- **Peat / reed flats** — flat light, bad footing, good for dumping what should not be found **[table talk]** already floated mud-as-disposal.
- **Tree cover** — denser belts toward the basin rim; sound dies oddly.
- **Squelch roads** — cart-possible in dry spells; nightmare after rain (party already expected this).

### Prior road complication [canon — separate place]

**Blackdoor Inn** murder (stab + sulfur, Vesper) happened *before* the mountains, not inside Halkin-Gaul. Party chatter already notes disappearances-without-trace vs a public stabbing feel like different tools. Treat as:

- possible related pressure on the frontier road, **or**
- a separate problem that stains the approach,

until you decide. Do not silently merge them into one neat mystery.

## Hazards and travel (diegetic)

**[canon]** Overcast, rain, cold, mildew, mud, empty roads on approach, wooden signs in bog.

**[proposal — times]** From the mountain saddle down into the basin: half a day of careful carting in fair weather; longer if a wheel finds a false shelf. Hall → deep reed flat: an hour walk if you know the posts; overnight if you do not. Night travel in the wet is how disappearances stay disappearances.

**[proposal — wildlife / threats]** Leave the *true* monster list blank until you pick a culprit class. Surface threats the town might admit: fever from standing water, lost carts, opportunistic bandits on the stone road, bog sinkholes. Player **[table talk]** list (hag / undead / “swamp people”) stays available as *rumor texture*, not confirmation.

## Session seeds (grounded)

1. **Hall steps.** Pipe circle. Ask about disappearances. Get shrugs, a wrong name, a story that ends mid-sentence. Discretion check: how hard do you push in public?
2. **Guard shack first.** Compare official missing list to what the hall admits. Note the shack’s nicer stone — who paid for it?
3. **Walk the rise edge at dusk.** Count how many houses sit a little too close to soft ground. Find a shuttered place that still has smoke — or no smoke when it should.
4. **Fingerpost survey.** One sign points to a peat cut that locals refuse to name. Camila’s “no warning signs” becomes ironic.
5. **Chelcins shadow.** If any rival adventurer shows up in town, the Blackdoor murder rides with them. Trust is rotten.
6. **Monster complaint vs vanishing.** Someone wants a beast killed in the reeds; someone else wants a quiet search for a cousin. The guild brief said *several* problems — force a priority fight at the table.
