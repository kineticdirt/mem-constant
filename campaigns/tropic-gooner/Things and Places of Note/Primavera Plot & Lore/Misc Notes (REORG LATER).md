## Island Overview

- **Population:** ~780,000
- **Economy:** $28.5 billion GDP (2024), driven by **tourism (38%)**, **technology (15%)**, **agriculture (12%)**, **manufacturing (10%)**, **financial services (8%)**, **healthcare & education (7%)**, **government (6%)**, and **other (4%)**.
- **Cultural Blend:** American, Pacific Islander, Hispanic (including Brazilian), Asian, and more.
- **Climate:** Hot, muggy (75–88°F / 24–31°C; 70–90% humidity) with a **wet season** (May–October) and a **dry season** (November–April).
- **Governance:** Hawaii-like federal authority over the mountain capital and the far northern/northeastern coasts.

# Numbered Regions (1–14)

### I. Middle Bay (Center)

#### 1. **Paradise** (Yellow Highlight – Central City)

- **Overview:**
    - A sun-soaked metropolis that fully embodies the island’s “live for the moment” ethos.
    - Glamorous beach resorts, upscale clubs, and neon-lit boardwalks define its nightlife.
- **Key Features:**
    - Luxury hotels, an emerging tech expo center, and yacht marinas.
    - Tourist slogan: “Live the Dream.”

#### 2. **Porto Lujara** *(alias Porto Lujuria)* (Grey Highlight – Central City)

- **Overview:**
    - Located on the eastern arc of the middle bay, blending commercial shipping with pulsating nightlife.
- **Key Features:**
    - Booming club district, waterfront conventions, and a synergy of finance/tech gatherings.
    - Known for the annual “Carnaval de Lujuria,” featuring flamboyant parades and all-night raves.

#### 3. **Jackedsonville** *(alias Crimson Quay / Crimzon Quay)* (Orange/Red Highlight – Central City)

- **Overview:**
    - Western middle-bay city (vibes.png canonical spelling). Lore sometimes calls it Jackedsonville after a later rebrand attempt.
- **Key Features:**
    - A rowdy casino district, diverse street markets, and risqué adult-themed shows.
    - Hosts “Quay Nights,” an EDM and performance-art festival each spring.

_(Together, Regions 1, 2, and 3 form the bustling **middle bay tri-city** zone.)_

### II. Southwestern & Southern Areas

#### 4. **Villa Miel** (Purple – Southwestern Town)

- **Overview (Older Lore):**
    - Once a quiet fishing outpost, historically famed for its honey production (“Miel”).
- **Key Features:**
    - Eco-friendly beach bungalows, shrimp farming, and the annual Honey Harvest Festival.
    - Maintains a family-friendly vibe, a calmer alternative to the neon cityscapes.

#### 5. **San Aurelio** (Red – Southwest/South City)

- **Overview (Older Lore):**
    - Traditionally a farmland region known for sugarcane and tropical fruits, it has rapidly urbanized into a vibrant city.
- **Key Features:**
    - Rum distilleries and a newly expanded shipping port.
    - Weekend markets still celebrate the area’s agricultural roots despite modern red-brick sprawl.

#### 9. **Ruby Harbor** (Red – Further Southwest/South City)

- **Overview:**
    - A lively coastal hub, sometimes overshadowed by Paradise or Jackedsonville but boasting its own carnival flair.
- **Key Features:**
    - The “Ruby Regatta” draws regional sailors; local seafood markets bustle at dawn.
    - Nightlife merges beach bonfires with colorful boardwalk amusements.

### III. Southeastern Towns

#### 6. **Seaside Springs** (Purple – Southeastern Town, _Older Name Restored_)

- **Overview (Older Lore):**
    - Long recognized for its volcanic hot springs and natural spa retreats.
- **Key Features:**
    - “Spring Soaks” tours of the mineral pools; small fishing cooperatives.
    - Calm evenings with beach bonfires and acoustic performances.

#### 7. **Orchid Falls** (Purple – Southeastern Town, just north of Seaside Springs — **NOT on vibes.png**)

- **Overview:**
    - Elevated slightly above the coastline, providing cooler breezes and scenic overlooks.
- **Key Features:**
    - Lush orchids around local waterfalls; an annual “Orchid Lantern Festival” merges art and nature.
    - A well-maintained road connects it directly to Seaside Springs.

### IV. Mountain Capital & Surroundings

#### 8. **Sierra Dorado** (Purple Highlight w/ Yellow Circle – Capital)

- **Overview:**
    - High in the central mountain range, under federal oversight akin to a U.S. state capital.
- **Key Features:**
    - Governor’s mansion, legislative buildings, cooler climate that favors tech/data centers.
    - Colonial architecture mingles with modern high-rises; day-trip access to mountain trails.

### V. Additional Coastal & Government Zones

#### 10. **Lagooni Seika** *(alias Lagoona Seica)* (City on the East Coast)

- **Overview (Older Lore):**
    - Once a sleepy lagoon outpost, it evolved into a casino hotspot with a cheeky reference to “seca” (“dry”)—ironic given its humid climate.
- **Key Features:**
    - Neon-lit boardwalks, lavish hotels, and a “Seica Sizzle Fest” celebrating local music and beach culture.
    - Strategically placed just above Sierra Dorado on the map’s east side.

#### 11. **Black Sand Beach Preserve** (Volcanic Shoreline)

- **Overview:**
    - A protected coastal stretch with dark volcanic sands.
- **Key Features:**
    - Sea turtle nesting sites, restricted eco-tours, and unique geological formations.
    - Minimal development ensures pristine wildlife habitats.

#### 12. **Nueva Vista** (Historic Quarter — **NOT on vibes.png**)

- **Overview:**
    - The island’s colonial heritage shines in this district, featuring cobblestone plazas and centuries-old cathedrals.
- **Key Features:**
    - Museums, boutique hotels in restored mansions, and cultural festivals that celebrate Spanish-era traditions.
    - A major draw for history buffs and architectural enthusiasts.

#### 13. **Portview** (Marina & Entertainment District)

- **Overview:**
    - A polished waterfront district separate from the middle bay tri-cities, offering a more cosmopolitan ambiance.
- **Key Features:**
    - Upscale restaurants, designer shops, and the “Portview Regatta” each year.
    - Marina facilities cater to yachts and cruise liners, fueling a sophisticated nightlife.

#### 14. **InterFederal Shores** *(alias Federal Shores)* (Northwest Coast Under Gov Control — vibes.png)

- **Overview:**
    - Pristine coastline under strict federal administration, akin to the capital’s Hawaii-like status.
- **Key Features:**
    - Protected coral reefs, wetlands, limited resort development, and a coast guard presence.
- **Connectivity:**
    - Federal roads with restricted access ensure environmental conservation and maritime security.

## Climate & Lifestyle

- **Year-Round Heat:**
    - Temperatures hover between 75–88°F (24–31°C); nights rarely dip below 70°F (21°C).
    - Humidity of 70–90% yields a steamy, Southeast Asian–style environment.
- **Seasons:**
    - **Wet (May–October):** Afternoon downpours, tropical storms.
    - **Dry (November–April):** Cooler evenings, less rain, and clearer skies.
- **Cultural Impact:**
    - Air conditioning is ubiquitous in offices, hotels, and public spaces.
    - Bikinis, board shorts, and breezy attire are the norm, reflecting the relaxed, often hedonistic atmosphere.
    - Siestas are common during peak heat; nightlife thrives in cooler twilight hours.
