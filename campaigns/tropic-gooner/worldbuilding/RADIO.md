## The dial

Every hotel lobby in Paradise has one on behind the front desk, every taxi out of the Porto cruise gate has one on the dash, and every bar on the Quay has one over the ice well with the volume knob that only goes down. The island's FM band runs 88–108 like anywhere in the states, and the county licenses it out of Sierra Dorado the way it licenses everything: slowly, and with a preference for people who don't make problems [proposal].

Six stations matter. The rest of the band is hotel weather loops, a sermon station that only comes in on the east side, and static.

---

## 93.5 KPRD — "The Dream" (Paradise, r01)

- **Format:** exotica and resort-lounge for the hotel strip — tiki-bar instrumentals, bossa-tinged lobby covers of salsa records, the occasional string arrangement of a song that was alive twenty years ago. Nothing with a tempo a buffet line couldn't keep up with.
- **Voice:** **Dee Amaya**, mornings [proposal face] — a voice like the chilled glass the music is drinking out of. She has read the same cruise-arrival list every weekday for nine years and has never once sounded surprised by it.
- **Reporting:** beach weather and surf flags, cruise arrivals and departure delays, gala-season social notes read off the society page, valet-lane traffic. Buffet prices when they change, which is the closest the station gets to investigative work.
- **Refuses:** anything that happens above floor 14, anything involving a gray van with a tiny barcode, the word "incident." A medical event at the marina gets reported as a traffic note, if at all [canon texture: ordinance 44-B, Stevens vans, the discretion clause].
- **Quietly belongs to:** the advertisers — and the two biggest are CiDance and the gala calendar the Visibility Board manages [proposal]. Nobody at the station thinks of that as ownership. That is how ownership works.

## 94.5 KLJR — "La Dura" (Porto Lujara, r02)

- **Format:** salsa dura and bolero out of the Porto warehouses — Fania-era hard salsa, son cubano, bolero after midnight, and on Sundays the brass rehearsal tapes when a float barn will lend them.
- **Voice:** **Tito Balaguer** [proposal face] — a retired dock checker who reads the shipping news the way he used to count crates: out loud, with the totals.
- **Reporting:** the port report — vessel arrivals, berth numbers, pilot-boat schedules, tide and draft. Union-hall notices. The Carnaval committee's minutes read in full, which makes La Dura the only press on the island covering the committee's other fifty-one weeks. Fish prices at the dawn market.
- **Refuses:** nothing, officially. Unofficially, its Night Ledger stories are always slightly wrong — the right name, the wrong address — and nobody on Ledger Row has ever once called in a correction [proposal, built on the canon Night Ledger forecast trade].
- **Quietly belongs to:** nobody. It is the only station on the dial nobody owns, which is why the docks trust it and why the hills sales office advertises somewhere else [proposal].

## 97.9 KQAY — "The Quay" (Jackedsonville / Crimson Quay, r03)

- **Format:** EDM out of the Quay — house and trance blocks overnight, the spring Quay Nights sets simulcast live [canon event], and vaporwave and chopped lounge in the hungover hours between the dawn seafood market and noon.
- **Voice:** **"DJ Overtime"** [proposal face] — nobody at the station uses another name; a former club kid who somehow ended up with the morning slot and reads the police log in the same flat voice he once used to announce drink specials.
- **Reporting:** the club calendar, cover charges, festival weeks, and the **blotter** — a sanitized police log read absolutely straight: times, blocks, one flat sentence each, no names the county didn't already print. It is the funniest ten minutes on the island and nobody involved laughs on air.
- **Refuses:** slot-odds stories, tribute stories, anything with the word "Tithe" in it. During Quay Nights, a power cut is reported as "a brief intermission" [canon texture: the licensed-club truce is a light-based organism].
- **Quietly belongs to:** the ad book — and Quay Rojo buys the way it protects: steadily, and in cash [proposal]. The station calls them community sponsors. Everybody knows the cadence.

## 101.3 KHUM — "The Hum" (island-wide, late night)

- **Format:** talk radio, 10 p.m. to 4 a.m., where every caller has a theory about the Dry Contract — the east-side humidity, what the gray vans carry, why the cruise ships changed their horn pattern the year the CRT was founded, Object 14 [canon anchors: the Dry Contract is a locked plot engine; Object 14 is the Ash List's standing meme]. Music only as bumper beds: drone and static-adjacent ambient, which the regular callers insist is part of the signal.
- **Voice:** the host answers to **"Marlow"** [proposal face] — patient, dry, never laughs on air, ends every call with "could be," which the regulars say in their sleep.
- **Reporting:** one topic of the night, announced at 10:00 sharp and exhausted by 3:30. The callers supply the evidence, such as it is. Once a month a caller with an unnervingly professional voice reads a correction to something nobody remembers being said.
- **Refuses:** nothing, and that is the problem — Coral Trace LLP keeps a standing tape delay on the show, and the show has never acknowledged it exists. The delay is seven seconds. Regular callers have learned to count it [proposal; Coral Trace's 48-hour NDA machine is canon].
- **Quietly belongs to:** nobody knows. The transmitter lease is a shell company on Ledger Row, which means the answer is either Night Ledger or someone paying Night Ledger to not know [proposal]. The Ash List mirrors the good episodes [canon habit of the List, applied].

## 89.1 KSDA — "Radio Sierra" (Sierra Dorado, r08)

- **Format:** the federal voice — big-band swing and American standards from the postwar migration wave [canon history], orchestral blocks on Sundays, and the occasional military-band recording that means a hurricane is coming.
- **Voice:** **Annabelle Frost** [proposal face] — a civil-service alto with perfect diction who has read the news on this frequency since before the CRT existed, and pronounces "Jackedsonville" like it is a foreign capital.
- **Reporting:** straight news, the island's only straight news — federal bulletins, county commission summaries, mountain-pass road closures, official weather, official anything. When Radio Sierra and the street disagree, Radio Sierra is usually the last to know and the first to be believed.
- **Refuses:** editorializing, call-ins, and the phrase "Crimson Tithe," which has never been said on this frequency. It has also never once reported on the humidity [proposal, consistent with the canon lock that the Dry Contract is real but not official].
- **Quietly belongs to:** the federal government, openly. It is the only station that will tell you who owns it, which is why half the island believes it and the other half leaves it on for the music [proposal].

## 105.5 KRBY — "The Harbor" (Ruby Harbor, r09)

- **Format:** boat radio — son cubano oldies and AM gold, the records the harbor's parents danced to, requests called in from anyone with a radio and a reason.
- **Voice:** **Old Man Reyes** [proposal face] — no relation to the Ash List's Marco, and he will tell you so on air; a semi-retired fisherman who reads the sea report like scripture and dedications like confession.
- **Reporting:** sea conditions, tide tables, small-craft advisories, the lost-and-found (one outboard engine, one wedding ring, one goat), and the church-hall calendar. The only island news it carries is weather.
- **Refuses:** politics, the tri-city, and anything that happened after about 1994.
- **Quietly belongs to:** the man who owns the antenna, who is Old Man Reyes. It is the only honest ownership structure on the dial [proposal].

## Between stations

The gaps — below 89, and a stretch around 96 — are static on purpose [proposal]: the county licenses those parts of the band to nobody, and the hotel radios pick up the humidity instead. In product terms, a station with no stream (`stream_status: "none"` in `stations.json`) renders as static in the player. La Dura and The Harbor are the two stations you'd actually want that you can't get off-island, which is its own kind of realism.
