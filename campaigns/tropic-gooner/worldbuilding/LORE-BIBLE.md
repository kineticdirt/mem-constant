## Island overview

Isla Primavera is an island state in the warm water between everywhere, Hawaii by way of Havana: pastel colonial streets going chalky in the sun, mid-century resort towers behind them, old American sedans held together by stubborn mechanics, and a jungle spine down the middle that the towns politely ignore. About 780,000 people live here. Another 2.8 million a year come through to misbehave for 6.5 days on average (Misc Notes figures, tagged 2024 there; see the year problem in Open questions). Tourism is the biggest slice of the economy by far, then a niche tech sector, sugar and rum agriculture, manufacturing, and the financial offices that wash it all. Paradisio County is the part that sells the postcards: three cities around the middle bay, Paradise and Porto Lujara and Jackedsonville, and most of the money moves through that water. The federal government keeps the mountain capital, Sierra Dorado, and the far northern coasts for itself, along with Base Fuerte Luna up in the interior.

The short history. The island was a Pacific Trading Company colony first: sugar plantations and a privateer port, worked mostly by indentured and enslaved labor imported from East and South Asia because that was the cheaper haul, with enslaved Africans present in smaller numbers. That labor stack is why the island's blood is what it is: mixed-heritage plurality, then white American, then Filipino, Japanese, Chinese, Vietnamese, Latino including Brazilian, a small native Pacific Islander remainder. It came out from under the company around the American independence era and spent the next century as a minor Pacific coaling-and-cargo stop. The Spanish-American War era brought the American question to a vote; the island voted overwhelmingly for statehood, a faction of independence holdouts disagreed, and the result was the Statehood War, about a year and a half of two U.S. divisions methodically losing patience with resistance pockets in the far south and far north. Statehood followed about a year later. Paradise, the old colonial main city, was stripped of the capital role as punishment or prudence, and a new administrative seat was cut into the highlands at Sierra Dorado. The twentieth century made the tri-city ports what they are, took a heavy Japanese bombing in WW2, and came out of it with a military buildup and a mainland migration wave of former servicemen who liked the weather and stayed. A small tech sector grew up on the back of the ports — sensor and chip fabs, robotics, maritime work. Then tourism ate the law. Vice got licensed, zoned, and priced, and the county learned to sell freedom by the week.

What lurks: the workspace rule for this island is that humanoid society won. Vampires, werewolves, and the rest got pushed to the fringes, and on Primavera the fringe is the night economy. Kindred hold the marina galas in Paradise and tax the casino alleys in Jackedsonville. A fae court harvests glamour off the Lagooni Seika boardwalk. A Garou pack runs an eco-NGO out of the Black Sand preserve and fights a quiet war against condo money. A fertility cult hides in plain sight as the Villa Miel harvest festival committee, and the GM has flagged that one as a foreground arc, not background color. Something is wrong with the humidity on the east side and the group that knows calls it the Dry Contract; that one is locked as a real plot engine, not a rumor to be debunked. Hunters end up here because the island advertises appetite, appetite leaves bodies, and the bodies get sanitized by a licensed contractor before the cruise passengers wake up. The Imbued are the people who notice the seam in the carpet. Sasha lives in Paradise. She noticed.

## r01 — Paradise

The north shore of the middle bay, North Central in county records, and the picture on the brochure. "Live the Dream," says the slogan, and the city is built to make that sentence load-bearing: luxury towers, a yacht marina, a neon boardwalk, a tech expo center the boosters keep calling emerging. Sasha's home base, by locked decision. This is the city that performs joy for a living and bills it by the night.

**Districts** (the pilot subzone set, canon as data, with my texture on top):

- **The Marina / Gilded Anchor** — yacht slips, the philanthropy calendar, and the yacht club that gives the Kindred court its name. Charity galas function as Elysium. Elder Marisol del Castillo is never seen eating in public, and people who find that funny stop finding it funny.
- **The Boardwalk strip** — the tourist face. Galaxy Fitness and the Java Jump flagship anchor the local end; licensed buskers, sunscreen, buffet steam drifting off the hotel side. CRT festival crates sit staged near the substation, visible on purpose.
- **The CRT substation block** — black-and-coral trucks, riot gear that never quite goes home, sonic drills on festival weeks. The gear is surplus and the doctrine is optics: clear the frame for the cameras.
- **PIU South edge** — the private university's hospitality program. Evening classes, because the students staff the hotel fronts by day. Intern labor is the load-bearing wall of the service economy here.
- **The CiDance tower district** — the flagship. Famous cheap lunch buffet that locals eat without shame; floor 14 and up is companion-package territory under the discretion clause, and the county can't see the guest list without a warrant. Table 6 at the buffet belongs to the Lunch Regulars, retired cops and fixers who trade information, not merchandise.

**Who runs what:** CiDance owns the tower district's silence. The Gilded Anchor owns the marina after dark. The CRT owns the street surface, selectively. The Visibility Board's donors own the question of which blocks get filmed. Stevens & Co. owns whatever happened, twenty minutes after it happens. [canon, all of it; the sentence structure is mine]

**Street texture:**

- Mornings smell like salt and espresso; the Java Jump line is white-collar and the deals are audible if you sit close.
- Afternoons go slow in the heat (the siesta is real) and the evenings come back loud.
- At night the marina rigging ticks in the wind and the gala crowds move between the club and the tower with the confidence of people whose incidents have a cleanup contract.
- A local stereotype: the Paradise native works three jobs selling relaxation and hasn't personally relaxed in years. Another: everyone claims a cousin who was "in the towers" the night something happened, and nobody says what.

**Hooks:**

1. A Stevens van is already parked at the marina before anyone places the medical call. Who tipped them, and what were they early for? (encounter deck r1-03)
2. The yacht-club RSVP list carries a name that died in county news years ago. Clerical error, or the guest is still attending? (r1-02)
3. A handbill in the Java Jump bathroom advertises a "sunrise gratitude circle" with no address. (r1-04)
4. A CRT festival crate turns up with a broken inventory seal, and a week later somebody's block party has a sonic cannon. (r1-04 is the flyer, r1-01 is the crate; keep them separate at the table)
5. A Crimson Tithe collector came north into marina parking once and left with a broken wrist, courtesy of an Anchor ghoul. Both courts pretended it didn't happen. Sasha heard about it before Jackedsonville did. [canon incident, date withheld pending the year question]

## r02 — Porto Lujara

The south curve of the bay, South Central in the records, and the oldest-feeling of the three cities, colonial bones under the neon. Porto Lujara is the working port that parties: commercial shipping and the cruise-comps trade by day, a club district and waterfront conventions by night, and once a year the Carnaval de Lujuria, when the county allows a brief window of public display before the sweep. If Paradise sells the dream, Porto Lujara loads it onto trucks.

**Districts** [district names are proposal; the anchor places are canon]:

- **The Carnaval Route** — the waterfront parade street. Vendor stalls, float barns, brass rehearsals for months beforehand. After the parade, the CRT runs the sweep and the route goes back to being a road.
- **Lujara Docks** — cargo and party-cruise unload, side by side. IFC cold-chain distribution sits near the port. Crate counts don't always match the manifest, and the people who notice get paid to stop noticing.
- **Ledger Row** — a fluorescent office strip of club-book accountants, union halls, and chandlers. Night Ledger keeps its offices here: off-books ledgers for half the nightlife on the island, and an uncanny record of CRT overtime before Carnaval.
- **The Annex hill** — the Coastal Community College culinary annex and the student quarter around it. Cheap food, catering gigs, kids hauling trays into suites that aren't on any tourist map.
- **The Porto hills** — construction. "Green condos," says the Development Consortium's signage, going up over bought-out abuelas. The money is Wyrm-tainted if you ask the right people, and the soil studies never seem to publish.

**Who runs what:** the dock foremen run the manifests. Night Ledger runs the arithmetic of everyone else's vice. The CRT runs the post-Carnaval sweep calendar; Mercado's overtime sheet exists and circulates. The Development Consortium runs the hills. The Carnaval committee runs exactly one week of the year and spends the other fifty-one negotiating about it.

**Street texture:**

- Fried dough, diesel, and salt air on the waterfront; brass from a float rehearsal two streets over.
- Dock coffee at four in the morning, cruise horns at six, club music still leaking out at seven.
- The culinary students move in packs with knife rolls and look exhausted in a way that's mostly theater and partly real.
- A Porto stereotype: locals can read a tourist's budget off the cruise lanyard color. Another: everyone has an aunt who sews parade costumes and an uncle who knows what's actually in the barrels.

**Hooks:**

1. The morning after Carnaval, one float is still sealed behind the docks. The route was cleared. The float wasn't on it. (r2-01)
2. Night Ledger's overtime sheet predicts a raid on a named club. The window passes. Nothing happens. Somebody paid the forecast, and the club's books are suddenly very clean. (r2-02)
3. A catering class gets an order: trays to a suite, no guest name, tip already paid in cash. One of the students is somebody's cousin. (r2-04)
4. The hills sales office keeps closing on properties whose elderly owners then leave the island entirely. The Consortium's environmental report is always "forthcoming."
5. Carnaval week is the one stretch of the year when the county looks the other way on purpose. Everyone with a sin to schedule knows it, books for it, and pays extra for it. [proposal texture, built on canon vice law]

## r03 — Jackedsonville (Crimson Quay)

West of the bay, West Central in the records, and the least filtered of the three. The casinos came first and the rest of the city grew around the carpets: the rowdy gaming district, the spring festival, the street markets, the shows the tourism board describes as "adult-themed" with a straight face. Locals call the harbor side the Quay and always have; the 2014 campaign to rebrand the whole city as Crimson Quay died in committee, but the waterfront kept the name, which is why the region wears it now. Jackedsonville is where the island's semi-legal stack is most visible from the street, and where the street is most aware of it.

**Districts** [names proposal; venues canon]:

- **The Row** — the casino strip: the Crimson Oasis with its polished floor and no-records VIP lounge, the Red Fortune with themed nights and irregular-betting rumors, and back rooms inside both that you enter by being known.
- **The Quay** — the harborfront club district. Neon Flux is the anchor room, and every spring Quay Nights fills it and the street behind it: EDM, performance art, and a licensed-club truce that holds exactly as long as the lights stay on.
- **The alleys** — speakeasies with unmarked doors, cheap live music, and the Renta-Girl cluster: a licensed outcall shell on paper, an unlicensed roster in practice, and a permanent spot on Stevens' priority-client list.
- **Main Street market** — daytime. Fusion stalls, mango salsa, ceviche, the smell of the grill over everything. Mar de Sabores does the catch of the day for people who sit down; Bodega Roja does sandwiches under digital billboards for people who don't.
- **The Book Nook block** — a gray strip of legal retail whose screens drift into stranger tabs after dark. Nelly's bookstore is here: late hours, research rabbit holes, and a jar of "true crime" tips from customers that is less funny than it sounds.
- **Stadium edge** — the multi-purpose stadium and the sports clubs around it. The gangs of this island sponsor clubs and scholarships the way old duchies sponsored churches, and Jackedsonville is where that habit is thickest.

**Who runs what:** the shareholders run the Row. Quay Rojo runs slot-fraud, protection, and the unlicensed margins, and pays tribute upward to the Crimson Tithe, the Anarch-flavored court that taxes the night and wants it loud. Their envoy answers to "Kingside," speaks for a collective, and has never claimed to be a prince. The CRT's posture here is Tier 2: stand outside, let the owner handle the interior, unless something goes viral. Rough Ride, the security-and-investigation firm that grew out of the old gangs, protects the largest legitimate money on the island, and people who move against them have a way of disappearing for a while.

**Street texture:**

- Ozone from the fog machines, sugared drinks, cigarette heat in the alley behind the club.
- Slot-floor carpet smell, which is its own category, and neon spill when the doors open between sets.
- Dawn seafood market running while the night crowd is still finding its shoes.
- Stereotype: nobody in Jackedsonville says "the casino." They say the carpet's name, and they can tell you the carpet's mood this month. Another: a Quay local either deals, books odds, or knows a guy — often all three by thirty.

**Hooks:**

1. Quay Nights, capacity crowd, and the power drops mid-set. The licensed-club truce is a light-based organism. Quay Rojo's collectors are already moving before the generators kick.
2. A customer returns a book to the Book Nook with a tip inside that reads like a crazed marginal note, except it maps, stop for stop, a Tithe collection route.
3. The alley's skim comes up short the same week the Tithe raises its tax. The collectors and the taxed both want the discrepancy found first.
4. A whistleblower with hotel incident photos hires Rough Ride for protection, not knowing what Rough Ride does to problems. [ties the canon gangs file to the locked taste decision that CiDance hunts whistleblowers]
5. A CRT wrong-door stack hits the tenement next to a licensed club. The club's patrons watch from the balcony with drinks. Someone films the wrong part.

## Aesthetic and naming guide

For anyone generating content (prose, NPCs, or procedural buildings) the island has a specific look, and it's cheaper to stay on-model than to fix it later.

**The visual language** is Hawaii filtered through Cuba. The old quarters are pastel colonial: two-to-four-story facades in seafoam, coral pink, butter yellow, mint; paint chalking and peeling in the salt air; colonnades and arcades at street level; hurricane shutters; clay tile and corrugated tin roofs; interior courtyards you can smell (wet stone, laundry, someone's lunch). Behind and above that, the American century: mid-century concrete resort towers, glassy and a little tired, tiki kitsch that went corporate decades ago. Sierra Dorado does civic block and colonial mansion side by side, mountain-cool. The docks do corrugated warehouse and crane. Nothing on the island looks new for long; the humidity eats finishes, and maintenance budgets are a fiction in some districts and a religion in others.

**Street furniture and machines:** 1950s American land yachts kept alive by gifted mechanics, 1980s Japanese imports, scooters, resort shuttles. Two vehicles to learn: the unmarked gray Stevens van with the tiny barcode (the barcode exists because their lawyers decided logos are subpoena bait), and the black-and-coral CRT truck (the stripe exists because a PR firm focus-grouped "local pride" after gray trucks polled as occupation). Both facts are canon, and both are the kind of detail this setting runs on.

**Sound and smell:** son cubano and bolero out of old-quarter windows, reggaeton out of car doors, EDM out of the Quay, hotel-lobby covers of all of it. Rum and coffee. Grill smoke, mango, ceviche, mochi donuts at the DingLing, buffet steam at the CiDance, diesel and fried dough on the Porto waterfront, fog-machine ozone in Jackedsonville. Heat 75–88°F year-round, humidity 70–90%, wet season May through October, dry November through April. Air conditioning is infrastructure. Clothing is minimal and nobody pretends otherwise.

**Signage:** hand-painted wall ads for rum and the lottery in the old towns; neon script on the Row; bilingual English/Spanish as the default, with Tagalog, Vietnamese, and Creole hand-lettering in the markets. One pipeline rule carries over from the map work and applies to any generated imagery: **keep text in a UI layer, never bake it into the art.** AI-melted lettering is the fastest way to break the illusion.

**Names.** Spanish colonial saints and landforms for the older layer: San Aurelio, Sierra Dorado, Villa Miel, Porto Lujara. English booster optimism for the American-era layer: Paradise, Ruby Harbor, Seaside Springs, Portview, East Bayby. American joke names that stuck: Jackedsonville, Puckall. Phonetic creole portmanteaus: Lagooni Seika, where the joke is that "seca" means dry and nothing there is. Corporate names are blunt mainland English (CiDance, Java Jump, Galaxy Fitness, Dick's Grocery, RG's) because the money is. People carry the labor history: Filipino, Japanese, Chinese, Vietnamese, Latino, and Anglo surnames in roughly that order of everyday frequency, with Creole first names threaded through the old families. [The naming-pattern rules are proposal extrapolated from canon names and canon demographics; the canon names themselves are locked by vibes.png.] **Street names and block businesses** for the tri-city focus follow the same mix — see **`STREETS.md`** (Live Dream Boulevard, Ledger Row, Quay Street / Bench Street, etc.; most streets [proposal] until promoted).

**Old money** [proposal]: a handful of planter-descended families still hold marina land, rum brands, and bank seats, and the gala circuit is their living room. One recurring whisper: the Anchor elder's surname, del Castillo, appears in colonial land grants that predate the marina by two centuries. Nobody brings it up at the galas.

## Timeline and calendar

Broad strokes. Exact colonial dates were never pinned in the source material and I haven't invented any.

- **Colonial era** — Pacific Trading Company possession. Sugar plantations, privateer port, indentured and enslaved labor drawn mostly from East and South Asia.
- **Post-American-independence era** — the island is cut loose, becomes a minor Pacific trade stop.
- **1800s** — a century of coaling, cargo, and quiet.
- **Spanish-American War era** — statehood vote passes overwhelmingly; holdouts launch the Statehood War; ~1.5 years, two U.S. divisions, resistance pockets in the far south and north crushed. Statehood follows about a year later. Paradise loses the capital; Sierra Dorado is built in the highlands.
- **1940s** — heavy Japanese bombing in WW2; the military buildup after it expands the ports and plants Base Fuerte Luna in the northern interior.
- **Postwar** — mainlanders and former servicemen flood in; the tri-city ports boom; the population roughly becomes today's mix.
- **Late twentieth century** — the niche tech sector: sensor and chip fabs, robotics, maritime systems.
- **1998–2010s** — CiDance rises (canon); the Safe Paradise Initiative forms and becomes the Visibility Board (canon); the Gilded Anchor elder arrives with the 2008 marina luxury money (canon); the 2014 Crimson Quay rebrand fails and the Tithe formalizes its street tax the same year (canon); a viral alley clip nearly costs the county a cruise contract (canon).
- **2016** — county ordinance 44-B shields hotel guest lists; the county contracts Stevens & Co. after two norovirus cover-ups fail (both canon).
- **2017** — the CRT is created after a single tourist riot on the Paradise boardwalk (canon).
- **2019** — **the present.** Locked: `wb-tg-date`. Stevens logs its first non-mortal cleanup at the yacht club (canon, dated 2019-08-12 in the registry) — if the table starts before August, that hasn't happened yet.

**The calendar problem, plainly:** the Tier-A org deep dives keep writing past 2019 — a cruise collapse in 2020, the Ash List rising in 2022, a Carnaval sonic-cannon mis-aim in 2024, incidents stamped 2025. The Discord RP scenes are dated March–May 2025, but those are table-time (when the posts were made), not necessarily world-time. Either the org chronology needs to slide back to fit 2019, or the 2019 lock is stale and the present is really 2025. This is Open question 1, and it is the one to answer first, because every org's "present" section depends on it.

**The sim clock:** for the map product, the soft target is **1 world day ≈ 48 hours IRL**, tunable, set in `map/diegetic-clock.json`. That's platform pacing, not physics, and it's separate from the diegetic year question above.

## Open questions for the GM

1. **What year is it, really?** The lock says 2019; the org deep dives run through 2025. Options: (a) keep 2019 and slide every org date back roughly six years — tedious but clean; (b) declare the present 2025 and retire the 2019 answer as stale — the registry just works; (c) keep 2019 as the calendar year and treat the org "present" sections as near-future rumor the table grows into. **This gates almost everything else.**
2. **Bay geometry.** `map.json` notes read Paradise north of the middle bay, Porto Lujara at the south tip, Jackedsonville west; `Laguna of the Isle` puts Paradise west and Jackedsonville east; the Paradisio Overview's county-records names (North/West/South Central) disagree with both in places. Options: (a) adopt the map.json notes (N / S / W) — closest to the art; (b) rewrite the map notes to match Laguna; (c) leave the compass fuzzy on purpose.
3. **Region chip labels.** The new convention makes the regions Lujuria and Crimson Quay, with Porto Lujara and Jackedsonville as the cities inside them — but `regions-ui.json` still displays the city names. Options: (a) relabel the two region chips to Lujuria / Crimson Quay; (b) keep city names on the chips and let the region names live in lore only; (c) show both, "Crimson Quay — Jackedsonville."
4. **One festival or two?** The vault gives Jackedsonville "Quay Nights" (spring, EDM/performance art) and also a "Lights & Lust Festival" (multi-day, EDM + burlesque + daytime carnival). Options: (a) same event — Quay Nights is what locals call it, Lights & Lust is the tourist-board brand; (b) two separate events on the calendar; (c) cut Lights & Lust as a dead draft.
5. **Who pushed the 2014 rebrand?** Canon says the attempt to rename Jackedsonville "Crimson Quay" failed and the Tithe formalized its tax that year. Options: (a) Kindred-driven from the start; (b) a genuine chamber-of-commerce campaign the Tithe rode; (c) both — mortal money fronting, Kindred money behind.
6. **Confirm the Book Nook's city.** `places-directory.md` puts Nelly's bookstore in Jackedsonville; the chronicle-history gap list asked for confirmation. Options: (a) Jackedsonville, Book Nook block — as written here; (b) Paradise boardwalk-adjacent; (c) Porto Lujara student quarter.
7. **The serial killer's nature.** Locked as a background thread until elevated. When it moves, options: (a) mundane predator the county keeps reclassifying; (b) supernatural and tied to an existing court; (c) a false lead that burns the party's time and teaches the island's real lesson.
8. **How much does the CRT know?** Options: (a) nothing — gear and overtime, no occult brief; (b) rumors at sergeant level, denial upward; (c) one quiet federal memo in a safe in Sierra Dorado.
9. **Pin the Statehood War.** The vault ties it to the Spanish-American War era without dates. Options: (a) lock 1898–1900; (b) leave it dateless "turn of the century"; (c) slide it later for a WW1-adjacent flavor.

## How to use this bible

- **What's canon vs proposal** is marked inline per the Canon status section. When in doubt, treat anything without a source file behind it as a proposal.
