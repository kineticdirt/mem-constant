# Isla Primavera — society: law, norms, and how people actually behave

**Status:** GM notebook draft, 2026-08-05. Companion to `LORE-BIBLE.md` / `GROUPS.md` / `STORIES.md`. Same grading: unmarked claims trace to SoT, **[proposal]** is mine, GM calls at the end.

## 1. Law

**The vice stack** (canon; full version in `island-vice-and-enforcement.md`): licensed and private is tolerated, unlicensed and visible is the crime, tourist corridors add a display rule. Play-relevant pieces: companion licenses, the house forty percent on club-attached work, CiDance floor 14+, drug tiers (private use is paperwork, street dealing is CRT), ordinance 44-B shielding hotel guest lists (CiDance's lawyers wrote it). The engine is the visibility tariff: sin is wholesale; what the county retails is certainty that none reaches a cruise passenger's feed.

Enforcement has three accents:

- **Paradise: cosmetics.** CRT Tier 1 is crowd-shaping for cameras; Tier 3 never touches a CiDance tower (canon). The law's real venue is the licensing hearing.
- **Porto Lujara: the calendar.** Raids are scheduled, and Night Ledger forecasts them like weather; a missed forecast is news (STORIES 5). Carnaval is the one licensed week of public display, then the sweep.
- **Jackedsonville: real.** Tier 2 means the van waits outside while the owner handles the interior. Tier 3 means a wrong-door stack in a poor block, and the Ash List counts the clips.

**Gun law** [proposal; feeds the gunplay sim]: strict on paper: county permit, may-issue, justification letter, non-resident fees triple. Open carry is banned in practice; a visible pistol in a tourist zone triggers Tier 1 by itself. Armed-security licenses exist, and Rough Ride holds a drawerful, which is how the old gangs got their gun rights back. Off-paper supply: dockside pistols in Porto (manifest arithmetic, one crate short), alley pieces in Jackedsonville (Quay Rojo keeps prices high; cheap iron is bad for everyone's business), almost nothing in Paradise; a gun charge there is the one crime the county prosecutes with enthusiasm.

**Courts and jails** [proposal]: a county lockup per city, weekend processing delays as pressure, and Coral Trace's 48-hour NDA-and-release pipeline for anyone whose employer can pay. Felonies climb to Sierra Dorado: slower, colder, cleaner-ish. Federal layer: reef stations on InterFederal Shores, MPs at Fuerte Luna (CRT avoids them, canon), capitol police (files, not flashbangs). Corruption is dry: nobody slides a judge an envelope. What's priced is scheduling: licensing queues, evidence logistics, who gets swept, who's pre-warned, whose block gets filmed.

## 2. Norms

- **Tourist/local boundary.** On shift, the tourist may be rude and the local may not; that asymmetry is the job. Off shift it inverts, and a tourist who's rude in a local bar at 1 a.m. has mistaken the island for the brochure. One safe channel: complain about anything except the cook.
- **Hospitality.** Greet the room when you enter; accept the coffee. Refusing food is a small insult. The auntie network is the real background check; one call outranks a reference letter.
- **Machismo, local variant** [proposal]: less chest-beating, more solvency performance. Status is paying the round, fixing the cousin's car, covering the funeral. The unforgivable insults are "cheap" and anything about a sister; "coward" is for tourists. Fights climb a ladder: posture, audience, the hold-me-back beat, one swing, then an elder mediates and somebody buys the round. Outsiders skip the ladder: police, or the alley, depending on the hour.
- **Family and church.** Catholic bones, evangelical growth, festival syncretism. Church committees control permits for anything with chairs and a PA. Grandmother approval is a zoning category in practice.
- **Tipping and the other economy.** Tips are wages; stiff the server and the room files it. Gray payments are never called bribes: an expedite fee, a donation to the right festival booth, a fixer's invoice marked consulting. Never hand cash to a uniform; Night Ledger's whole industry exists to launder that gesture into respectability.
- **What you don't ask about.** Who a kid's other parent is, if it isn't on the mailbox. What a server does on her phone between shifts (everyone knows, nobody says; the screen rule is a social rule too). Who was at the marina last night. Anything with a date from the war years [proposal].

## 3. Psychology

- **The service mask.** Performed ease is the island's core professional skill. To the wearer it isn't hypocrisy, it's craft; pride attaches to doing it well. The local read is whether the mask drops after shift; never dropping it burns out, never wearing one doesn't last a season.
- **Time.** Island time is a class instrument, not laziness. Tourists rush because they paid for six and a half days; locals are late on purpose, because arriving on someone else's clock concedes something. The four o'clock rain is the only appointment everyone keeps.
- **Rumor velocity.** Rumor outruns the police radio and is more accurate about everything except numbers: table 6, the dock net, the group chats. The Ash List exists because rumor eventually wanted footnotes.
- **Class signals.** Not cars; everyone's car is old and someone's cousin keeps it running. Real signals: AC quality, dental work, a mainland degree, whether you glance at prices at the CiDance buffet, linen that survives the humidity. Old money is land and surnames. New money is condos, suspected accordingly.
- **Expats and retirees.** Came for cheap sun, stayed to defend "their" island from change; the condo association is their parliament. Once they perceive the vice stack: moral exit, or full conversion and a permanent stool somewhere with a view.
- **The cruise mind.** Six and a half days of permission, budget legible in the lanyard color (Porto dockworkers read it at a glance). A bad cruise season feels like drought; locals resent the dependency and the tourists in one breath.
- **The ten-second read.** Shoes, sunburn stage, watch tan-line, tip timing, whether you greet the room. Newcomers fail by being too watchful; the island's other predators fail by being too smooth [proposal, playable].

## 4. Men and women

From the labor stack: women run hospitality front-of-house, nursing, teaching, retail, and the creator economy; men run docks, construction, fishing, security, and driving; kitchens are mixed and always were (canon: the island's best-known ex-marine is a cook). The counting rooms skew female: club books, co-op rosters, ledger clerks. Local stereotype: women hold the money, men hold the boats. Two-thirds true; the third is the interesting part.

**Dating.** Local courtship is public and family-visible: sharing a buffet table reaches three aunts by evening. Tourist dating is app-fast (canon: the pairing apps and their mis-pairs are literally scavenger weather for the Crimson Tithe). The goodbye is priced in from the first drink; forgetting that is a recognized local genre of heartbreak.

**Street reality.** The harassment gradient tracks tourist density: worst on the boardwalk and the Quay at festival hours, where drunk strangers outnumber consequences and the display rule protects cameras more reliably than women. This is the locked noncon-institution theme at street level: texture and consequence, not decoration. The countermeasures are mature: pairs after shift, the known-safe-bars list, bartenders with a code word and a cab number, the co-op's own door arrangements. The CRT's absence from that list is the finding.

**Machismo's bill.** Provider pride on service wages produces the quiet debt economy: the round you can't skip, the sister's tuition, the boat repair, borrowed from no-paperwork lenders at Quay Rojo-adjacent rates. Enlistment is the respectable pressure valve (canon: Ellaine and Toga both took it); it exports the problem for eight years and sometimes brings it home with a pension. The shame of being seen as cheap bankrupts more men than the interest does.

**Older women.** Institutional power, full stop. Grandmothers run family vetting, church committees run the permit economy, and Villa Miel's harvest committee is the south's most powerful civic body, which is why something hides inside it (canon: the Choir; most committee ladies are just committee ladies, which is the cover working as designed). In the Porto hills every buyout routes through an abuela; the informal titles live with them. The Consortium hiring smooth grandsons to do the asking is the whole gentrification story in one move.

**Queer life** [proposal, built from the island's own logic]: the visibility tariff applies to identity the same way it applies to vice. Open where licensed, lit, and revenue-positive: the Quay's clubs, festival weeks, long stretches of Paradise's service class. Quiet where the money is older: church towns, the docks, family businesses. Nightlife made gender presentation more negotiable than the mainland average decades early, and queer locals read enforcement posture the way fishermen read weather; they had to learn first.

## 5. Housing

- **Ownership.** Old families hold land, mainland money holds condos, and a wide middle holds family homes with titles that wouldn't survive an audit, which is why audits are unpopular.
- **Paradise:** you don't live in Paradise, you staff it. Worker dorms behind the towers, hot-bedding in high season, the dawn bus from where people actually live. Staff housing comes out of wages at rates that would scandalize anywhere workers could afford lawyers.
- **Porto Lujara:** port-family tenements, abuela-owned, stairs that know your name. The cheapest real life in the tri-city, and the hills above are where the buyout letters go.
- **Jackedsonville:** casino-adjacent walk-ups and rooms by the week. Downstairs is always a business, and the rent tracks the festival calendar like a tide.
- **Rent, in story terms** (no dollar figures until the GM wants them): a gym trainer's commissions cover a shared room, barely (canon: Ellaine's math). One good cruise week's tips pay a month of a Porto tenement. One night in a floor-14 suite costs three months of the same room. A dock foreman owns his mother's house in installments owed to his siblings.
- **The money loop** [proposal, culturally load-bearing]: locals crew the same cruise ships that dock at Lujara and send pay home to the port that serves them; balikbayan boxes arrive from mainland relatives; allotments flow off Fuerte Luna. The loop pays for roofs, school fees, and one enormous party a year.
- **The fringe.** Storm squats in the hills, liveaboards who haven't paid marina fees in years, preserve-edge camps the wardens half-tolerate, and the people the sweep calendar pushes block to block on a schedule everyone but tourists understands. The Consortium's unpublished soil studies are a housing story too; the Black Sand Pact's condo war starts at this fence line (canon).
