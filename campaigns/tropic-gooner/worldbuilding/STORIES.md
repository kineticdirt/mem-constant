# Isla Primavera — story seeds

Three cities, nine seeds. Street-level: no prophecy, no chosen bloodline, nobody saving the island. Just people with jobs, debts, and appetites, and the things that eat in the same restaurants.

**Canon status:** same grading as `LORE-BIBLE.md`: factions and places cited from the registry are canon; the specific situations are playable proposals. The `wb-tg-factions` lock applies throughout: former PCs (Ellaine, Nelly, Red, Toga, and the rest) may wander through any of these as ambient faces. They are not quest givers, and nobody owes the party anything on paper.

---

## Paradise

### 1. The early van

A body goes down on the marina side of the CiDance tower: a guest, a "medical event," the kind of thing the discretion clause exists for. The problem is the gray van with the tiny barcode was already in the service lane when it happened, engine running, before anyone dialed. The party is close enough to see the timestamps not line up: on a lobby camera, in a valet's memory, on a food-deliverer's phone.

**Factions:** Stevens & Co., CiDance, the Gilded Anchor (the guest was on a gala list), the Ash List (wants the footage).

**Sideways:** (a) The Ash List publishes the timestamp gap and Coral Trace serves everyone in a week, including the party. (b) The "body" is alive, sedated, and being moved for reasons the Kindred would like kept quiet; now the party has seen a person in a bag. (c) The van crew is Team 7 and their actual job that night was the car two spaces down.

**What it reveals:** In Paradise, the response to an incident is faster than the incident, because the incident was scheduled.

### 2. Table 6

The Lunch Regulars hold court at the CiDance buffet, and this week the gossip has a shape: a retired detective's notebook (the paper kind, decades of names) didn't come home from table 6 on Tuesday. By Thursday, three different people have quietly asked the buffet staff if anyone turned in a notebook. The party gets asked too, because they were seen eating there, which tells them something about how small Paradise really is.

**Factions:** the Lunch Regulars, the Visibility Board (the notebook reads like an optics-calendar source), Coral Trace, whoever actually pocketed it.

**Sideways:** (a) The notebook is boring for forty pages and then isn't, and the page that matters is about someone currently hiring the party. (b) The detective doesn't want it back; he wants to know who looked. The party is the bait. (c) A PIU hospitality intern found it and is trying to sell it, badly, out of a dorm room.

**What it reveals:** Paradise runs on information the way Porto runs on cargo, and the cheapest intelligence on the island comes with a lunch buffet.

### 3. The gratitude circle

Handbills appear in the Java Jump bathrooms and the PIU South study lounges: a "sunrise gratitude circle," free, no address, a time and a hand-drawn sun. A hospitality intern, the one who covers the party's usual coffee order, stops showing up to shifts, then answers messages with copied-and-pasted serenity. Her roommates are splitting between "she's in love" and "she's in trouble."

**Factions:** Honeycomb Choir (foreground cult, per locked taste), PIU admin (wants zero press during enrollment season), the CRT (a missing student in Paradise is an optics problem, not a case).

**Sideways:** (a) The circle is a genuine fringe-of-the-fringe feeding ground and the Choir's middle managers are as scared of it as anyone. (b) The intern comes back fine, grateful, and quietly recruiting, and the party has to decide what "fine" is worth. (c) The flyer is a Vice Board-blessed containment funnel: gather the susceptible somewhere countable. The ugly part is that it's working.

**What it reveals:** Even the cults get a tourism variance in this county, as long as they book a private room.

## Porto Lujara

### 4. The sealed float

The morning after Carnaval, the sweep has come and gone, and one float is still shrink-wrapped behind the docks: unregistered, unclaimed, and humming faintly with a generator that should have run dry. The float barn crew wants it towed before the CRT does its paperwork pass. The parade committee wants it to have never existed. A Night Ledger clerk wants to know why its build fee was paid in cash by a shell that also paid for three hotel suites the same week.

**Factions:** the Carnaval committee, Night Ledger, the CRT, Stevens & Co. (eventual cleanup), whoever's cargo is inside.

**Sideways:** (a) The cargo is contraband and boring (barrels, cash, someone's entire product line) and the interesting part is whose sweep it survived. (b) The cargo is alive, or was recently. (c) Opening it voids three different understandings at once, and the party spends the rest of the arc being politely bought by people they've never met.

**What it reveals:** Porto Lujara's true civic institution is the week of sanctioned looking-away, and everything on the island gets scheduled around it.

### 5. The raid that didn't happen

Night Ledger's overtime forecast, the one that is never wrong, calls a CRT Tier 3 on a specific alley club, down to the hour. The party happens to be inside, or next door, or holding something that shouldn't be there. The hour passes. Nothing. Then the club's books show up on a desk in Sierra Dorado flagged "exemplary," and the club's owner starts making phone calls from a parking lot, which he has never done before.

**Factions:** Night Ledger, the CRT, the Visibility Board (someone bought the forecast), the club's actual backers.

**Sideways:** (a) The raid was rerouted to a wrong door across town, and the family in that apartment would like someone to know. (b) The forecast wasn't bought — it was *fed*, and Night Ledger's clerk figures that out about the same time the party does. (c) The club owner interprets the reprieve as protection and starts acting immortal, which is its own problem.

**What it reveals:** In Porto, information about violence is a commodity that trades hours ahead of the violence, and a miss is louder than a hit.

### 6. Trays for no guest

A CCC culinary annex catering gig: twelve trays, a suite floor of a mid-tier hotel off the Carnaval Route, no guest name, cash tip paid in advance through the instructor. The students joke about it until one of them recognizes the hotel china from a completely different hotel. Then the annex gets a second order, same terms, and a third, and the instructor stops taking questions about where the money comes from.

**Factions:** the CCC annex, the Gilded Anchor (the feeding roster needs caterers who don't ask), CiDance (it isn't their building and they noticed), the Ash List (a student posted about it).

**Sideways:** (a) The suites are a feeding rotation and the party has been seasoning the delivery chain. (b) It's mundane (a donor's private week) and the horror is how hard that is to believe once you've seen the invoices. (c) The instructor is protecting the students by eating the curiosity himself, and he's about to be promoted out of reach.

**What it reveals:** Porto's service economy will deliver anything anywhere, and the town's real social safety net is instructors, foremen, and aunts who know when to stop a kid from asking.

## Jackedsonville (Crimson Quay)

### 7. When the lights go out

Quay Nights, second set, capacity. The power drops. Not a grid brownout, a cut. The licensed-club truce that keeps Quay Rojo collectors standing politely outside is, as everyone in the district knows, a light-based organism. The party has about ninety seconds of darkness and a choice: help the club get the generators up, get a specific person out the back, or be somewhere else entirely.

**Factions:** Quay Rojo, the Crimson Tithe (their scavengers work the dark), the CRT (Tier 1 line forms the moment the crowd spills), Neon Flux's unnamed ownership.

**Sideways:** (a) The cut was a Tithe move to feed in the dark, and the club wants payback with interest. (b) The cut was the club's own insurance scam and the party is now a witness to arson-adjacent fraud. (c) In the dark, something that isn't with any faction eats well, and the Tithe is as rattled as everyone else.

**What it reveals:** Jackedsonville's order is a set of handshake deals with amps and lighting rigs holding them up, and everyone knows the exact wattage of civilization.

### 8. The marginalia

A regular returns a true-crime paperback to the Book Nook with margins full of dates, initials, and street corners. Nelly files it under "customer is unwell" until a second book comes back with margins that continue the first one's route, and the route, plotted on the city, walks the Quay's alleys on the same nights the Crimson Tithe collects. The regular hasn't been in since. The tip jar, meanwhile, has started including folded notes addressed to nobody.

**Factions:** the Book Nook's orbit, the Crimson Tithe, Quay Rojo (their tribute route is on the map too), the Ash List (Marco would trade a semester for this).

**Sideways:** (a) The regular is a Tithe defector leaving a confession in installments, and the collection route is his own. (b) The books are bait, planted to see who plots them on a map, and the party just did. (c) The route is real, the regular is dead, and the second book was finished by somebody else.

**What it reveals:** The Quay's gray strip runs on the county's oldest rule (screens are ignored until somebody screams) and the bookstore is where the screaming gets catalogued.

### 9. The protection

A casino floor manager with hotel incident photos hires Rough Ride for protection, believing the advertising. Rough Ride takes the contract. Then CiDance's people, who hunt whistleblowers as a matter of locked policy, make Rough Ride a better offer, and the firm faces the rare situation of a client it has already been paid to become a problem for. The party gets pulled in as couriers, lookouts, or the only people the manager trusts, which is its own diagnosis.

**Factions:** Rough Ride, CiDance, Coral Trace (the NDA machine), the CRT (a viral clip would force a Tier 3 someone doesn't want).

**Sideways:** (a) Rough Ride splits the difference: the manager disappears "for a while" and comes back with a condo and no memory of wanting to talk. (b) The photos are real but the story they tell is worse than whistleblowing: they show a cleanup, and Stevens now has an interest too. (c) The manager is fabricating, the firm finds out first, and the party is holding evidence of a con aimed at the one organization on the island that cannot be conned safely.

**What it reveals:** In Jackedsonville, security is a service the way the tide is a service, and the difference between a guard and a collector is mostly invoicing.
