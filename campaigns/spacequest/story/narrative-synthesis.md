# Narrative synthesis — tone & stakes

## Horny horror as humor engine

The **horny** and the **horror** aren’t two modes you switch between — they’re the same engine. Comedy comes from **inappropriate appetite under pressure**; dread comes from realizing the **same appetite is the trap**. Laugh, then notice the teeth.

**Campaign default:** **over-the-top** sexuality and hive **suggestion** as pressure — but **otherwise serious** stakes (survival, identity, corporate betrayal). Pleasure is a **weapon** the Amalgam uses; scenes can still **lubricate** social / plot beats without every moment being explicit.

## Death vs assimilation

**Death** is possible but not the default threat — it’s almost a relief compared to the real outcome. The station’s horror is **assimilation**: bodies, desires, and loyalties rewritten until the party *wants* to stay, or can’t tell they shouldn’t. That keeps the tone **erotic-satire** without cheapening loss: what you lose is **self**, not just HP.

## Everyone expendable — for different reasons

If every PC is “expendable” in a **different** way, the ensemble stays fair and interesting:

- **Corporate math** — anyone replaceable on a spreadsheet.  
- **Biology / rarity** — some bodies are worth more *alive and compliant*.  
- **Psychology** — who breaks first under temptation or telepathic noise.  
- **Social graph** — who gets sacrificed to save face, ship, or secret.

No single character owns “plot armor”; the **reason** you might be thrown away shifts scene to scene.
